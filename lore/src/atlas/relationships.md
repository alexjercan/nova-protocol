# Relationship summary

This is the Atlas's closing comparison. Each faction describes its own position
in its article. The table brings those relationships together without making
employment, ownership, public legitimacy, and private allegiance the same thing.

## Dependencies and tensions

| Relationship | What connects them | Where interests separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EWI and Earth | Industry, resources, capital, employment, and recruitment | Public dislike does not remove dependence or the Board's influence |
| The Board and EWI management | Corporate authority and rewards for useful results | Local managers can conceal failures and pursue their own security |
| EWI and Fleet | Protection of recognised shipping and the resource system | Influence does not make every destructive act useful or every officer loyal |
| EWI and Kestrel | Competition, pressure, destruction, and forced acquisition | Kestrel's people do not all accept the same response to its loss |
| EWI and licensed operators | Access to essential services and markets under EWI terms | Nominal independence does not provide equal bargaining power |
| EWI and its workers | Wages, housing, supplies, and restricted access to passage | Dependence can coexist with anger, private support for the Kites, or a wish to leave |
| EWI and marginal communities | Irregular work, recovered material, and informal exchange | Local tolerance of useful activity is not approval of organised resistance |
| The Kites and employed supporters | Concealed allegiance and practical help | Exposure can threaten homes, relatives, and livelihoods |
| The Kites and other independents | Possible trade and shared problems | Independence does not imply common ethics, trust, or Kite membership |
| Fleet and people called pirates | Enforcement against a publicly defined threat | Salvage, refuge, and armed seizure can be treated as one problem despite different conduct |

Earth is a planet and political setting, not a population with one opinion.
The Board is EWI's leadership, not an unrelated rival corporation. Licensed
operators and independent communities are categories of people and businesses,
not a single organisation.

## Read each institution's account

- [EWI](../factions/ewi.md#relationships) controls much of the system without
  directly operating every workplace or knowing every allegiance.
- [The Board](../factions/board.md#relationships) benefits from EWI's reach and
  rewards useful management, but its formal mechanisms remain open.
- [Kestrel](../factions/kestrel.md#relationships) provided a viable alternative
  before the forced sale; its people carry different parts of that history.
- [Fleet](../factions/fleet.md#relationships) protects recognised order under
  strong corporate influence, not a consistent rule against harming workers.
- [The Kites](../factions/kites.md#relationships) defend collective freedom
  while depending on scarce resources and relationships inside the economy.
- [Other operators and communities](../factions/operators.md#relationships)
  can trade, assist, exploit, or refuse without sharing one political identity.

## Power without omniscience

EWI can make independent operation unaffordable and can use covert violence.
It cannot automatically make every local decision consistent with the Board's
interests. Fleet can overpower a poorly equipped craft without identifying all
supporters of a concealed community. The Kites can organise mutual support
without having solved provisioning, government, or the consequences of force.

Kestrel's fate establishes a major example of corporate violence, not a
complete account of every acquisition or every other pirate community. There
is no fixed market share and no promise of a successful revolution.

## Public position and private belief

An EWI employee's contract is not a confession of loyalty. A pirate label is
not proof of a particular act. A worker can dislike the acquisition without
knowing the attack was deliberate. The [story brief](../story/fall-of-kestrel.md#truth-account-and-knowledge)
keeps those distinctions explicit.

Individual relationships belong in [the character articles](../atlas.md#characters).
Halloran and Okono are not assumed to be crewmates, and Pell is not assumed to
command the entire Kite community. Those are substantive choices, not facts a
summary should create.

mechanisms, and [Language and dialogue](../language.md) for terms and naming.
