# Language and dialogue

This guide defines the book's terminology and the proposed way its people
speak. It separates **a thing, the institution's name for it, and a character's
opinion of it**. Clear language must not make an abusive system sound neutral
simply because its contracts use neutral words.

The title and editorial conventions below are this edition's writing choices.
New slang and character voices are proposals, not proof of a uniform culture.

## Titles and forms of address

| Term | Use | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **station director** | Head of an EWI station, including Calloway at Datum | Corporate and operational authority, not a Fleet rank |
| **Director Calloway** | Formal introduction or direct address | Use lower-case "the director" as an ordinary reference |
| **captain** | Person in command of a ship; "Captain" in direct address | Does not by itself establish a commissioned rank or name Meridian's captain |
| **commander** | An explicitly military command appointment or a reviewed rank | Not a generic substitute for every person in charge |
| **engineer** | Okono's established profession and assignment | Do not promote him to chief engineer without a decision |
| **the Board** | EWI's corporate leadership when the context is clear | Not every executive, officer, supervisor, or employee |
| **boss** | Informal crew speech about someone with authority | Avoid as the title in infoboxes and neutral reference prose |

**Why director:** Datum is an industrial operation and a corporate home.
"Captain" would make its head sound like a vessel's commanding officer;
"commander" would imply a military post. Station director expresses Calloway's
control of work and services without creating an EWI military hierarchy.

Pell's rank and present authority remain open. His name is sufficient until
those decisions are made. No rank, medal, or insignia is established by a
portrait.

## Names and capitalisation

Write **EarthWorks Industrial (EWI)** on first introduction. Use EWI after that.
Retain **Dorian Pell**, **Calloway**, **Halloran**, and **Okono** with these
spellings. First names other than Dorian remain open.

Use **the Shelter**, **the Roost**, **the Kites**, and **the Board** in running
text, with the ordinary article lower-case unless it begins the sentence. Use
**Datum** and **Meridian** without adding "the" to the proper name. "The ship
Meridian" is a description, not a different name. Capitalise Earth Fleet; use
Fleet as its short form when the reference is unambiguous.

| Identity | Naming rule | Vocabulary examples, not an inventory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EWI | Survey terms for ships and stations | Meridian, Azimuth, Parallax, Datum |
| Kestrel | Birds for ships; nesting and sheltering places for stations | Osprey, Harrier; the Shelter, the Roost, the Nest |
| Earth Fleet | Virtues for ships; station convention open | Resolute, Vigilant |
| The Kites | Kestrel-style names for their own craft and places; ironic corporate or contract terms for captured craft | the Roost; Rotation, Ledger, Severance |

Only the Shelter, Datum, the Roost, and Meridian are established named objects.
These conventions do not fix colours, uniforms, founders, speech, or technology.
There is no additional convention for Earth or the Board.

## Working vocabulary

| Term | Meaning in neutral prose | Do not conflate it with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **station** | A facility with an established station identity | A ship, a particular layout, or guaranteed residential capacity |
| **base** | A place serving an organisation's operations | Ownership, military status, or all of its members living there |
| **home** | A person's lived attachment and place of residence | Legal ownership or satisfactory conditions |
| **berth** | A ship space, passenger place, or sleeping space, specified locally | A guaranteed right to passage simply because a bunk exists |
| **worker** | A person doing the labour | A loyal supporter of the employer |
| **employee** | An employment relationship | Meaningful freedom to refuse its conditions |
| **licensed operator** | A business operating under EWI terms | An EWI department or an independent government |
| **independent community** | People living outside direct company control | Full self-sufficiency, innocence, or Kite membership |
| **salvage** | Recovery of material | Armed seizure; legal ownership must still be explained |
| **piracy** | Armed predation on ships or cargo, when describing conduct | Every activity that official reporting labels "pirate" |
| **forced sale** | Kestrel's established loss of independence through sale to EWI | A voluntary partnership between equally placed parties |
| **reconstruction** | The work that produced Datum at the Shelter's ruined site | A simple renaming or proof that all earlier evidence disappeared |

"Merger" belongs in an attributed corporate account unless we specifically
establish it as the legal form. "Transfer" can describe employment paperwork;
it does not make the workers' choices free. "Slavery" can express a character's
judgement of the conditions. Neutral prose should describe the coercive labour
system directly rather than invent a legal property status for workers.

## Truth, claims, and certainty

Use "EWI destroyed the Shelter" for authorial truth. Use "EWI blamed a reactor
failure" for the official account. Use "suspects", "believes", "heard", or
"can demonstrate" only when that person's position is established or labelled
as a proposal. Do not turn suspicion into evidence through confident narration.

Y0 is the book's reference year, not yet a calendar used in dialogue. Y-4 and
Y0 are fixed anchors. Other dated milestones remain explicitly proposed.
Physical measurements, if introduced, are stated in meters; an illustration's
pixel dimensions are not measurements of the setting.

## Registers and euphemisms

The following register examples are **proposed writing tools**, not quotations
from an established scene or a complete dialect shared by a faction.

| Speaker or context | Tendency | Example of the distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EWI public language | Present control as service, necessity, or opportunity | "Return eligibility" instead of saying who refuses passage |
| EWI management | Attribute gains to decisions and losses to circumstances | "We increased output" but "the delay occurred" |
| Working crews | Name concrete costs, faults, people, and time lost | "Whose leave was cancelled?" rather than "What was the labour impact?" |
| Kestrel recollection | Compare an actual practice, not an idealised golden age | A kept promise or a right that could be used |
| Independent residents | Speak of shares, obligations, repair, and trust | Freedom expressed through a decision they can actually make |
| Fleet official reporting | Distinguish recognised trade from threats to order | Attribute a pirate classification; do not adopt it as proof of conduct |

Workers can use corporate terms sincerely, ironically, or because that is the
language of their daily paperwork. The Kites do not all speak in bird imagery
or historical pirate slang. Dialogue should remain understandable without a
glossary of decorative future words.

## Character voice

| Character | Proposed normal voice | Under pressure | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Halloran](characters/halloran.md#voice) | Dry, specific; asks who changed a promise and why | Drops humour and insists on the exact point | Every line comparing EWI with Kestrel |
| [Okono](characters/okono.md#voice) | Precise, practical; separates what works from what is safe | Short instructions, fewer qualifications | Endless jargon or engineering metaphors about people |
| [Pell](characters/pell.md#voice) | Direct, personal; names obligations and who bears a cost | Interrupts abstractions with a concrete question | Constant speeches, threats, or certainty about unknown facts |
| [Calloway](characters/calloway.md#voice) | Controlled, courteous, managerial | Narrows choices and invokes procedure; polish can slip | A villain explaining his crimes to uninformed workers |

For every character, decide what they notice, what they will not say, how they
ask for help, and what changes when they are comfortable. A voice is more than
a preferred set of nouns. Humour, hesitation, kindness, and embarrassment should
not belong exclusively to one person.

## Precedence and open questions

The story brief owns the incident's established causal and knowledge boundaries.
The Atlas timeline summarises chronology. Character articles own their detailed
voice proposals; this table is a summary. A newly written line of dialogue is
not evidence that an event happened.

Open language decisions include regional accents, languages used besides the
book's English, Fleet ranks, crew slang, and how station directors delegate
shift authority. Do not infer an accent, ancestry, or nationality from a
surname. See [ordinary life and culture](questions/world.md#ordinary-life-and-culture)
and [current wants and relationships](questions/history.md#current-wants-and-relationships).
