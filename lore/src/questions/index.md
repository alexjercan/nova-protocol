# Review guide

The book now has a geography, institutions, people, and a historical sequence.
It is not yet a complete operating model. This catalogue collects **144 review
entries across 24 topics**, including choices that can remain open and gaps
that must be solved before dependent details become reliable.

An unanswered question is not a hidden fact. An option in this catalogue does
not establish an institution, a technology, an event, or a character's belief.

## Status of the material

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Established | The accepted historical foundation or reviewed world model |
| Proposed | A new detail, date, mechanism, or visual design awaiting review |
| Open question | Several answers could work; a choice has not been made |
| Gap | Accepted claims need a connecting mechanism, scale, or explanation |

Unlabelled setting prose records the established model. Proposed dates remain
proposed on the timeline and in the articles that use them. A more detailed
paragraph or a persuasive illustration does not automatically approve its
contents.

The book is an out-of-universe reference. **Authorial truth, the official
account, individual knowledge, suspicion, and proof must remain distinct.**
At the time of the Shelter attack, only Calloway and the Board know the charge's
real purpose. Neither Pell nor the placement crew knows it is intentional.

## Consequential decisions to make first

These recommendations are proposals. They are a practical starting order for
review, not a request to answer every question at once.

| Decision | Options and consequences | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical depth | A short industrial rush makes most institutions recent; several decades allow careers and established homes before Y-4 | Review the [multi-decade chronology](../atlas/timeline.md#proposed-working-chronology) as a set before accepting individual years |
| Journey times | Short journeys put most of the worker trap in permissions and prices; long journeys add separation, resupply, and medical risks | Start with journeys measured in months rather than days, then check whether all proposed dates remain feasible |
| Habitat gravity | Rotation or other physical arrangements impose design limits; broad artificial gravity permits a different industrial architecture | Prefer limited, explained habitat arrangements over an unrestricted gravity technology; do not infer either from the concept art |
| Food and maintenance | Near-total imports create brittle settlements; near-total self-sufficiency weakens some company pressure | Use partial local capacity with consequential imported supplies and replacement parts, then identify what those actually are |
| The worker's exit | Explicit permanent bondage makes recruitment harder to disguise; a promised exit undermined by costs and conditions preserves a public contract story | Develop an apparent route to departure that most workers cannot practically complete, without making the coercion less real |
| The Roost's secrecy | Concealing all habitation places a high burden on detection rules; concealing scale and allegiance permits some visible activity | Explore limited declared salvage or caretaker activity, but settle what "disabled" means before adopting this model |
| Kestrel's transfer | An immediate single transaction compresses the aftermath; staged practical dependence and later paperwork allow distinct pressures | Put emergency loss of independence before the proposed Y-3 formal transfer; review how each step works |
| Fleet's chain of authority | Direct corporate command makes EWI almost a state; captured public command preserves a claim to neutral order | Keep a public command structure shaped by EWI influence, and specify who can authorise coercive action |

## Gaps that currently carry the most weight

1. **Dependence without total control.** EWI dominates resources and institutions,
   yet smaller operators and the Kites persist. We need specific costs,
   information limits, and inconsistent interests, not a general statement that
   the company sometimes overlooks them.
2. **A home at a disabled station.** The Roost requires supply, power, and
   activity. An entry in a register cannot explain its secrecy.
3. **Acquisition without acquiring every person.** Kestrel's forced sale is
   established. The documents, service dependencies, and threats that bind its
   workers to EWI are not.
4. **Covert violence with a small circle of knowledge.** The placement crew must
   believe its task is ice work while the charge can destroy the Shelter. The
   explanation must preserve the established knowledge boundary.
5. **Rapid reconstruction.** Datum's Y0 scale is fixed. The means of building,
   supplying, and occupying it within four years need a viable model.
6. **A large community with limited supplies.** The Kites need food, health
   care, maintenance, newcomers, and disagreement, not only raids.
7. **A powerful relay that is not a universal switch.** Datum must matter without
   automatically controlling every possible message to Earth.
8. **Oppressive conditions with ordinary lives.** People need reasons to form
   attachments, make plans, and enjoy things that do not merely illustrate an
   atrocity. These lives must not make the abuse seem harmless.

## Source and scope boundaries

The accepted foundation recorded in this book and the owner's subsequent
decisions are the story authority. Do not recover retired story pages from
Git or consult old tasks, scenarios, implementation code, comics, or art for
story facts. They are not material to reconcile with this setting. Repository
working instructions still apply, but implementation details are not canon.

The CRT presentation and selected visual reference supply style only. They do
not fix faction colours, faces, uniforms, technology, or station layouts.

Keep development in `lore/`. Do not modify game or web content, create missions,
or prescribe a campaign, revelation, battle, or ending. Characters have
histories and current wants, not assigned future arcs. Use no task machinery
unless tracked work is requested.

## Current review decisions

The first story brief now follows **Kestrel's fall through the start of Y0**,
not all pre-Y0 history. The Atlas holds the wider chronology. The language guide
uses **station director** for Calloway and **captain** for vessel command;
neither creates a complete rank system.

The [character profiles](../atlas.md#characters) and portraits are development
proposals. Halloran's role and possible assignment to Meridian, Pell's current
authority, and the characters' personal connections remain open. The [ship
article](../ships/meridian.md) also leaves Meridian's primary work undecided.

Those choices should be reviewed before a ship design, complete crew roster,
or scene dialogue makes them appear settled. No character's portrait supplies
an age, nationality, rank, or new participation in the attack.

## How to close a question

An owner decision should update the relevant article and any dependent date,
relationship, or knowledge statement. Remove or revise the corresponding open
entry rather than leaving two contradictory descriptions. New questions can
emerge from an answer; completeness is not a reason to pretend those are solved.
