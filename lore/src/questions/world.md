# Places, technology, and daily life

These 48 entries concern the physical and social conditions people inhabit.
They are questions, not a catalogue of technologies or customs already present.
Use the [atlas](../atlas.md#locations) to distinguish named places from spaces
that still need development.

## Geography and settlement

**Gap:** Saturn is an inhabited industrial region, but its actual geography is
not mapped. Without it, distance and access can change to suit any paragraph.

1. Where do major operations sit relative to the rings and to one another?
   Which distinctions matter to working crews rather than only to a map?
2. What distinguishes a ring station from a station elsewhere in Saturn's
   region, and where do Datum and the Roost belong within that distinction?
3. How many other significant communities are needed to make "largest station"
   and "largest independent community" meaningful comparisons?
4. Which parts of Earth are heavily damaged, which offer better lives, and who
   has realistic access to those alternatives?
5. Which off-Earth customers or inhabited regions need to be named now, and
   which can remain distant without making the economy vague?
6. Are most Saturn residents temporary recruits, long-term migrants, or people
   born there? How does that mix change the meaning of "home"?

## Resources and industry

**Gap:** Mining drives the economy, but the commodities, buyers, and machinery
have not been chosen. EWI's power needs a material basis.

1. What does ring mining extract and sell, in what processed forms, and to
   which kinds of customers? Why choose Saturn rather than another source?
2. What does EWI still mine on Earth, and how does that business connect to its
   space operations instead of merely sharing a name?
3. Which stages are done by small crews, which require large facilities, and
   which stages give EWI the strongest control over competitors?
4. What equipment and replacement parts cannot be made locally? Who controls
   access to them, and how quickly does a shortage stop work?
5. How much useful material becomes waste, what happens to it, and which forms
   can independent salvage recover without inventing an unlimited resource?
6. What do workers actually do during an ordinary shift, and which hazards
   come from the work itself rather than deliberate neglect?

## Habitats and gravity

**Gap:** Stations are homes, but their engineering constraints are open. The
concept art does not establish gravity, atmosphere, or a floor plan.

1. What gravity conditions do people live and work under, and how are those
   conditions produced? Which activities occur in different conditions?
2. How are air, water, power, and heat managed, and which systems need continual
   external supplies or specialist servicing?
3. How large are the occupied parts of the Shelter, Datum, and the Roost, and
   what population can each support without implausible hidden capacity?
4. What physical separation exists between industrial hazards, housing,
   management spaces, medical care, and communal areas?
5. What is practical to build, move, reuse, or repair within the four years
   between destruction and Datum's established Y0 scale?
6. What signs does an operating habitat produce, who can observe them, and what
   must be explained before the Roost's apparent disabled status is credible?

## Transport and cargo

**Gap:** Cargo receives priority and workers rarely get leave, but transport
capacity, cost, and time have not been defined.

1. How long do journeys take within Saturn's region and between Saturn and
   Earth? Are typical journeys routine services or individually arranged?
2. What propulsion and energy constraints shape those journeys, and what does
   this require of fuel supply, servicing, and cargo capacity?
3. Why is passenger transport expensive relative to cargo, and which costs are
   real constraints rather than prices or permissions imposed by EWI?
4. Who owns or operates passenger berths, and can a person buy passage from
   anyone not directly controlled by EWI?
5. How do recruitment journeys, relief crews, medical transfers, and ordinary
   leave compete for capacity without becoming the same process?
6. What happens to work and residents when a delivery is late or lost? How much
   reserve is affordable to EWI facilities, small operators, and the Kites?

## Communications and information

**Gap:** Datum has a direct Earth relay. Its importance must coexist with the
fact that alternatives have not been ruled out.

1. What delay is inherent in a message, and what further delay comes from
   schedules, routing, processing, price, or corporate decisions?
2. What makes Datum's link "direct", and what does it offer that another relay
   or a ship carrying messages does not?
3. Who can send personal messages, at what cost, with what privacy, and through
   whose equipment? Can employment status change that access?
4. How do workers receive news, recruitment claims, official notices, and
   conflicting reports from other communities or Earth?
5. What records are kept of traffic, and who can inspect, alter, challenge, or
   preserve them? Do not assume an all-seeing surveillance system.
6. Which information can be widely known while remaining hard to verify? How
   do rumours about EWI differ from evidence about the Shelter specifically?

## Food, health, and maintenance

**Gap:** Material scarcity is established, but the necessities and their
supply chains need enough detail to make deprivation concrete.

1. What food is imported, grown, processed, or recycled locally, and who owns
   the capacity to produce it?
2. Which shortages change meals, which produce lasting illness, and which can
   make a settlement uninhabitable? Avoid treating every shortage as identical.
3. Who provides routine care, emergency treatment, medicine, and recovery time
   in EWI operations, licensed workplaces, and independent communities?
4. What happens to workers with injuries, chronic illness, disability, or age
   limits on the work they can perform?
5. What decides whether a fault is repaired, deferred, concealed, or worked
   around, and who bears the danger of that choice?
6. How do the Kites obtain care and technical expertise without every need
   being solved by scavenging convenient supplies?

## Family and identity

**Gap:** Recruitment and remittances connect households across space, but those
households and their choices remain largely undescribed.

1. Can dependants accompany a worker, who pays for them, and what happens to
   their housing or status if the employment ends?
2. How do births, childhood, education, and care for older relatives fit life
   at Saturn, if those are part of the settled population model?
3. Who receives remittances, how reliable are they, and what costs or controls
   stand between the worker's wages and the recipient?
4. How do relationships persist through separation, and when does pressure to
   keep earning conflict with a family's wish for reunion?
5. Which identities matter most to residents: birthplace, workplace, station,
   crew, family, community, or something not yet developed?
6. What does a person lose socially by leaving EWI, leaving the Kites, or
   leaving Saturn even when the move is materially possible?

## Ordinary life and culture

**Gap:** People need meaningful lives beyond employment and resistance, without
making oppressive conditions merely an attractive industrial backdrop.

1. What ordinary pleasures are cheap and available: games, music, food,
   stories, exercise, private hobbies, or other activities?
2. Where can people gather without working, paying for every moment, or being
   watched by a supervisor? Who can be excluded from those spaces?
3. What traditions, beliefs, or rituals help people mark departures, losses,
   achievements, and time spent away from Earth?
4. What does privacy mean in crowded housing, and how do residents negotiate
   noise, possessions, intimacy, and time alone?
5. How do humour, slang, and naming differ between workplaces or communities
   without turning every faction into one uniform personality?
6. What do people disagree about that is not the Shelter or EWI: taste,
   manners, care, pride in work, belief, generations, or ways of living?

voices. It does not settle the accents, customs, or technology asked about here.
