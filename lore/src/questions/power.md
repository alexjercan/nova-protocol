# Power, work, and communities

These 54 entries turn the broad [relationship model](../atlas/relationships.md)
into questions about who can make decisions, who can refuse them, and what
happens next. No legal system or community government is established merely by
being offered as an option.

## Employment and the worker trap

**Gap:** Workers are effectively trapped through employment and dependence,
but the exact path from recruitment to inability to leave is not yet written.

1. What does the recruiter promise about pay, term, housing, leave, and return
   passage? Which promises are explicit and which are carefully implied?
2. What deductions and compulsory purchases consume wages, who sets their
   prices, and can a worker inspect or dispute the account?
3. What must happen before a worker is permitted and able to leave? Is the
   main barrier money, contract status, transport access, or a combination?
4. What happens when someone finishes a term, refuses renewal, is dismissed,
   becomes unable to work, or cannot meet a required payment?
5. How do people obtain rare leave, and does unequal access reward rank,
   connections, scarce skills, compliance, or something else?
6. What can workers do collectively or individually to improve conditions,
   and what practical reprisals make those actions difficult?

## Law and personal status

**Gap:** Coercion is real, but the legal language and public justification need
to explain it without treating a signed document as free consent.

1. Which authority recognises employment contracts, property claims, company
   licences, residency, and passenger permissions at Saturn?
2. Are workers legally free to resign while practically unable to survive the
   result, or do additional formal restrictions prevent resignation?
3. Who hears a complaint, where is that process available, and what costs,
   delays, risks, or conflicts of interest make it ineffective?
4. How are former employees, fugitives, rescued people, and dependants
   classified? What changes when a person loses recognised status?
5. What distinguishes abandoned material from company property, and who can
   make or challenge that classification in a salvage dispute?
6. Who can detain, remove, fine, or physically coerce a person, under what
   claimed authority, and with what possibility of review?

## Management and the Board

**Gap:** The Board is powerful and local managers are uneven. The chain between
those facts is not a defined corporate structure.

1. How is the Board constituted, how are members replaced, and who outside it
   has a meaningful financial or organisational claim on the company?
2. Where and how does the Board operate, and how visible are individual
   directors to workers, customers, Fleet, and the public?
3. What decisions can Calloway make at Datum without approval, and which
   matters must move through a regional or corporate chain?
4. What targets, budgets, reports, and promotion incentives encourage neglect,
   concealment, coercion, or occasional local tolerance?
5. What reaches the Board when a manager fails, and how does the company
   distinguish useful deception from a failure that threatens production?
6. What specific leverage allows the Board to shape public appointments and
   enforcement without making every institution an EWI department?

## Earth and civilian power

**Gap:** Earth is constrained by resource dependence and corporate influence,
but "Earth cannot act" is not yet an institutional explanation.

1. What civilian authority speaks for Earth's external interests, and how does
   it relate to whatever political divisions remain on the planet?
2. Who benefits from EWI's resource dominance besides its directors, and which
   institutions would lose something by challenging it?
3. What legal or public channels exist for opposition, and how does EWI
   neutralise them without needing to control every conversation?
4. How do pollution, labour abuse, and distant worker suffering enter public
   debate, and what keeps outrage from changing policy?
5. Are there businesses, public bodies, or communities capable of surviving
   partial independence from EWI? What limits the scale of that independence?
6. What obligations does Earth claim toward citizens or residents working in
   space, and where does actual protection fail those claims?

## Earth Fleet

**Gap:** Fleet protects recognised order under EWI influence. Its command,
resources, and use of force still need a concrete model.

1. Who funds, supplies, appoints, and commands Fleet, and who can authorise a
   deployment or the use of force against a station or ship?
2. What is Fleet's actual size, distribution, and ability to remain at Saturn?
   Which bases and servicing arrangements would that require?
3. What occupies crews when they are not dealing with pirates, and which
   practical skills remain strong despite limited combat training?
4. How is a target classified as criminal or hostile, what evidence is needed,
   and who can challenge a corporate accusation?
5. What happens when a crew refuses an order, aids someone treated as a pirate,
   or reports conduct that implicates a politically protected company?
6. What distinguishes Fleet's use of inspections, seizures, arrests, and lethal
   force, and who bears responsibility when workers are harmed?

## Licensed operators

**Gap:** Licensed businesses extend EWI's reach without being direct
operations. The licence needs obligations and limits, not just a label.

1. What exactly is licensed: access to a site, use of infrastructure, sale of
   material, a business identity, or a combination of rights?
2. What does an operator actually own, and what equipment or services become
   unavailable if the licence is suspended or not renewed?
3. Who buys the output, how are prices set, and can an operator sell elsewhere
   without losing access to essential services?
4. What obligations does EWI owe an operator, and what prevents the operator
   from enforcing those obligations when they conflict with corporate aims?
5. How are workers employed and housed within licensed businesses, and when
   can those conditions differ meaningfully from direct EWI employment?
6. What allows some operators to assist independents, and what competing
   interests would make others report or exploit them instead?

## Kestrel as an employer

**Gap:** Kestrel offered better rights while remaining a viable industrial
competitor. Both sides of that contrast need substance.

1. Which rights were materially better: leave, passage, pay, housing, safety,
   representation, medical care, or the freedom to change jobs?
2. How were those rights enforced inside Kestrel, and what happened when a
   manager wanted to ignore them?
3. Who owned and managed Kestrel, and how much say did workers have without
   assuming the company was cooperative or charitable?
4. What competitive advantage or business arrangement sustained its position
   against a much larger and more coercive corporation?
5. What did workers dislike about Kestrel, and how did experiences differ
   between ships, stations, roles, and lengths of service?
6. What warnings or pressures preceded the Shelter attack, and how did Kestrel
   respond without knowing the destruction was being planned?

## The Kites

**Gap:** Freedom and opposition hold a large community together, but membership,
allocation, authority, and limits on force are not settled.

1. Who counts as a Kite, who can join, and how does membership differ from
   residence, private sympathy, temporary refuge, or trading with the community?
2. Who decides the allocation of food, space, care, equipment, and dangerous
   work when there is not enough for everyone?
3. What authority do armed crews have over residents, and what can residents
   refuse even when a commander claims the community's survival is at stake?
4. How are disputes, harmful conduct, theft within the community, and exclusion
   handled without assuming a complete legal system already exists?
5. What uses of force or treatment of captured people divide the Kites, and
   what limits, if any, are shared across otherwise different crews?
6. What happens to people who want independence but oppose raids, decline to
   follow Pell, or no longer want to participate in resistance?

## Other independents and piracy

**Gap:** The Kites are not the only story. Other independent lives and forms of
predation need motives and relations of their own.

1. What kinds of non-Kite communities exist around Saturn, and what sustains
   each beyond serving as an ally, victim, or enemy of the Kites?
2. Which activities provide stable income or supplies outside EWI employment,
   and which are intermittent, dangerous, or dependent on personal favours?
3. Why do particular crews turn to armed piracy rather than repair, salvage,
   smuggling, informal work, or remaining in company employment?
4. Who buys or uses seized goods, and how does that connect piracy to people
   who publicly condemn it?
5. How do independent groups establish trust, settle disputes, share care, or
   protect themselves from predatory neighbours?
6. How much should piracy beyond Saturn be developed now, and what can remain
   a wider condition without importing a second setting or campaign?
