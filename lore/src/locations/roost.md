**The Roost** is a former [Kestrel](../factions/kestrel.md) station that serves
as the base of [the Kites](../factions/kites.md). It is listed as disabled.
The Kites are secretive, but that administrative description is not a physical
method of hiding a community.

The Roost's name follows Kestrel's convention for stations: places where birds
shelter or nest. It does not establish a shape, an emblem, or a specific
internal organisation.

## Before Kite occupation

The Roost belonged to Kestrel. Its original purpose and its condition before
the Shelter's destruction are not yet fixed. It may have been a minor or a
substantial facility; neither choice follows from its name.

EWI acquired Kestrel's interests, which creates a significant gap: how did a
former Kestrel station become available to the Kites? Its paperwork, practical
condition, and whoever knew about it must fit the sale rather than exist in a
separate unexplained history.

## A base and a home

The Kites are Saturn's largest independent community, but that does not place
every member at one station. Supporters work within the official economy and
armed crews can be away from their base. The size of the resident population
must be distinguished from the size of the wider community.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed use of space</strong>
<p>Let the inhabited area be smaller than the old industrial structure. Residents maintain a limited set of safe rooms and work areas while other sections remain unusable. This gives repair work and crowding a daily presence, but does not by itself solve detection or life-support requirements.</p>
</aside>

Material hardship should have consequences for maintenance, privacy, health,
and the ability to receive newcomers. Freedom from EWI does not create an
unlimited supply of food or spare parts. Equally, deprivation alone does not
explain why people choose to defend the community.

## Secrecy and exposure

The station's existence as a former Kestrel object need not be secret for its
current political role to be secret. But occupied spaces require supplies,
power, and activity. What EWI believes about the Roost, what local managers
know, and what can be observed are separate questions.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Concealment options, not an answer</strong>
<p>One model conceals habitation in what is believed to be an unused shell. Another permits some apparently routine salvage or caretaker activity while concealing the community's scale and allegiance. The second reduces the burden of hiding every visit, but requires us to define what "disabled" allows and who maintains the false account.</p>
</aside>

No safe haven is guaranteed. EWI's tolerance of some scavenging does not mean
it knowingly protects the Kites' headquarters.

## Open questions and gaps

The Roost's ownership history, provisioning, visibility, and social government
need to be developed together. An unexplained failure to inspect it cannot
carry all four.
