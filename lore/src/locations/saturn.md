**Saturn's rings** are an industrial region dominated by
[EarthWorks Industrial](../factions/ewi.md). They are also home to people
whose wages, housing, relationships, and ability to leave depend on that
industry. EWI's power extends beyond the facilities it owns. Smaller operators
need access to services and markets on terms it can largely dictate.

Y0 begins four years after the destruction of [the Shelter](shelter.md),
Kestrel's main base. Kestrel's forced sale removed a substantial alternative to
EWI employment. It did not establish that Kestrel had been the last competitor.

## The resource economy

Ring resources supply ships and off-Earth industry. Earth remains a source of
capital, specialist equipment, customers, and recruits. The economy does not
require bulk ice to be shipped to Earth's surface. Its exact commodities,
routes, and production figures remain open.

EWI gained an early lead in large-scale space mining. It did not necessarily
invent spaceflight. Its expansion carries the practices already associated
with its terrestrial operations: pollution, weak worker protections, and
pressure on competitors.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed date: Y-24</strong>
<p>Place EWI's establishment of a major Saturn mining presence in Y-24. This leaves two decades of industrial growth before the Shelter's destruction, but does not establish a monopoly, a first human arrival, or the age of every station.</p>
</aside>

## Three overlapping sectors

| Sector | How it operates | Main dependence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct EWI operations | Company-owned facilities with company employees | EWI controls employment and the services workers need |
| Licensed operators | Smaller businesses working under EWI terms | Access to transport, servicing, purchasing, or facilities |
| Unlicensed communities | Salvage, repair, informal trade, and sometimes theft or piracy | Uncertain supplies and relationships inside the official economy |

These are economic positions, not three uniform factions. A licensed business
can exploit its workers. An EWI employee can quietly support the Kites. An
unlicensed salvager need not belong to them or take part in armed raids.

## A place to live

Stations are homes as well as workplaces. Leaving a bad employer can mean
losing a room, familiar neighbours, and access to supplies at the same time.
For some residents, Earth is the home they want to reach. For others, their
home is already here.

People join EWI despite its reputation. Some expect to avoid the worst
conditions. Others understand the risk and still need the income. Sending
money to family and being able to visit that family are different things.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed everyday texture</strong>
<p>Shared meals, amateur games, messages from home, and small repairs done for a neighbour give people lives beyond their employer. None makes the conditions acceptable. A familiar crew can be a reason to stay even when the company is a reason to leave.</p>
</aside>

## Power and its limits

[Earth Fleet](../factions/fleet.md) protects the resource system. EWI presents
itself as that system's essential provider. Its Board can shape policy and
senior appointments, but cannot know every private allegiance or make every
local manager act in concert.

[The Kites](../factions/kites.md) survive within those limits. They seek freedom from EWI
and oppose its rule, but their existence does not promise a successful
revolution. At Y0, survival, coercion, and resistance remain unresolved
conditions of life.

## Open questions and gaps

A useful regional model needs more than a list of stations. Mining sites,
residential capacity, services, customers, and journeys must fit together.
The specific resources and buyers are especially important: EWI's power should
rest on identifiable dependencies rather than the word "mining" alone.
