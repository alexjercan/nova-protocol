**The Shelter** was [Kestrel's](../factions/kestrel.md) main mining base. It was
substantial enough that destroying it helped break a company EWI had found
harder to displace through ordinary pressure. It was a workplace and a home,
not merely an asset in the later acquisition.

The station no longer exists as an operating Kestrel base at Y0.
[Datum](datum.md) occupies the rebuilt site.

## The working station

Kestrel offered better worker rights than EWI. The Shelter therefore belongs
to the memory of a materially different employment system, without requiring
Kestrel to have been free of industrial danger or commercial priorities.

Its position as the main base implies organisational importance, but does not
supply a floor plan. The number of residents, processing capacity, storage,
repair functions, and services for dependants are still open.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed place detail</strong>
<p>Make the Shelter a combined home, maintenance base, and point of coordination for dispersed crews. Its loss then removes the practical means of resuming work as well as a place to live. Review those functions before using them to explain the sale.</p>
</aside>

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed date: Y-10</strong>
<p>Place the Shelter's establishment as Kestrel's main base in Y-10. This gives it six years in that role before the attack without fixing the company's founding date. The station could be older; construction history remains open.</p>
</aside>

## Destruction and competing accounts

In Y-4, an EWI crew placed a demolition charge believing it was for breaking
ice. The charge was intended to destroy the station. It killed people at the
Shelter and the workers who placed it. Only Calloway and the Board knew its
true purpose.

EWI blamed a failure of Kestrel's reactor. Pell, then serving under Calloway,
disobeyed orders and went to rescue Kestrel people without knowing the
explosion was deliberate.

The [story brief](../story/fall-of-kestrel.md#truth-account-and-knowledge) records the full
knowledge boundary. It does not assign casualties, exact rescue numbers,
orders, or further participants.

## The loss of a home

The destruction and its aftermath forced Kestrel to sell. Former workers
entered EWI contracts; others refused and fled. That broad outcome is fixed.
The loss of personal belongings, records, places of assembly, or specific
services should not be asserted until those details are reviewed.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed memory and disagreement</strong>
<p>People who lived under Kestrel remember different parts of it: better rights, difficult work, familiar neighbours, or decisions they disliked. EWI employment can make the comparison sharper without turning every recollection into nostalgia or every survivor into a militant.</p>
</aside>

## Open questions and gaps

How did a charge supposedly intended for ice work reach a position from which
it could destroy the base? What did its placement crew believe about their
location and safety? The cover story and the physical event need to fit each
other without adding another knowing conspirator by accident.
