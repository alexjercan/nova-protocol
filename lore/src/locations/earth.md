**Earth** is both EWI's place of origin and an active part of its mining
business. It is not a clean, distant alternative to industrial Saturn. Heavy
machinery, noise, dust, smoke, oil spills, and dried lakes are part of the
terrestrial damage associated with the company.

That does not establish that the entire planet is uniformly ruined or that
every person lives under the same conditions. The geography of wealth,
pollution, and opportunity remains to be developed.

## Industry and political power

[EWI](../factions/ewi.md) is widely disliked for its environmental damage and
labour practices. Its control of resources and the Board's influence over
institutions make that dislike difficult to turn into effective opposition.
People can recognise exploitation while remaining dependent on its products or
wages.

Earth supplies capital, specialist equipment, customers, and recruits to the
space economy. Ring mining serves ships and off-Earth industry; the economic model does not
require all extracted ice to reach Earth's surface. What is actually shipped to Earth remains an open economic question.

[Earth Fleet](../factions/fleet.md) protects the recognised resource system.
EWI's influence reaches senior appointments. The exact relationship between
civilian government, Fleet command, and the Board is not yet a constitution.

## Recruitment and the idea of home

Some workers leave because conditions on Earth are worse for them. Others
expect a temporary sacrifice to improve their family's position. Knowing
EWI's reputation does not reveal the full experience of dependence in space,
and understanding the risk does not necessarily create a better option.

Once employed at Saturn, a person can send credits home while being unable to
afford passage. The recipient of those credits may depend on the worker
remaining employed. Family loyalty can therefore strengthen a trap that neither
side wants.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed social detail</strong>
<p>Recruitment presents the contract as a route to an improved life rather than a permanent departure. Families disagree over whether another period of earnings is worth another period apart. A worker can defend the decision to leave without defending the company.</p>
</aside>

Earth remains a desired destination for some residents of Saturn. Others have
partners, workmates, and homes in the rings. For them, return to Earth would be
another displacement, not a universal happy ending.

## Before Y0

EWI's terrestrial operations predate its early lead in large-scale space
mining. The [proposed chronology](../atlas/timeline.md#proposed-working-chronology)
places that space expansion in Y-40; this is a working date, not a settled age
for the corporation. No founding date, city of origin, or real-world calendar
year is established.

## Relationships

- **EWI's Board:** exercises resource-based political influence, but its home
  or headquarters is not fixed here.
- **Earth Fleet:** protects trade and recognised order; it is not independent
  of corporate influence.
- **Saturn's workers:** supply labour and maintain family and financial ties.
- **The Kites:** oppose EWI, but their reputation, sympathisers, and access to
  public audiences on Earth remain open.

## Open questions and gaps

The book needs regional variation on Earth, a credible recruitment process,
and a clear account of who can resist corporate influence and at what cost.
These are not answered by saying that EWI is powerful.
