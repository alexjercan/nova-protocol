**Kestrel** was a substantial independent mining company whose scale made it
harder for EWI to displace than smaller operators. It was much smaller than
EWI, but not a marginal scavenging business. Its main base was [the
Shelter](../locations/shelter.md).

Kestrel provided better worker rights than EWI. That contrast is central to
[Halloran's](../characters/halloran.md) experience, but does not establish a
worker-owned company, a charitable mission, or ideal management.

## Work and independence

Kestrel remained industrial. Mining, maintenance, cargo, and the need to keep
operations functioning shaped its business. Its independent position offered
an alternative to EWI employment and control, not an escape from work itself.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed labour contrast</strong>
<p>Give Kestrel predictable leave, a real route to return passage, and limits on deductions for housing and necessities. These would make Halloran's comparison practical rather than sentimental. Exact entitlements, enforcement, and affordability remain to be reviewed; "better" must become a concrete difference.</p>
</aside>

How Kestrel sustained those conditions while competing with EWI is a major
open question. Better rights might rest on different margins, skills,
contracts, or management choices. None of those explanations is established
merely by the fact that workers were treated better.

## Before the destruction

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed dates: Y-16 and Y-10</strong>
<p>Place Kestrel's substantial Saturn operations in Y-16 and the Shelter's establishment as its main base in Y-10. These are milestones in a proposed chronology, not the founding date of the company or proof of when every asset was built.</p>
</aside>

The Roost also belonged to Kestrel. Its original purpose, period of operation,
and later disabled status remain open. Bird names for ships and nesting or
sheltering places for stations are naming rules, not an inventory beyond the
objects already established.

## Destruction and sale

In Y-4, EWI deliberately destroyed the Shelter and blamed Kestrel's reactor.
Only Calloway and EWI's Board knew the attack's real purpose at the time.
The destruction and its aftermath forced a sale; the financial and legal
steps are still to be developed.

EWI took over Kestrel's interests and brought former workers under EWI
contracts. Halloran was among them. Others refused and fled. Survivors and
contract refusers formed the human basis of the Kites. The phrase "merger"
does not make these choices free or settle the transaction's terms.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed date: Y-3</strong>
<p>Complete the formal transfer in Y-3, after emergency dependence has already narrowed the choices of workers and the remaining business. This separates practical loss of independence from final paperwork. The sequence and duration require review.</p>
</aside>

## Legacy at Y0

Kestrel is not an established surviving rival at Y0. Its interests were
absorbed by EWI. Its people, memories, skills, and names persist in different
places: within EWI employment, within the Kites, and potentially elsewhere.
Their exact distribution remains open.

Kestrel was not necessarily EWI's last competitor. Its fate must not silently
settle every other company's history or EWI's market share.

## Relationships

**EWI** was a much larger rival and became the acquirer after deliberately
destroying Kestrel's main base. **The Board and Calloway** knew the attack's
purpose; ordinary EWI workers did not share that responsibility by employment.

**Former workers** have different relationships to the lost company. Halloran
experienced transfer into EWI. Survivors and contract refusers supplied the
human foundation of **the Kites**, not a continuation of Kestrel's corporate
government. Specific dealings with **Fleet** or other operators are not fixed
merely by Kestrel's size or the broader economic model.

See the [relationship summary](../atlas/relationships.md).

## Open questions and gaps

The book needs the actual difference in worker rights, a viable competitive
business, and a credible collapse process. Kestrel's managers, owners, worker
representation, and response to the official reactor account are all open.
