**Earth Fleet** protects shipping and the economic system on which Earth
depends. [EWI](ewi.md) is deeply embedded in that system.
Its Board's influence reaches policy and senior appointments, making Fleet
protection and corporate interests closely aligned.

[Earth](../locations/earth.md) is not uniformly prosperous or satisfied with this arrangement.
EWI's pollution and labour practices are widely disliked. Dependence on its
resources and its influence over institutions make opposition difficult to
translate into effective action. A bad reputation alone does not remove its
power.

## Protection and legitimacy

EWI cargo is recognised as legitimate trade. A licensed competitor can also
supply legitimate goods. Unlicensed salvage is vulnerable to being described
as theft of corporate property. Armed seizure creates an actual piracy threat.
These activities are not equivalent, even when enforcement treats them as one
problem.

Official reporting often collapses distinctions between raiders, salvage crews,
and people sheltering fugitives. The label "pirate" can therefore describe an
activity or serve as an accusation. Not every scavenger is a Kite, and not every
Kite is an armed raider.

Fleet's institutional distinction is between protecting recognised order and
acting against those classified as threats to it. It is not a consistent rule
against harming innocent people. Individual crews can dislike that distinction,
accept it, or fail to question it.

## Why not destroy the competition?

A competitor's facilities also contain productive equipment, workers, and goods.
Destroying them can damage the system Fleet exists to protect. For EWI,
financial dependence or acquisition usually preserves more value than an open
attack.

The Board's influence can obtain selective enforcement and manufactured
justifications. It does not make overt violence costless. There is no absolute
barrier that guarantees a competitor's safety, but neither does commercial
competition automatically become a naval target.

[The fall of Kestrel](../story/fall-of-kestrel.md) illustrates the difference. The official
account describes an industrial failure followed by EWI taking over Kestrel's
interests. Authorial truth is a concealed corporate attack. Fleet participation
in that attack is not established and must not be inferred.

## Capacity and experience

Fleet has little hands-on combat experience. There is no established major war
that keeps its crews practised, and combat training and drills are not especially
strong. Piracy exists beyond Saturn, but those other regions remain outside this
edition's scope.

Limited combat experience does not imply an inability to navigate, maintain
ships, or rescue people. Nor does it prevent a well-equipped vessel from
intimidating a poorly equipped crew. Fleet can protect cargo, inspect ships,
seize goods, and make arrests without every encounter becoming a battle.

Military force can destroy an identified hostile craft. It cannot, by itself,
identify every concealed allegiance in the workforce. The Kites' presence inside
ordinary economic relationships makes suppressing a community a different
problem from winning a fight.

## Historical depth

Fleet's founding date and earlier mandates are open. The absence of an
established major war does not mean Fleet first appeared in response to the
Kites. Its organisation and political culture need a history independent of
the Shelter's destruction.

<aside class="proposal">
<strong>Proposed institutional culture</strong>
<p>Promotion rewards orderly service, reliable cargo protection, and avoiding politically embarrassing incidents more than combat initiative. This can produce competent routine crews and cautious senior officers without making every person corrupt or every ship ineffective.</p>
</aside>

## Relationships

Fleet protects **Earth's** recognised shipping and resource interests in a
system strongly influenced by **EWI and its Board**. That alignment does not
establish that a station director can command a Fleet ship. A specific chain
of public and corporate authority still needs to be defined.

**The Kites and other people called pirates** face enforcement that can blur
salvage, refuge, and armed predation. **Licensed operators** can still carry
recognised trade; commercial competition alone does not automatically make a
ship a target. No Fleet participation in the Shelter attack is established.

See the [relationship summary](../atlas/relationships.md).

## Open questions and gaps

The key gap is command: who can authorise coercion, which claims count as
sufficient cause, and how EWI affects those decisions. Fleet also needs a
credible scale, supply system, and ordinary working routine. None is supplied
by the virtue names used for ships.
