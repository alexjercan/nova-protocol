# Nova Protocol story bible

This is the small, revisable source for the mainline story. It defines the
story before final dialogue, lore pages, or later chapters are authored.

## Story promise

Industrial workers survive a political act they do not understand. They are
not chosen heroes. Skill, loyalty, and bad luck put them in the path of forces
that treat working ships and their crews as expendable.

## Point of view

- The player commands Cutter One and is an experienced engineer on a first
  shift at its helm.
- Cutter One is a work crew, not a lone hero ship.
- The mainline stays close to what this crew can see, recover, hear, and infer.
- Political context arrives through consequences and conflicting evidence, not
  an omniscient explanation.

## Established setting

- The setting is human and grounded. There are no current alien or
  supernatural elements.
- Earth supports industrial, civilian, and naval fleets.
- Earthworks Industrial operates Meridian, a large carrier that is workplace,
  transport, and home to several industrial crews.
- Pirates and runaways survive with stolen, salvaged, and rebuilt ships.
- Earth naval ships patrol routes, enforce policy, protect traffic, and project
  force.
- Narrative organizations are not runtime allegiances. Player, Enemy, Neutral,
  and Unmarked describe current game relations, not political identity.

## Established cast

### Cutter One captain

The player character. An experienced engineer taking a first helm shift. Their
name and detailed history are not fixed.

### Copilot

Runs the flight checks and challenges unsafe assumptions. Their caution about
the repaired RCS manifold comes from the loss of Prospector Six.

### Engineer

Works the cargo rig. Dry, practical, and willing to turn an unauthorized orbit
into a documented engineering test.

### Deck Chief

The crew's supervisor aboard Meridian. Owns the work sheet, maintenance
release, and final ordinary order before the attack.

### Meridian Control

The carrier's duty channel. It makes Meridian feel inhabited before its silence
makes the loss legible.

The crew roles currently lack names, personal wants, and explicit relationships.
Those must be defined before the complete arc or final dialogue is approved.

## Established events

1. Cutter One leaves Meridian for three routine recoveries.
2. The crew verifies repaired maneuvering and guidance systems while working.
3. It takes an unauthorized orbit behind a survey body.
4. It recovers a third unmanifested but validly tagged crate.
5. A dark Earth Navy warship emerges from cover and destroys unarmed Meridian.
6. Cutter One survives unnoticed and receives an automatic distress signal.
7. The cutter returns to the wreck and recovers three recorders.
8. A five-ship cleanup group enters from the attacker's route.
9. Cutter One escapes with evidence, whether or not the group detects it.

The third crate is ordinary. It is not secret cargo and does not cause the
attack.

## Central unresolved questions

These block the full campaign outline:

- Who stole or controls the naval warship?
- Why was Meridian selected as the demonstration target?
- What political result does the attack seek?
- Why does a cleanup group want the recorders destroyed or recovered?
- What happened to Cutter One's copilot and engineer between chapters?
- What distinct fact does each recorder reveal?
- Who can receive the evidence, and why should the crew trust them?
- What does the crew want after immediate survival?
- What choice ends the campaign without turning the crew into galactic saviors?

## Knowledge rule

For every chapter, track three layers:

1. What actually happened.
2. What Cutter One can reasonably know.
3. What each organization claims happened.

A reveal is useful only when it changes an action, relationship, or
interpretation. Lore that changes none of these waits outside the chapter.

## Durable lore direction

Accepted story facts can later become campaign lore pages in the wiki:
organizations, crews, people, ships, places, and events. The future lore pages
must clearly separate political identity from the mechanical allegiance model.
Do not build the encyclopedia or a lore data format until the complete story
spine proves which entries are needed.
