# Current chapter diagnosis

This pass compares player action, story information, character movement, and
payoff. It diagnoses the current implementation. It does not prescribe final
dialogue.

## First Shift

| Scene | Player action | Story work | Current strength | Revision opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Departure | Listen, then burn to a mark | Establishes Meridian, Cutter One, crew competence, ordinary work, and a deadline | Credible workplace language and a clear home ship | Four role labels arrive quickly. Give each recurring crew member a name, immediate want, and relationship to the captain. |
| RCS check | Stop and fly a four-mark box | Shows professional caution and the Prospector Six accident | Tutorial action has an in-world reason | The long held conversation delays play and tells a complete outside tragedy before attachment to this crew is secure. Test what can move into action or visual detail. |
| Salvage | Recover two crates in the plate | Shows routine competence | Sparse comms respect player workload | The crates produce confirmation but no character pressure or small disagreement. One beat could reveal how this crew works together without turning cargo into a mystery. |
| Navigation | Lock and complete two GOTO legs | Finishes the cutter's maintenance release | Repetition teaches through work | The second leg changes no relationship or situation. Its short exchange should earn a later crew choice or joke. |
| Orbit | Approach and complete a lap | Gives the crew playfulness and mild rule-breaking | The documented donut is the clearest crew identity beat | It is the best seed for a shared crew relationship. Later chapters should pay back who proposed it, who covered for it, and who logged it. |
| Return | Recover the last crate and fly home | Restores the ordinary schedule before the break | The normal final order sharpens the attack | The unmanifested crate receives repeated attention despite being intentionally irrelevant. Ensure this reads as workplace friction, not a false mystery promise. |
| Approach | Watch an unidentified naval hull close | Escalates plume, silence, identity, and weapon alignment | Information arrives with the physical approach | Cutter One cannot act. Keep the lines terse and let framing, scale, and silence carry dread. |
| Salvo | Watch Meridian's destruction | Breaks the ordinary world | The physical silent sequence is the strongest story delivery | The loss is mainly the carrier as an object. Earlier named people, spaces, and routines would make each visible impact cost something known. |
| Aftermath | Hold and listen | Confirms silence and points toward survivors | The automatic beacon is a restrained handoff | The survival state of the copilot and engineer is unclear even though they were aboard Cutter One. Resolve this before chapter two. |

## Cross-chapter findings

- The inciting incident is clear. The larger dramatic question is not.
- The setting is strongest when work procedure reveals character.
- Character continuity is weaker than spatial continuity.
- First Shift has concrete voices but few names or personal wants.
- Second Shift has concrete objects but no information attached to them.
- The player performs many mechanical decisions but no meaningful story choice
  yet.
- The attack sets up a political mystery, but the campaign has not agreed what
  truth lies behind it.

## Immediate design tests

Before changing scenario code, the full outline must answer:

1. Which Cutter One crew members survive and remain aboard?
2. What does each recorder reveal in sequence?
3. What causes the cleanup group's entrance at that moment?
4. Who does Cutter One seek after escape?
5. What decision closes the campaign's worker-scale arc?
