A minimal 1-1-1 ship turns at exactly the same rate as a hundred-section capital. That is not a bug - it is 2b03a2f8, "Make controller authority size-independent in acceleration units", doing precisely what it says. The model settled on 2026-08-19 is that attitude authority is bounded by two limits, not one, and that the binding limit is different for a fighter and for a capital. Two authored knobs carry it, one authored knob is deleted, and size falls out of the physics instead of being legislated in either direction. Every radius here is given in world units and in metres. Nova runs at 1 u = 10 m; an earlier pass of this calibration dropped that conversion and came out ten times too sharp, which is 03. Every hull that ships has had its inertia, mass and centre of mass measured out of avian, and every one of them lands structure-bound and sharper - that is 06. The one thing still genuinely open is the content retune, 09.8.

01

Two knobs. One deletion.

Read this one first

Delete max_angular_acceleration: 0.5 from the controller. It stops being authored and starts being computed. That is the whole change. Everything below is how it gets computed.

Add max_torque to the controller. How hard this computer's reaction wheels twist the ship. Two computers, twice the torque. No cap, no curve, no stacking rule. It has no settled value yet - the shipped hulls are measured but nothing the size of the crossover exists to measure, and that is 06.

Add load_limit, one global constant. How many G the hull takes before it tears. 8 G = 78.48 m/s2, decided 2026-08-19. Not authored per section: hull metal is hull metal, so there is never a "which section's limit" question to answer.

The game already has the rest. Every frame it knows the ship's inertia I and the distance r from the centre of mass to the furthest section. r is a distance in world units and load_limit is a real acceleration, so the structural line carries the scale conversion - that is 03, and it is where the previous draft of this page was wrong by a factor of ten:

torque ceiling      = total max_torque / I                  can I push that hard?
structural ceiling  = load_limit / (r * METRES_PER_UNIT)    will it survive it?
turn acceleration   = min( the two )                        whichever gives up first

Same controller, same constant, and the answer comes out different because the ships are different. On the fighter the torque ceiling is 115x higher than the hull will take, so the structure binds and the ship is sharp. On the capital I has gone up 3,162 times while r has only gone up 10, so the torque ceiling falls straight through the structural one and the ship is a barge. A capital has to spend real tonnage on attitude control to get back to its own structural limit. That is 08.

The middle row is the one to trust, because it is the only one taken from a hull that exists. The cargoa corvette ships today - 4.9 x 3.2 x 1.6 u, so 49 x 32 x 16 m - and its mass, inertia and centre of mass are read straight out of avian: 15.86 t, I = 35.73, arm 2.76 u (27.6 m). It comes out structure-bound, with its torque ceiling standing 14.8x above what its hull will take, and it flips in 2.10 s against today's 5.00 s. A real shipped corvette sitting one row below the idealised 1-1-1 and behaving like one is the strongest evidence on this page that the model is right. The readings are 06.

What each knob does to feel

  • max_torque is the capital's knob. A 1-1-1 already carries about 115x more torque than its hull will take, so you can halve it and a fighter pilot will not feel a thing.
  • load_limit is the fighter's knob. Every hull inside the crossover is structure-limited and feels it immediately. A torque-limited hull does not feel it at all until it has bought enough torque to reach its own structure.

The two barely overlap, so tuning one does not smear across everything. That is the point of there being exactly two.

02

The defect is that the model has no size in it

Working as specified

The complaint is that a 1-1-1 - controller, hull, thruster - "is impossible to handle, it's very hard, turns slow and meh". Every number behind that feeling is deliberate. max_angular_acceleration: 0.5 rad/s2 was chosen for a target flip time, from the bang-bang relation:

t_180 = 2 * sqrt(pi / alpha)      = 5.01 s at alpha = 0.5
w_avg = sqrt(pi * alpha) / 2      = 0.627 rad/s = 36 deg/s

Five seconds for a 180 is a fine number for a capital ship. It is an absurd number for three sections bolted together, and the current model has no way to tell the two apart - the PD converts the acceleration limit into whatever torque each hull needs, so the same 0.5 rad/s2 lands on every hull in the game.

History - each model was half the physics

Before 2b03a2f8: max_torque, the propulsive limit alone. Correct as far as it went, but with no structural cap it over-punished size, because inertia carries r^2. Big ships were unsteerable.

Now: max_angular_acceleration, the structural limit alone, and flat. Correct as far as it goes, but size stopped mattering at all.

Neither was wrong. Neither was complete.

03

One world unit is ten metres, and forgetting it costs a factor of ten

Read before any number below

Nova runs at 1 world unit = 10 metres. One hull section is a unit cube, so a section is 1 u = 10 m on a side and a 1-1-1 ship is 3 u (30 m) long. A load_limit in G is a real acceleration in m/s2, but r comes out of the physics world in units, so the two do not divide until one of them is converted:

alpha_ceiling = load_limit / (r_units * METRES_PER_UNIT)      METRES_PER_UNIT = 10

The near miss - this page was wrong by 10x until 2026-08-19

An earlier draft read the radii as metres. A 1-1-1's r became 1.5 m instead of its true 1.5 u (15 m), every structural ceiling came out ten times too high, and the ship classes were nonsense - a "capital" whose 15 u (150 m) arm read as 15 m, and a "1-1-1" three metres long instead of 3 u (30 m). Calibrating against those numbers landed on 2 G, and 2 G at true scale flips a 1-1-1 in 3.10 s against today's 5.00 s. It would have shipped a change nobody could feel, and would not have fixed the complaint that opened the task.

Inertia was not wrong the same way. I is a game-unit quantity and the anchor I(1-1-1) = 2.5 stands as it is: three unit cubes of mass 1 in a line about their centre of mass is exactly 2 * 1^2 + 3 * (1/6) = 2.5. Only the structural side crosses into SI, so only the structural side needed the conversion.

Where the constant lives, and why the model cannot just use it

METRES_PER_UNIT = 10.0 does exist - in nova_ui::units - but its own module doc says "Display-only. World-space transforms, physics, content RON and AI tuning keep raw world units; only the strings a player reads pass through here." It is a formatting policy, and nova_ship does not depend on nova_ui at all, so the physics cannot reach it. The scale is otherwise recorded in a single doc comment on an unrelated gunnery range constant. This model needs the scale as a physics constant, so the work has to promote it into a crate both sides can see rather than declare a second one.

04

Two independent limits; the real one binds first

The model 
alpha_max = min( total max_torque / I ,  load_limit / (r * METRES_PER_UNIT) )
             \____ propulsive ____/      \________ structural ________/
  • I is angular inertia, in game units. It is not a function of r and no formula for it belongs in the code: avian already computes it exactly from the real mass distribution, and pd_controller.rs already reads ComputedAngularInertia. That is 06.
  • r is the distance from the centre of mass to the furthest section - not the geometric centre - in world units, taken to that section's outer face. BodyRadius is in nova_ship::prelude, but it is a scenario-obstacle radius that ships leave unset, so this arm has to be derived rather than read off it.
  • load_limit is the structural load the hull tolerates at that point, in m/s2. One constant for the whole game, set to 8 G = 78.48 m/s2.

The calibration decided on 2026-08-19 is those two numbers and one more: the crossover sits at 10 u = 100 m. Below it structure binds; above it torque binds. Only the crossover involves max_torque, which is why max_torque is the one quantity here that is still open.

Small ship: I is tiny, so max_torque / I is enormous. The structure binds. Capital: I is enormous, so max_torque / I collapses. The torque ceiling binds. Two different reasons for two different ships, both physical, and one expression covers both.

The propulsive curve is a DRAFTING DEVICE, not a prediction

The structural curve is arithmetic and holds for any hull. The propulsive curve is not: drawing max_torque / I against a radius needs I to be a function of r, and it is not one. I is a moment of the actual mass distribution - two hulls with the same furthest-section arm can differ in it by any factor you like, depending on where the mass sits. The curve here is I(r) = 2.5 * (r / 1.5)^p, anchored exactly on the 1-1-1 (three unit cubes in a line about their centre of mass is 2 * 1^2 + 3 * (1/6) = 2.5) and swept onwards by an exponent. It exists so the plate has a second line to cross. Nothing like it goes into the code, where avian computes I exactly and pd_controller.rs already reads it.

The readings in 06 say what the device is worth: across the 1.5 u to 2.9 u the fleet actually covers, p = 3.5 runs 1.2x to 1.9x light and no single exponent survives being fitted two different ways. Extrapolated to the 10 u crossover that disagreement is worth 3.9x in max_torque, which is why the number is provisional - and why the ship dots on this plate are placed at their measured I and never at the curve's.

05

Size falls out for free

Nobody has to invent it

A point at distance r from the axis feels tangential acceleration alpha * r and centripetal acceleration omega^2 * r. Both scale with r. So the same structural limit binds harder on a bigger ship, and the two familiar handling numbers appear without anyone authoring a size curve. Write r_m = r * METRES_PER_UNIT for the arm in metres:

alpha_max = load_limit / r_m           rate of change of turn rate
omega_max = sqrt(load_limit / r_m)     sustained turn rate

How these were obtained

Every section resolves to a SectionCollider::Cuboid of its authored box - bbox_max - bbox_min - not to its GLB mesh; the mesh is render-only. Each carries ColliderDensity(1.0), and avian sums the child colliders into the ship root's rigid body. Spawning the nine cargoa colliders under one RigidBody::Dynamic and reading the components back reproduces the parallel-axis arithmetic to four decimal places, so the two agree and neither is an assumption.

The arm needs a stated convention, and it is worth 28 % on the arm. r here runs from the measured centre of mass to the outer face of the furthest section, which is the rule that gives the 1-1-1 its 1.5 u (15 m). On the corvette it gives 2.76 u (27.6 m); to that section's centre instead it would be 2.25 u (22.5 m) and to its furthest corner 2.88 u (28.8 m) - a flip time anywhere from 1.90 s to 2.15 s. The face rule is the one drawn in Plate 2 and the one every number on this page uses. Note also that the corvette's COM sits 0.24 u (2.4 m) aft of its geometric centre, so half the bounding box - 2.45 u (24.5 m) - is not the same quantity and is not used here.

One name to fix on the way past

BaseSectionConfig::mass reaches avian as ColliderDensity, so a section's real mass is mass * collider_volume and the same authored number means different tonnage on two differently sized parts. Its own doc comment (base_section.rs:226) says so and the receiving parameter (health.rs:163) is named density; the field is the only thing still called mass. It is 1.0 on every section in the game, so it carries no information - but this model is the first thing that makes handling depend on mass at all, which makes it the first thing that will send a modder to that field. Deleting the vestigial field is 20260819-173840, and it does not block this task.

There is no exponent to find, because there is no law

The obvious next move is to fit the readings and use the fit. It does not survive contact with them. Anchored on the 1-1-1, the three shipped hulls want exponents of 3.94, 4.36 and 4.43, and a single p = 4.33 does hold all four points to within 14 %. But drop the anchor and fit the three shipped hulls against each other and the slope through them is 5.04 - and at the 10 u crossover those two answers are 3.9x apart in max_torque. An exponent that depends on which of your own data you feed it is not measuring anything.

It is not measuring anything because there is nothing there to measure. I is not a function of r. It is the second moment of where the mass actually sits, so two hulls with the same arm differ in it by whatever their layouts differ by. The corvette makes the point on its own: its fuselage carries 43 % of the ship's mass and contributes 16 % of its yaw inertia, because it sits 0.08 u (0.8 m) from the centre of mass; its nose carries 16 % of the mass and contributes 31 %, because it sits out at the end of the arm. Move one part and I moves without r moving at all. No curve in r can know that. So no inertia formula belongs in the code, and none is needed: avian computes I exactly from the real distribution and pd_controller.rs already reads ComputedAngularInertia. The curve on these plates is scaffolding for the drawing, and it comes down before anything is implemented.

What still has to happen before the capital end is retuned

The shipped fleet is measured. Nothing near the 10 u (100 m) crossover exists to measure, so max_torque is still pinned against a curve extrapolated 3.4x past the largest hull in the game. Build a hull at crossover scale - or accept a torque number chosen by feel and let the crossover land where it lands - before any capital is retuned. Retuning capitals against 1501 today would tune them to an arithmetic artefact.

What the gap does NOT touch

Every shipped hull is structure-bound, and a structural flip is pure arithmetic with no I in it: 1-1-1 1.55 s, racer 1.87 s, cargoa 2.10 s, cargob 2.17 s, and a hypothetical mid hull 2.83 s. Those are as settled as load_limit is, and the 8 G calibration stands on them untouched. Only the hypothetical capital's 8.14 s moves with the extrapolation, and it moves the safe way - a heavier inertia makes a capital slower still, never sharper. So the retune of everything that ships can start now; only the capital end waits.

07

The two loads add as a vector, so a hard turn spends the margin

The flight envelope

The tangential and centripetal loads are not bounded separately. They are perpendicular components of one acceleration at the tip, so they add as a vector. The arm is in metres here, r_m = r * METRES_PER_UNIT, because load_limit is in m/s2:

sqrt( (alpha * r_m)^2 + (omega^2 * r_m)^2 )  <=  load_limit

divide through by r_m:   alpha^2 + omega^4  <=  (load_limit / r_m)^2

That is an envelope, and it has the shape every pilot recognises. At rest the whole budget is available as alpha. At omega_max the centripetal load has eaten the entire budget and alpha is zero: the ship is holding the turn and can do nothing else. A big ship becomes committed to the turn - not by a rule somebody wrote, but because the arithmetic leaves it no margin.

The point is integrated in real time at the console's settings; a large hull genuinely takes many seconds to reach its own corner, which is the mechanic. The two load readouts trade against each other while the resultant sits on the limit throughout.
The bang-bang 180 is a reference figure, not an envelope solution

Every flip time on this page is the standard 2 * sqrt(pi / alpha), which is what the task record uses and what today's 5.00 s was set by. It assumes alpha stays available all the way to the halfway point, and the envelope above does not quite allow that: omega saturates first, at exactly a quarter turn whatever the hull, after which the ship coasts at omega_max. Integrating that boundary gives 1.83 s for the 1-1-1 against its bang-bang 1.55 s, 2.21 s for the racer, 2.49 s for the cargoa, 2.56 s for the cargob, 3.35 s for the mid hull, and 8.15 s for the capital - which barely moves, because a torque-limited hull spends its flip under the flat clip rather than out on the arc. The comparison against today's 5.00 s stays like-for-like because both sides are computed the same way.

Risk - an invisible envelope reads as sluggishness

This diagram is the mechanic. If the player cannot see it, a physically correct limit is indistinguishable from arbitrary heaviness - which is exactly the report that opened this task. A load readout on the HUD, or the hull protesting as the resultant approaches load_limit, is what converts a constraint into something a pilot can fly. Without it this work can land correct and still feel worse. The build-screen half of the same problem is owned elsewhere - 09.7.

08

What if the ship is so big I cannot turn it?

Fit controllers, and pay for them

Then fit controllers. Torque adds: a thousand controllers is a thousand times the torque, and nothing in the model caps the sum. This is why stacking needs no rule of its own. Controllers add torque, the structure caps the result, and the ceiling is the same min() it always was. STACK_AUTHORITY_LIMIT goes, and nothing takes its place.

Fitting them is not free, though, and the model already knows why. A controller is a section and a section has mass. Mass at radius d in world units from the centre of mass adds m * d^2 to the very inertia it is trying to overcome, so the wheels turn up on both sides of the fraction:

alpha_torque(n) = n * max_torque / ( I_hull + n * m_ctl * d^2 )
                                              \___ what the wheels cost ___/

n large:          alpha_torque -> max_torque / ( m_ctl * d^2 )    the mounting wall

The wall does not depend on how many you fit. It depends only on where you bolt them. Amidships, d is small, the wall sits far above the hull's structural ceiling, and enough wheels will always reach it. Out at the tip, d = r, and the wall can sit below the structural ceiling - at which point no number of wheels ever gets there. Setting the two equal gives the hull size where tip mounting stops working at all. Both sides cross the scale here, so METRES_PER_UNIT appears in the answer:

r_wall = max_torque * METRES_PER_UNIT / ( load_limit * m_ctl )     world units
       = 191.3 u  =  1913 m                      at the console's settings

None of that was authored. It is what reaction wheels do, and it arrives free with doing the inertia correctly.

At the provisional calibration this trap is theoretical

Pin max_torque through the crossover and the load_limit cancels out of r_wall entirely: it reduces to I(r_x) / (m_ctl * r_x), so raising or lowering the G limit does not move it. What does move it is the curve exponent from 06 - and because that curve is scaffolding rather than a prediction, so is this wall. At p = 3.5 it stands at 191 u (1913 m), about 13x the radius of the hypothetical capital above, so tip mounting is not currently a way to lose. At p = 3 it falls to 74 u (741 m) and starts to bite; at a solid p = 5 it runs out to 3292 u (32,922 m) and is pure theory. All three are the same extrapolation past the largest hull that exists. What is not extrapolated is the shape of the argument: mass at radius adds m * d^2, so a wall exists wherever the wheels are bolted, and only its position is unknown.

Where you bolt them decides whether more of them helps. Both curves climb in proportion to the count while the hull's own inertia dominates, then flatten onto their own wall. Amidships the wall is far above the hull limit, so the structure catches you first and the ship gets its physics back. At the tip the wall can land under the hull limit, and then the ship has bought inertia and nothing else - drive the hull slider past 191 u (1913 m) to see it happen. Controller mass is one section, 1 t, and it is counted in I but not in r - these wheels are fitted inside the hull, not bolted onto its outside. max_torque, load_limit and the exponent come from the console in 05; the hull radius here is its own.

Size stays dominant, and that is the point. A capital that fills itself with reaction wheels still stops dead at its own structural ceiling, and that ceiling is 10.0x below a 1-1-1's - the plain ratio of 15 u (150 m) to 1.5 u (15 m). Controllers buy a big ship its physics back. They do not buy it a fighter's.

09

Settled, and what is left open

Owner review, 2026-08-19
09.1 Two authored knobs, and no third Settled

max_torque on the controller, load_limit global, max_angular_acceleration deleted and not replaced. The bar a third knob had to clear was that a modder reads it without a physics lesson, and nothing else cleared it. It is not only a taste call: the structural ceiling already caps the result, so any further fraction laid on top would be a fudge factor doing a job physics has done. Stacking gets no rule for the same reason - controllers add torque and the hull stops them.

09.2 load_limit is one global constant, not a per-section knob Settled - refinement deferred

Hull metal is hull metal, so there is no "which section's limit binds" question to answer and no new field on every section. The honest refinement - min over sections of (strength_s / r_s), so one fragile part on a long boom limits the whole ship and moving it inboard measurably improves handling - is a real design lever and a possible later pass. It is out of scope here, and nothing on this page depends on it.

09.3 Thrusters do not change Settled

flight/thrusters.rs keeps the convex QP that deliberately nulls net torque, so engines give pure translation and the PD controller owns rotation alone. Off-axis engines genuinely do produce torque - thruster_impulse_system calls apply_linear_impulse_at_point - and a player who exploits that to swing a ship is doing something legitimate and stays able to. It is not an entry in the torque budget and the model does not account for it. The balancer buys clean, predictable translation and a single owner for rotation, and that is worth more than the recovered authority.

The comment at thruster_section.rs:361 about "a COM-centered engine torques the ship it must not touch" is about a stale-pose bug. It is not a claim that thrusters cannot torque, and must not be read as one.

09.4 A damaged ship's envelope moves, and that is the feature Settled

Both r and I change as sections die. Lose the nose and r shrinks, so the structural ceiling rises and the ship turns sharper than it did intact. Intended. Lose a controller and the torque ceiling drops, which a torque-limited hull feels at once and a structure-limited one does not feel at all. Both are the model working. The only requirement is that the HUD makes it legible at the moment it happens, so a player reads it as damage and not as a bug.

09.5 load_limit = 8 G has no physical derivation, and does not need one Settled 2026-08-19

8 G is a chosen test point. There is no measurement behind it and no reference to appeal to: the question it answers is "how strong is spaceship hull metal", which is a game-design choice and nothing else. What makes it defensible is where it sits. It is the single dial that sets the whole size curve - raise it and every ship sharpens, lower it and everything commits harder - while the ratios between ship sizes are fixed by geometry whatever it is set to. The 10x between a 1-1-1 at 1.5 u (15 m) and a capital at 15 u (150 m) does not move when the dial does. One number to playtest, and the first one to tune.

What the dial was worth getting right: the same calibration read at the wrong scale gave 2 G, and 2 G flips a 1-1-1 in 3.10 s where 8 G flips it in 1.55 s. Against today's 5.00 s the first is a change a player might not notice. See 03.

09.6 The shipped hulls are measured; the crossover is still extrapolated Narrowed 2026-08-19 - blocks the capital end only

Done: the racer, cargoa and cargob were spawned and their ComputedAngularInertia, ComputedCenterOfMass and ComputedMass read back. I is 10.86, 35.73 and 48.79; arms are 2.18 u (21.8 m), 2.76 u (27.6 m) and 2.93 u (29.3 m). All three are structure-bound with 11x to 38x of torque headroom, so their flip times are settled and the retune of shipped content is unblocked.

Not done, and not doable by measurement: the crossover sits at 10 u (100 m) and the largest hull in the game is 2.93 u (29.3 m). Pinning max_torque there means extrapolating a curve 3.4x past every data point, and the curve's exponent is worth 3.9x in the answer depending on which hulls you fit it through. Either build a hull at that scale or set max_torque by feel and let the crossover fall where it falls. Detail is 06.

09.7 The build-screen readout belongs to the EDITOR Settled - delegated to epic 20260812-131912

Not an open question and never this task's to answer. The editor epic recorded it on 2026-08-19 under "the engineer's NOVA OS": the build screen owes an engineer's panel - turn rate, torque, which limit binds, mass, thrust, power - the way Factorio puts craft time and power draw on the machine itself. The attitude readout is that panel's first tenant, and it is designed there, with the rest of the panel, rather than bolted on here.

What that delegation does not do is make the requirement go away. This work must not land in a release where nothing shows the number. A big enough hull genuinely cannot turn; fitting controllers is the answer, and it is only a decision if a player can see the "0.03 rad/s2, torque-limited" that provokes it. Ship the two knobs into a build screen that is still silent and the frame-rate lesson from 20260819-012130 repeats exactly - a correct number nobody can see reads as the game being broken, which is the report that opened this task. So: sequenced behind the panel, not blocked on it, and both in the same release.

09.8 The retune is the expensive half - and the only thing still genuinely open Open - size it first

Every authored ship's handling changes, and the direction is not the same at both ends. Everything that ships sharpens by 2.3x to 3.2x - a 1-1-1 goes from 5.00 s to 1.55 s, the racer to 1.87 s, the cargoa corvette to 2.10 s, the cargob to 2.17 s - while a hypothetical capital gets 1.6x slower, 5.00 s out to 8.14 s. Both halves are intended: a capital should be a barge, and that second half is the retune cost.

The surface is small where it is authored - 5 hulls in assets/base/ships/base.content.ron and 4 controller sections carrying max_angular_acceleration: 0.5, generated from nova_authoring builders, not hand-edited. The surface is unbounded where it is generated: wfc_ships produces hulls with no notion of a structural budget at all, so it needs a rule that assigns one. The campaign is tuned to current feel; that tuning is what actually has to be re-flown.
