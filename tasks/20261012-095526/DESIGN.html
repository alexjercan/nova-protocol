Nova Protocol / open world / design investigation

Open-World Progression Directions

2026-10-11, revised 2026-10-12 after owner feedback on stations. Research only, nothing approved, no code written. Codebase read at HEAD 6a8b90829; online survey of 40+ games (about 120 searches, 150 pages). The three papers in tasks/20260930-100831/ (2026-10-10) are the nearest prior work and are folded in, corrected where HEAD moved on.

The open world lets you mine, carry, throw, trade, fight and dock, and since 2026-10-09 it remembers everything you did. What it lacks is a reason to come back: nothing you earn changes the ship, credits buy only ammunition and plates, a lost section is gone for good, and no place is different from any other place.

Short answer. Make loss the currency and the station the place it is redeemed. Stations are generated like planets and ships: every living civilization has at least one, and an extinct one leaves a dead station you can only loot. A station is a dockable structure with services no ship offers: rebuild destroyed sections, refit a section for a better one from its stock, buy a whole ship, recycle a derelict you towed in, store parts, sell what you brought back, and post objectives. Its content enriches the existing TAB panes (Map, Ship, Inventory) and adds one pane for what has no home there. Pair that with a New World wizard where you pick a start role (industrial, scavenger, transport, bounty hunter, military, pirate) that resolves to a small hull sized for the inner ring, so the Line Warship becomes a goal instead of the start. Everything else here (tiers by distance, jobs on the Map, salvage, regional shortages, a chart) hangs off that anchor and needs no reputation number. A hired fleet and an owned station land right after Stage 2 so the industrial, scavenger and pirate goals are reachable (decision 13); the civilization stance graph (D13) waits for its research note (decision 12).

Constraints this page accepts as given

  • Relations are Player / Enemy / Neutral today. Decided 2026-10-12: a civilization stance graph (D13) goes to a research note before any code and is built after Stage 2; never a reputation score.
  • No crew simulation, no skill trees, no character levels. The ship is the character sheet.
  • No fuel and no thrust that stops for lack of it. No crafting tree; the only assembly is a station service that turns parts into a section, and trading raw for processed goods fills the rest.
  • Seeded procedural world; closed scripting vocabulary; explicit authoring that fails at lint, then at load.
  • Themed click-based TAB panes; the : command modal stays for commands and cheats.
  • Mods must be able to add stations, items, sections and services through content kinds.
  • Story mode is fine as it is. This page is about New Game only.
  • Exists Verified at HEAD with a path:line.
  • Decided An owner decision recorded in a task file.
  • Proposal A design idea from this page or the prior papers.
  • Open A choice the owner has to make.

Part 2

Four axes of progression that need no levels

Across the surveyed games, every progression system that survives the "no factions, no levels" constraints falls on one of four axes. Nova already has state on each axis; what is missing is a way for the state to improve.

Ship

Capability of the hull: sections, their condition, plating, hold, mounts. The character sheet.

Today: can only get worse. Health, ammo, hold and credits persist (FrozenShipState, spaceship.rs:583-615); nothing adds a section.

Place

Somewhere that is yours or that knows you: a station with your parts, a depot, a sector you cleared.

Today: every visited cell is remembered as left (frozen.rs:270-279), but no place offers anything another does not.

Chart

What you know: surveyed clusters, found wrecks, prices seen. Knowledge as the upgrade (Outer Wilds, Starsector found objects).

Today: the Map shows rings and a hub around the ship; no visited record, no catalogue, no blueprint set.

Unfinished business

Threads the world owes you: a job you accepted, a shortage you heard about, a wreck you marked.

Today: retaliation and targets drop at retirement (ai/frozen.rs:19-42); the world holds no grudges and no promises.

The directions in Part 3 are grouped by the axis they move. The recommended spine moves Ship and Place first, because the research is clear that a service you can only get at a dock is the strongest "come back" driver, and because the owner's station idea sits exactly there.

Player loop

  1. Every living civilization has at least one station, more where it is larger or more advanced, planned per cell like ships and planets. An extinct civilization leaves a dead station: lootable, strippable, no services, no admission.
  2. The Map shows stations as contacts; a visited station keeps its last-seen service list and open objectives on the Map, read-only, so you can plan a return without docking.
  3. Dock. TAB panes gain station content: Map lists the station's objectives and marks them; Ship gains the shop (upgrade a section, rebuild a missing one); Inventory gains station stock and storage. A Station pane holds what has no home elsewhere: the civilization's ship roster, a news or town page.
  4. Each service shows its bill and any refusal reason before Confirm: Rebuild, Refit, Store or withdraw, Sell, Buy station stock, accept an objective.
  5. Undock. The station remembers its stock, your stored parts and the objectives you hold. A rebuilt section regrows its plating on its own, so the paintjob is never a separate purchase (shell_skin.rs:1-33).

What makes it different from docking with a ship

  • Scope of work: only a station does structural work (Starsector Restore, Avorion repair dock, Highfleet Shipworks are all dock-only).
  • Storage: your parts and goods live there, so you return. Sections are never items; the station assembles them from parts.
  • Stock that differs by station, so one yard is the only source of a part.
  • More jobs, and the roster and news pane. Ships post jobs too (below); a station posts more of them.

Jobs on the Map (folded from D6, 2026-10-12)

  • Stations and ships both post jobs. A frozen ship already keeps stock and credits and never leaves its cell, so an open job rides the same ledger and the poster is where you left it.
  • Jobs you accept sit on the Map with markers, not in a board screen. Objectives on the HUD come from the same stack.
  • Stage 1 job types need no located body: bring N of an item (procurement), buy my surplus, deliver this to a civilization role, priced by mass and value. Payout needs item and credit grant actions, which none exist today.
  • Later job types name a generated body and wait for the naming bridge (streamed entities are scoped but not addressable, streaming.rs:176-182): recover a marked item from that wreck; escort that hauler to the next cluster, pirates scaling with its cargo value (Endless Sky); answer a distress call, some of them fake (Elite); neutralize a named hull in hostile space. Folded from D11 on 2026-10-12: these are scripted jobs, never ambient systems.
  • Some offers expire so "come back now" exists; keep the generator small and physical so jobs are never the only thing to do (Freelancer's chore boards).

What exists Exists

  • Docking as a mechanic and an event: one FixedJoint per hull, admission rules, OnDocked/OnUndocked (docking_section/connection.rs:176-250; nova_events/src/lib.rs:435-461).
  • Thrusterless hulls already spawn and survive: the gauntlet Battery, block_wreck_plate (main_menu/gauntlet.rs:100-107; ships/mod.rs:47-48).
  • Civilizations with centroids, status, advancement and a drawn side give a station a natural owner and tier (civilizations.rs:25-47).
  • Frozen ships persist stock, credits and lootable flags; a station would ride the same ledger (FrozenShip, spaceship.rs:583-615).
  • Themed TAB panes with the plan-then-confirm pattern (crates/nova_interface/src/inventory/mod.rs:1-33).

What has to be built Proposal

  • A station identity: a Station content kind and object kind, a dynamic thrusterless body with controllers, a frozen body type. See Part 6.
  • Generator placement: at least one station per living civilization at the cluster nearest the centroid, extra stations by size and advancement, a dead station per extinct civilization, all with the cell-plan id scheme (generation.rs:509-511).
  • Services as content with item costs, each assigned to a pane (Map, Ship, Inventory, Station); docked-only gating with rollback on undock (the lesson kept in tasks/20260824-125943/TASK.md).
  • Stations and ships as a source of jobs from day one: open-world events (sector entered, mined, taken, traded, credits changed; none exist in events.rs:76-160), item and credit grant actions, jobs saved with the world (scenario variables and objectives are not in WorldSaveState, save/mod.rs:103-117), objectives rebuilt on resume.
  • Station storage: a second inventory on the station body, capped.
Mod seam: Content::Station (design id, services, stock, storage cap); mods add stations and services that name their items and sections; unknown ids fail at lint.Pitfall to avoid: a base that only refuels or restocks goes dormant (Everspace 2 home base); a home station that duplicates a public one is a "vanity piece" (Elite carrier).

Player loop

  1. A section is destroyed. A share of its recipe parts drops beside the hold canisters and can be picked up like one.
  2. The ship flies on. The Ship pane marks the placement as "missing" with a rebuild bill.
  3. At a station: Rebuild assembles the design section at full health from its parts recipe plus credits; parts you brought back count against the recipe.
  4. Enemy sections are worth more intact than shot off; the Ship pane shows the salvage value of a lock before you fire.

Why this is the cheapest "sense of progress"

The game already tracks the whole physical ledger of the ship across saves. Giving loss a remedy turns every fight into a before/after the player can see on the Ship pane, which is the Starsector d-mod and Cosmoteer 85% lesson: loss re-enters the economy instead of being subtraction.

What exists Exists

  • Section health graph, prune and cascade, detached pieces that despawn after 30 s with no gameplay state (integrity/mod.rs:1-27; frozen_piece.rs:1-9; explode.rs:79-86).
  • Plating is already handled: plates are fixtures shed by damage and regrown as a pure function of structure, so plate repair "re-attaches" them for free (fixture.rs:1-56; shell_skin.rs:1-33). The owner's "repair the plating that fell off" is the existing M0; the gap is the section underneath.
  • Plate repair rule: 20 HP per plate, anywhere, atomic (inventory.rs:885-948) Decided.
  • Saved destroyed state: a missing FrozenShipState.sections entry means destroyed (frozen.rs:294-299).

What has to be built Proposal

  • The rebuild transaction belongs to the Stage 1 station card (D1): respawn one design section into the live graph and add its entry back to the frozen state; a refused plan changes nothing (same shape as plan_item_trade).
  • The one standalone task in D2: on OnDestroyed, spawn canisters for the remaining hold (and a share of the destroyed section's recipe parts in the same drop). Decided 2026-09-28, reaffirmed 2026-10-12, NOT implemented; a destroyed ship's hold vanishes today (grep in nova_gameplay/src/integrity/*.rs finds nothing). Needed so salvage exists at all.
  • A per-section parts recipe ("build cost") owned by the section config: named part items such as turret barrel, turret body and section panels, plus credits. Sections themselves are never items.
  • No player Neutralized state Decided 2026-10-12: OnDestroyed is the only run-ending event in free play; a no-weapons run is a run. Neutralization stays a scenario-mode tool for AI hulls. A player with no controller cannot steer to a station; Load last save is the exit (decision 9).
Mod seam: a build block on a section (credits, part items, strip yield fraction); lint refuses a strip yield worth more at bid than the recipe at ask.Pitfall to avoid: rebuild dearer than replacement for common sections (Starsector restore formula); an opaque bill (Highfleet, Avorion, MW5); a clean kill paying less than a messy one (MW5).

Player loop

  1. New World is a wizard, not one button: seed, then a start role. Industrial, scavenger, transport, bounty hunter, military, pirate (reconciled to six on 2026-10-12, decision 13). Each role resolves to a base hull plus a kit (credits, plates, ammo, ore) sized to the inner ring's advancement, so the first ship sits at the bottom of the same linear scale the generator uses for everything else.
  2. At a station, Refit (Ship pane): pick a placement, pick a section the station stocks of the same family and footprint. The panel shows cost, thrust floor, controller count, hold delta.
  3. Add a section: pick a free attachment point on the hull, pick a section the station stocks; the same post-build checks validate the result. This is the step that reopens more of the deleted editor, so it follows swap, never precedes it.
  4. Buy an entire ship: the station's catalogue, tiered by its civilization's advancement, so a rim yard sells what an inner one cannot. Hold, credits and bindings move; refused if the hold does not fit.

Slots without slots

Mass, thrust floor, controller count and hull-cube hold already act as a budget (ship_layout.rs:15-33 validates generated layouts this way). Showing that budget in the refit panel is the EV Nova / Star Valor lesson: trade-offs instead of stat inflation, and small hulls keep a role.

What exists Exists

  • Nine base hulls from Cutter to Line Warship, several unarmed (ships/mod.rs:58-111).
  • ShipDesignSource::{Inline, Prototype{patches}} and a bounded per-kind patch vocabulary: thruster magnitude, controller torque, turret fire rate, and so on (ship_design.rs:303-321; patch.rs:155-176).
  • Per-family capability scores the generator already uses to tier sections by advancement (ship_parts.rs:57-73, provisional).
  • Post-build validation of thrust floor, controller count, lanes and connectivity (ship_layout.rs:15-33).

What has to be built Proposal

  • A New World wizard in the main menu: a Content::StartRole catalogue (hull design, kit, starting side, lore line) that mods can extend; lint refuses unknown hulls and items. Today New Game takes only a seed (OpenWorldSession { seed }).
  • The player ship saved as an owned Inline design, not a catalogue reference, so a swap or an added section is a design edit the save keeps.
  • A live section-replace seam plus a frozen-state rebase; the thaw reapplies old health and ammo, so a swapped section needs a fresh entry.
  • A binding rule: a swap keeps the section id so weapon keys survive (open_world.rs:107); an added section gets a new id and an unbound key until the player binds it.
  • Attachment points as section data: which faces of a hull section accept which footprints. The generator already knows lanes and connectivity (ship_layout.rs:15-33); the player-facing rule has to be the same one.
  • Station ship catalogues tiered by advancement, drawn from base and mod ship designs; the pirate role needs a side rule (decision 5b).
  • The in-game ship editor task was deleted for v0.16.0 (tasks/20261007-085535/TASK.md:12). The owner reopened station-side upgrades on 2026-10-12: swap, add, buy, at a station only, never free placement Decided.
Mod seam: sections (exists), ship designs (exists), a build cost per section (new). Player loop

  1. Advancement rises with distance from the origin. Outer rings have richer traders, heavier patrols, better sections on wrecks and rarer ore.
  2. A physical gate marks each ring: dense debris that damages thin hulls, a sensor-noise band that blinds short radar, heavier Armored traffic. The gate is readable on approach, not a wall.
  3. The key to each gate comes from behind the previous one: heavy hull cubes from rim wrecks, a long-range sensor section from an advanced yard.
  4. When the frontier moves out, old rings refresh: extinct-civilization drones escalate, a shortage appears (Terraria hardmode, Starsector derelict drones).

What exists Exists

  • Advancement in [0,1] rising with centroid distance, saturating at 1000 km; stock, credits and ship parts scale with it (civilizations.rs:115-132; sector_ships.rs:92-108; ship_parts.rs:57-73).
  • Three environment fields (material density, volatiles, human activity) per position (environment.rs:19-30).
  • Four ore kinds mapped to four items; asteroid kinds are a Rust constant (mining.rs:151-158).
  • The curve is Linear or Smoothstep to a 1000 km saturation plus a seeded offset in [-0.1, 0.1) (civilizations.rs:41-44, :206-214); the pick is still open (TODO(20260923-110307), civilizations.rs:103-104).
  • Why large ships sit near spawn: civilizations stand 240 km apart (civilizations.rs:25), so the first ring already reads 0.24 and the offset can lift it to 0.34; hull clearance and spine length then interpolate linearly with advancement (ship_layout.rs:89-95, :797-799, :1054-1068), so a 0.34 civilization fields hulls a third of the way to the maximum. The size ceiling is not the problem; the curve and the saturation radius are.

What has to be built Proposal

  • A slower curve with a far radius picked by play, and an offset that no longer swamps the inner rings. The owner's "many small, then medium, large, very large" is a convex ramp (slow early, fast late), which is what Smoothstep already does; a logarithmic curve is the opposite shape. Table in decision 4b.
  • Hazard bands as sector properties (debris density, sensor noise), applied to hulls by plating or section stats. No fuel, so the gate cannot be range; it must be damage, sensing or firepower.
  • Ore tiers by ring and asteroid kinds as content so mods can add ores.
  • A refresh rule for visited rings, designed with streaming: a frozen cell is replayed as left, so a refresh is a delta applied at thaw, not a regeneration.
Mod seam: asteroid kinds and ores as content; hazard tables keyed to advancement bands.Pitfall to avoid: "no more challenge after 0,0" (Avorion); gates that are needle-in-haystack hunts (Subnautica fragments); enemy scaling to the player instead of the place (Everspace 2).

Player loop

  1. Lock a derelict. The readout names each section's family, score, condition and scrap value.
  2. Dock. Strip one section: a timed action; it detaches as a piece and breaks down into its recipe parts in your hold, more of them the healthier it was.
  3. Or tow the whole thing: the docked pair flies as one assembly today. Fly it into a station's service radius and Sell derelict: the station takes it off your joint and pays scrap value for every remaining section.
  4. Stripped wrecks stay stripped; a sold hull is gone from the cell. New salvage needs new cells, which is exploration, not farming.

No blueprints, no known-design set, no teaching a station Decided 2026-10-12: the owner wants few, good features. Salvage pays in parts and credits that the station services (D1, D3) spend. This is the "trade raw for processed" loop: wreckage in, a rebuilt or better section out.

What exists Exists

  • Extinct civilizations leave derelicts: the intact design with seeded breaches, half stock, lootable, no controller (ship_layout.rs:473-486; sector_ships.rs:299).
  • Docked Take from a lootable partner; a repeat claim is refused by frozen state (inventory.rs:519-550; frozen.rs:270-279).
  • The generator already refuses one section id from two unrelated packs (ship_parts.rs:5-10).

What has to be built Proposal

  • A Strip action on a docked lootable partner that uses the existing detach path, yields the section's recipe parts scaled by health, and refuses on a non-lootable hull.
  • Sell derelict as a delivery, not a dock: completes when the docked pair enters a station's service radius; the station removes the derelict body and credits scrap value. One new gate, no new docking rule.
  • Why not dock the pair to the station: one connection per hull (docking_section/connection.rs:100-108), so your hull's port is busy and the derelict has no free one. Many-to-many docking is its own seam (decision 6b), not a dependency of salvage.
  • Strip yield fraction per section as content beside the recipe; lint refuses a yield worth more at bid than the recipe at ask.

Model Decided 2026-10-12 in outline

  • The authored ask_cr and bid_cr on an item are its reference price: the normal, expected, fair number. Nothing else is authored per item.
  • A trader's actual price is computed at the moment of docking: reference times a scarcity factor times a demand factor. Scarcity reads the trader's stock over its target stock (low stock, high ask; glut, low bid). Demand reads the civilization's environment fields: material-poor space pays more for ore, volatile-poor for ice, advanced space sells processed goods cheaper.
  • The factors are content rules keyed by item category and environment field, not per-item overrides, so a mod item with a category and a reference price prices itself.
  • A flat station fee so generic arbitrage loses; money comes from shortages, events and jobs (Starsector's 30% tariff).
  • Two halves with different costs. Variable prices need only stock and environment, both present at dock time, so they can land before the clock. Restock is the drift of stock back toward target over play-time, and it is the first consumer of the world clock (decision 8).
  • Shortage events: "plates short at Harrow for N play-hours", the Rebel Galaxy famine and Starsector disruption that players actually chase. Needs the clock.
  • Cargo mass already caps hauling physically, and more hold means more hull cubes and more thrust, so the trade ladder is a refit ladder.

What exists Exists

  • Fixed per-item ask and bid, deliberately no market (inventory.rs:186-191; tasks/20260926-174806/TASK.md:45) Decided for the first slice only.
  • Finite trader credits and stock that freeze with the sector (sector_ships.rs:104-107).
  • Environment fields per position (environment.rs:19-30).

What has to be built Proposal

  • Prices first: a pricing function that takes the reference, the trader's stock and target, and the civilization's environment, and returns ask and bid; plan_item_trade reads it instead of the fixed fields (inventory.rs:573-657). Target stock per trader comes from the generator's stock plan (sector_ships.rs:92-108).
  • Pricing rules as content keyed by ItemCategory (Raw, Repair, Ammo, Food, Parts exist today) and environment field; lint refuses a rule that names an unknown category or a factor outside its bounds.
  • Restock second: a play-time world clock in the save and a catch-up rule at thaw for stock recovery. Nothing frozen simulates today (nova_world/src/lib.rs:116-117).
  • Map knowledge: a trader's last-seen prices show on the Map after you traded there, so the chart carries price knowledge.
Mod seam: reference prices and categories on items (exist); pricing rules per category and environment field as content.Pitfall to avoid: all fountains and no sinks (X4, Elite billions); a trade loop that stops mattering once credits pile up (Rebel Galaxy); robbing traders: a station of the robbed civilization refuses service for a play-time window (decision 10).

Shape the owner described, 2026-10-12 Research note first

  • Each civilization is its own team. It holds a coarse stance toward the player and toward every other civilization: Ally, Neutral, Enemy. No float, no fame.
  • Your actions move one civilization's stance: destroy or loot its ships and it turns Enemy; its allied neighbours follow; its rival neighbours warm. One hop only, then it stops. Thresholds and a grace count, so one stray shot is not a war.
  • Proximity (owner direction 2026-10-12): edges exist only between neighbouring civilizations, so an event in one cluster never reaches a civilization a hundred sectors away. Civilizations already carry centroids 240 km apart (civilizations.rs:25), so a neighbour is a centroid within a cluster radius, the one parameter to pick. A bloc is then by construction a patch of adjacent civilizations, and the far side of the map stays Neutral to you until you fly there.
  • The seeded local edges carry real variety: blocs, rivals, loners. That is what prevents "enemy with everyone": every enemy you make has a rival next door, so a yard always exists that takes you, and single-hop propagation cannot chain across the map.
  • Rewards hang on Ally: cheaper refit, the roster and escorts for sale, the right to buy a station in their space (D10). Enemy: refused docking, patrols engage on sight. Neutral: today.
  • The hired fleet (owner interest 2026-10-12): several escorts bought from an Ally roster that follow you across sectors, hold, and engage your target. Part 5

Recommended spine and sequence

The order follows dependencies, not preference. Stage 1 delivers the owner's station idea in its smallest honest form and the loss-currency loop it exists to serve. Stage 2 makes the ship a career. Nothing before Stage 5 needs a world clock, which is the single largest blast radius in the list (every frozen body type).

Stage 1. Station body + rebuild

D1 (smallest form) + D2.

  • A Station kind: dynamic, thrusterless, controllers for attitude, docking sections; at least one per living civilization and a dead one per extinct civilization.
  • Services in the existing panes: Rebuild destroyed section (Ship), Buy stock and Store parts (Inventory), station objectives (Map), roster and news (Station pane). Bill shown before Confirm.
  • Jobs that need no located body (procurement, surplus, delivery) posted by stations and ships; open-world events, item and credit grant actions, jobs saved and rebuilt on resume.
  • Separate task, same stage: canister drop on destruction; a share of the destroyed section's parts in the same drop.
  • Map chart layer (sector grid, visited cells, cluster types, found stations and wrecks); a visited and found record in the save from the first format bump (decision 9b).

Proof: a thrusterless hull with controllers stays level under a gravity well and a torpedo hit; an orbiting station survives three orbits and a torpedo hit without tumbling or decaying. Then: dock, rebuild a destroyed bay, accept an objective, undock, cross a sector, resume from disk: the bay is there with fresh state and plating, the objective is still on the Map. A refused rebuild changes nothing.

Stage 2. New World wizard + station upgrades

D3.

  • New World wizard: seed, then a start role that resolves to a small hull and kit at inner-ring advancement.

  • Buy a station from a living civilization; its stock and price rules become yours.

Stage 3. Salvage

D5 + the D4 curve and tier tables.

  • Strip on a docked lootable hull, parts scaled by health.

Why not charter-first, as the 2026-10-10 paper recommended

That paper is right that a shop answers the wrong question when the world has no "why go there". But the owner's complaint is specific: the ship never gets better and nothing pulls them back to a saved world. A charter gives direction without capability; a station with rebuild and refit gives both, and the research is unanimous that dock-only structural work is the strongest return driver. Charters remain the best way to point the player at the first station, which is why charter 0 sits in Stage 6 as content rather than Stage 1 as a system, and why D9's chart layer can land any time after Stage 1 if the owner wants direction sooner.

Part 7

Pitfalls from other games, mapped to Nova

Each line is a documented failure in a shipped game and the rule that avoids it here. Sources are in Part 9.

  • Restore dearer than replace. Starsector's restore formula (hull x 1.2 x 1.2^n) made players buy new ships. Rule: rebuilding a common section costs less than buying it; only rare taught designs cost more.
  • Opaque bills. Highfleet's hypothetical full price, Avorion's one-block shock, MW5's share pricing. Rule: the Station pane shows the exact bill and the refusal reason before Confirm, like the Inventory preflight today.
  • Clean kills pay less. MW5 charged more shares for a headshot mech. Rule: salvage value scales with remaining section health, and the Ship pane shows it on a lock.
  • Loss as pure subtraction. Elite permanent ship loss drove players away; Kenshi limbs and Starsector d-mods were accepted because loss re-entered the economy. Rule: a lost section leaves parts behind, a scarred hull still flies, Load last save stays.
  • Centre is the end. Avorion: "no more challenge after 0,0, the galaxy is not evolving". Rule: design the ring refresh with streaming before the first tier table ships.
  • Gates as walls. Subnautica's ten-hour fragment hunt. Rule: a gate is readable on approach and the key is behind the previous gate; the chart shows counts.
  • Bigger is the only upgrade. Elite engineering favours big hulls; NMS class upgrades make C and B pointless. Rule: the refit panel shows the mass and thrust budget; small hulls keep speed and signature.
  • All fountains, no sinks. X4 and Elite end with useless billions. Rule: rebuild and refit are the sinks and scale with the ring; a flat station fee kills generic arbitrage.
  • Boards as chores. Freelancer's filler missions. Rule: four physical job types, some expiring, never the only thing to do.
  • Numeric goals that change nothing. Hardspace's debt, Cosmoteer's fame. Rule: the capstone build flips the rings.
  • Real-time upkeep. Elite carrier's weekly bill is a "ball and chain". Rule: the clock counts play-time only; nothing charges while the game is closed.
  • Locked verbs. Space Engineers blocks you cannot repair until unlocked. Rule: strip, loot and repair are never gated; only building a design is.
  • Fuel as a tax. Sunless Sea's poverty loop, Highfleet's landed burn. Already avoided: no fuel by decision; gates are damage and sensing, not range.
  • Enemies scale to you. Everspace 2: "obsoletes progression". Already right: advancement is by place, not by player.
  • Enemy with everyone. A single standing that only falls strands the player (Elite notoriety, Starsector's hostile-to-all pirate play). Rule for D13: stances are per civilization, civilizations have rivals, and there is always one way back.
  • Stance becomes diplomacy. Treaties screens and faction quest lines are a different game. Rule: a stance is a Map colour and a refusal reason, nothing more.

1. What a station is in content Decided

  1. New Content::Station kind with its own object kind and frozen body type. Consequence: one more variant through merge, lint, spawn and freeze; services have a real owner. Decided
  2. A flagged Content::Ship with a static flag and a services block. Consequence: cheaper to add, but every ship consumer has to learn the flag and a wreck and a station share a type.

2. Who places stations Decided

Owner picked option 1 on 2026-10-12: generator placement, no authored stations in the base world.

  1. The generator: at least one per living civilization, more by size and advancement, a dead one per extinct civilization. Consequence: many stations in every seed with no authoring; identity comes from the cell plan; the Map fills with places that differ. Decided
  2. Authored hubs in the bootstrap scenario. Consequence: the same stations in every world; residency across streaming needs a rule.
  3. Both. Consequence: two identity schemes to keep straight in the save.

2b. Where station panes are reachable Decided

Owner picked option 1 on 2026-10-12: what a station provides happens only while docked; what you hold or have seen stays visible undocked.

  1. Transactions (rebuild, refit, buy, store, accept a job) only while docked. Held jobs and their markers stay on the Map from the moment you accept them. A visited station keeps its last-seen service list and open objectives on the Map, read-only. The roster and news pane only while docked. Consequence: the Map becomes a planning tool, the dock stays the place where things happen. Decided
  2. Everything only while docked. Consequence: nothing to plan from; every check is a trip.
  3. Roster and news readable from the Map once visited. Consequence: stale information unless the station simulates while frozen, which needs the clock.

3. Station body Decided

Owner direction 2026-10-12: stations are dynamic bodies affected by gravity and hits; their controllers damp spin so they self-level. Needs a test. Two sub-points fall out; both decided below.

  1. Dynamic, thrusterless, with controller sections; sections invulnerable in v1; first proof is the self-levelling test. Consequence: no new physics for docking; the planner must respect drift. Decided

3a. Drift Decided

Option 1 plus an orbit variant. A cluster with a planet gets its station in orbit: placement sets a circular orbital velocity at materialize, and the frozen body keeps pose plus velocity so the orbit resumes on return. A cluster without a planet gets a standing station beside a rock with clearance. Hits nudge either kind; no station-keeping logic; the frozen pose is whatever it was when you left.

Rejected: station-keeping controllers (hidden thrust) and snapping back to the authored pose on load (undoes a hit the player saw).

3b. Docked pair Decided

Docked to a living station, the station owns the helm and the player cannot take it; the station's controllers hold the pair. A dead station (extinct civilization, no services) is a derelict: helm passes to the docked player like any lootable hull. Neutralizing a living station into a derelict waits for station integrity, which v1 rules out (sections invulnerable).

Rejected: letting the player fly the pair (a station that can be towed away by its own customers).

4. Where rebuild happens Decided

Rebuild and refit happen only while docked at a station, paid in credits and parts; plate repair stays anywhere. Sections are never items. A section's cost is a parts recipe owned by the section config (for example a turret barrel, a turret body and section panels); the station assembles the recipe into the section. Strip on a neutralized or derelict hull yields that section's recipe parts scaled by health. Lint refuses a recipe whose parts at bid are worth more than the recipe at ask, so a strip-and-rebuild loop never profits.

Rejected: rebuild anywhere with parts (no return driver, the station is a shop); rebuild at any docked Industrial ship (every trader becomes a yard, the place axis stays empty).

Rejected: Swap and buy only, no adding. Consequence: no attachment-point data needed; a hull's shape is fixed for life, so growth means buying. Rejected: Never; stock is finite forever. Consequence: every station runs dry; the world is a finite mine. An activity clock (sector crossings since last visit). Consequence: cheaper, no catch-up rule, but farmable by pacing.

Rejected: Also end the run when the player's last controller section is destroyed, as a bootstrap handler on the open-world scenario, not a new state. Consequence: no drifting dead end, one more handler, but a ship that could have been docked by momentum is written off. A tow to the nearest station for a fee. Consequence: softer loss, one more service, and a stranded-player rescue.

Part 9

World first, scenarios as the harness Open

Owner direction 2026-10-12, for discussion: the open world is the main mode and the first citizen. Scenarios and nova_events are a harness put on top of it to make the world work in a bounded form. Today the dependency points the other way.

How it stands at HEAD Exists

  • New Game inserts OpenWorldSession { seed } and then loads the open_world scenario named by the base bundle (world_setup.rs:362-365, base.bundle.ron:304). That scenario spawns the player hull and three lights and says of itself "the world is the scenario" (open_world.rs:5-7).
  • The generator arms only while a scenario with role: OpenWorld is live, a session exists and exactly one player ship stands (nova_world_base/src/lib.rs:165-167, :248-252). Any other scenario disarms it. The scenario is the root; the world is a plugin that watches the scenario's role.
  • Chapters, backdrops and the tutorial run in a void: authored objects only, no WorldConfig, nothing streams. Scenario state (variables, objectives, timers) is not in the save (save/mod.rs:103-117), and generated bodies are scoped but not addressable by a handler (streaming.rs:176-182).
  • The role is read in four places: the arming system, scenario lint (lint/scenario.rs:88), probe snapshots (snapshot.rs:437, :2202) and the world-base tests (nova_world_base/src/tests.rs:348-495).

The four role readers above, the picker's assumption that a chosen scenario starts without a world, and any probe that snapshots a world-less run. What must fail loudly. A generated-world harness with no player spawn; a bounded area larger than the active window (160 km cube, nova_world_base/src/lib.rs:139-145); a handler that names a generated id at lint.
