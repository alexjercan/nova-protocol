## Independent review

- An independent review found that relocating an escort or its hull alone could break their authored separation. The same-cell, same-cluster rule for ordinary companions is not a numeric distance limit (an existing test accepts a rock 12 km from its hull). The retry now checks both members and translates a placed escort/hull pair together. A synthetic pair in the reproduced two-well sector previously separated by 8,003 m under the unpaired retry; with a fixed mate the retry faulted. Joint relocation retains the 1,900 m separation, clearances, deterministic positions, and at-most-one-well starts for both. The final disposable 512-seed census generated all 64,000 manifests and 95,248 mobile bodies, with all 65 placed escort/hull pairs in the authored distance band; none of those 65 needed relocation. Log: `/tmp/static-well-escort-atomic-census.log`. Discovery logs: `/tmp/static-well-escort-fixture-valid.log`, `/tmp/static-well-escort-fixture-atomic.log`; permanent regression: `clusters.rs` test `an_overlapping_escort_and_its_hull_move_together_into_one_safe_sector`.
- Final read-only review of joint translation (`clusters.rs:704-912`) found no grounded correctness defect in ownership, mutual clearance, well classification, mutation order, determinism, or fault propagation. It did not audit retry performance or rerun tests. Other focused review found no policy defect in the inspected overlap detection, deterministic seed, clearance checks, retry order or manifest path. The reproduced-rock test alone does not prove a relocated body's one-well tangent or impossible-case fault. The general velocity test covers tangent starts; the recorded 8,192-retry failure shows the named fault path, but neither proves all possible layouts.

## Limits

- The sample does not prove that all seeds can find a valid candidate. Exhaustion still returns a named generation fault, and sector streaming still panics on that fault. A seeded tangent is an initial velocity, not proof of long-term orbital stability. A translated ship retains its original rotation even if its nearest well changes. No matched performance comparison was run; the later visual check is one frame.

## Limits (addendum)

- One frame and a ~153 s Waystation slot with no errors after the local-mod startup failures do not establish orbit boundedness. The 512-seed census checks starting placements, not long-term orbits; the probe covers 360 frames of scenario correctness, not long-term stability.
- The existing `nova_world` test `a_materialized_asteroid_opts_into_gravity` now moves its rock, retires its owning root, then reprepares the same generator and seed in the same app. It asserts the returned rock's stable ID, manifest velocity, dynamic body, gravity opt-in, absence of a well, and new parent root. This proves deterministic rematerialization in the direct materialization path, not the open-world streaming scheduler, orbital phase reset, or long-term orbit behavior.
