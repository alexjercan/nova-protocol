## User facts
- Finish all v0.16.0 work and verification first. Review the last sprint for content quality, how interesting it is, and whether play is fun. The player's experience: getting started is hard, the learning curve is steep, and the game is interesting but not yet very fun. Intended challenge must remain, but repeated frustration may be excessive.
- Specific reported pain: destroyed enemy ships can yield nothing; quest ammunition costs can exhaust supplies for a small reward and block progress; manual docking is hard; RCS depletes at critical moments; exploration is difficult. Do not simply widen the world window without checking performance. Compare the current 3x3x3 vicinity with a proposed 5x5x5 vicinity before changing it.
- Plan v0.17.0 as a "make the world alive" sprint. Research dynamic events, a dynamic jobs list, random or varied restock timers, ambient moving ships, and other ways to make the world feel active. Consider quality-of-life mechanics: easier canister pickup, canister packs with much greater capacity in suitable cases (possibly dynamic size) so defeated-ship loot can fit; a speed-and-rotation sync/align assist with another ship; RCS-powered roll; a neutralized ship braking with RCS; obstacle-avoiding autopilot routes; more interesting torpedo or weapon behavior. Do not implement these v0.17.0 ideas yet.
- Keep docking manual. Distinguish an alignment assist from autopilot docking. Retain skill and meaningful consequences; evaluate unfair dead ends and needless friction.
- Do online research and use Special Fable for independent game-design/content review. Use worker-opus for approved fixes and codex agents for delivery specifications. At most 2-3 parallel agents; no more than two Fable agents over the whole session and do not allow subagents to delegate to Fable. Run UI captures only on an isolated display, never DISPLAY=:0.

## v0.16 source audit (2026-10-26; mechanisms, not a player-flow verdict)
- Starting roles are not equally equipped: credits range from 1,000 (military) to 2,200 (industrial), with 1,000 PDC rounds for four armed roles and 2 torpedoes for military (`crates/nova_authoring/src/base_content/start_roles.rs:31-179`). Market prices vary by region and stock; ordinary unheld jobs expire at 1,200 game seconds, escort offers at 3,600, and station restock recovers over 1,800 (`crates/nova_authoring/src/base_content/market.rs:69-135`). Job reward versus ammo cost remains unmeasured.
- A destroyed section recovers `floor(strip_yield * part_count * health_share)` (`crates/nova_gameplay/src/inventory.rs:1543-1551`; `crates/nova_scenario/src/wreck_cargo.rs:61-159`). Single-count parts at 0.5 strip yield always round to zero; an empty wreck may spawn no canister. Cargo intake takes a whole canister only if free hold mass permits, and leaves a refused canister in the world (`crates/nova_ship/src/sections/cargo_intake_section.rs:596-646`). This explains a possible 'no reward' experience without proving the user's specific encounter.
- Starter RCS is a 300 m/s delta-v budget with 2 seconds idle before recovery at 100 m/s per second (`crates/nova_ship/src/flight/state.rs:158-244,667-668`); a fully drained reserve nominally refills after about 5 seconds *without further use*. Docking already has a sight tied to the nearest-pair logic and requires faces within 10 m, 15 degrees, relative speed at most 5 m/s, and relative angular speed at most 5 degrees/s (`crates/nova_ship/src/sections/docking_section/mod.rs:117-176`; `crates/nova_hud/src/docking_sight.rs`). These are current mechanics, not proposed features. A real docking play must decide whether the constraint is legible.
- Boards already cap at three station jobs and rotate eligible offers on their posting clocks; held jobs pin slots (`crates/nova_gameplay/src/jobs.rs:89`; `crates/nova_world_base/src/jobs.rs:158-346`). Jobs reuse nearby generated wrecks/hostiles and spawn escort/distress bodies on acceptance (`crates/nova_world_base/src/jobs.rs:386-683`). Dynamic events/ambient traffic must add observable behavior, not duplicate existing ship presence or job renewal.
- Basic Training already teaches 13 gated flight/navigation/combat beats as a standalone scenario, not a guided open-world start (`crates/nova_authoring/src/base_content/scenarios/tutorial/mod.rs`). The chart presents never-visited cells as outlines and shows a seven-by-seven cell reach centered on the player (`crates/nova_interface/src/map/chart.rs:8,65-68`). Whether first-session guidance or exploration density is deficient needs a player flow, not this static audit.
- Evidence limit: no new-game onboarding, ammo/reward, salvage pickup or exploration play was reproduced in this source pass; severity and any request to change intended skill are unverified.

## External research (2026-10-26; analogies, not Nova play evidence)
- [W3C Gaming Accessibility GAP Analysis](https://w3c.github.io/rqtf/gaming-accessibility-gap-analysis.html) calls out intentional friction but explicitly lacks a method to distinguish it from avoidable barriers. Nova must validate that distinction in play, not assume it.
- [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 108](https://devdocs.xbox.com/build/game-principles/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/108) recommends separable, granular assists instead of a single global difficulty slider; this is guidance, not outcome data for Nova.
- [CCP's EVE ecosystem outlook](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/the-eve-online-ecosystem-outlook/287097) documents region-specific payouts reacting to activity. MMO economics do not establish useful restock or job cadence for Nova.
- [EVE salvaging documentation](https://support.eveonline.com/hc/en-us/articles/213014209-Salvaging) records separate looting/salvage steps and hard container constraints; it does not justify copying those limits for Nova canisters.
- [Star Citizen flight-model design notes](https://robertsspaceindustries.com/en/comm-link/engineering/15031-Design-Notes-Flight-Model-Changes-In-Star-Citizen-Alpha-20) distinguish coupled drift countering from manual decoupled flight; align/brake assist could preserve manual docking, but the notes contain no Nova user outcome.
- [ORCA paper/project](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ORCA/) describes local multi-agent collision avoidance; it is not evidence that this specific algorithm fits Nova's large obstacles or deterministic paused-map routes.
- [The Art of Screenshake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U) and [Juice It or Lose It](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/juice-it-or-lose) show layered impact feedback, not proof that adding effects fixes weak ammo/reward loops. No strong primary source was found for a space-game torpedo feel, roll rationale or obstacle-aware autopilot design. Secondary Elite/Descent material must not be used as product rationale.
