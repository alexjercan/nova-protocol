Nova Sound Bench

Combat in a vacuum would be silent, and a silent fight is a boring fight. Nova's guns sound the way a film's guns sound - present, bright and physical - and the game does not apologise for it.

A realism mode, where every cue is instead conducted through your own hull or synthesized by the ship's computer, stays on the table as a future setting. It is deliberately not what these are, and it costs nothing to keep open: every world sound is mod content behind an asset reference, so that mode is a second set of files under the same names.

The bench

One decision this raises

The gun runs at 100 rounds a second. The cue that plays it is one sound per round, throttled to twenty a second, because a hundred audio entities a second is not something to spawn - so the shipped gun can only ever rattle. Press the two takes back to back and the gap is obvious.

The fix is the standard one: a held fire loop while the trigger is down, with the single round kept for the ragged ends of a burst. That is a change to the turret cue in ship_audio, which the engine lane currently owns - so it is a call to make, not something to slip in behind it.

Anatomy - every cue is these three layers

Transient

0 - 8 ms

The crack. Broadband and bright: where a gun gets its edge and most of its identity.

Body

10 - 200 ms

Filtered noise carrying the mass, 80 - 800 Hz. The chest punch.

Ring

up to 400 ms

Three to six detuned modes, the hardware answering.

Rules that hold across the set

  • Designed for the rate it is heard at. The PDC is never heard alone, so it is shaped to stack at a 10 ms period - 48% of the fire loop's energy lands on exact multiples of 100 Hz, and that is the buzz. This is the lesson of this round.
  • Mono. The engine pans it. A pre-panned file cannot be placed.
  • Full spectrum. Punch lives under 500 Hz, identity lives 2 - 8 kHz. A cue with only the first is dull; only the second is thin.
  • Attack under 5 ms on anything that is an event.
  • No musical intervals, no arpeggios, no bare square waves. Tonal content belongs to the NOVA OS interface voice, and the separation is what makes both legible.
  • Peak at -3 dBFS and let the per-cue volume constants do the mixing. The file is not where balance lives.
  • Deterministic. Every cue seeds from a hash of its own name, so a rerun is byte-identical and retuning one cue cannot touch another - which is why the railgun is unchanged after a round spent on the PDC.
