Hull Voice Audition

Sound does not travel in vacuum, so everything the pilot hears is either conducted through their own hull or synthesized by the ship's computer as feedback. That is why the game has sound at all, it is what the Vacuum setting turns off, and it is the whole sound-design brief: a gun heard through a deck plate, not through air.

Five cues rendered against that brief - a fast repeat, a heavy one-off, a small hit, a big break and a bed. If the language holds here, the remaining thirty-five follow it.

The bench

Anatomy - every world cue is these three layers

Transient

0 - 8 ms

The mechanical event: a click, a crack, a strike. Broadband and very fast. It is the edge, not the weight.

Body

10 - 200 ms

Filtered noise carrying the mass, 80 - 800 Hz. This is where a PDC gets its chest punch.

Ring

up to 400 ms

Three to six detuned modes, the structure answering. A ring is metal; a tail is a room, and there are no rooms.

Rules that hold across the set

  • Mono. The engine pans it. A pre-panned file cannot be placed.
  • No musical intervals, no arpeggios, no bare square waves. Tonal content belongs to the interface voice, and the separation is the point. This is the no-chiptune rule, stated so it is checkable.
  • Bulk energy under 2 kHz. Air carries the top end and there is none. Every clip below is at 99.5% or better.
  • Attack under 5 ms on anything that is an event.
  • Dry. No tail past about 400 ms anywhere.
  • Peak at -3 dBFS and let the per-cue volume constants do the mixing. The file is not where balance lives.
  • Deterministic. Every cue seeds from a hash of its own name, so a rerun is byte-identical and adding a cue rewrites no other cue's bytes.
