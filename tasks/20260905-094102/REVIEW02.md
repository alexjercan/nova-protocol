# Story review 02

This review checks whether `STORY.md` answers the questions raised in
`REVIEW01.md`. Most of that review is now resolved. The remaining questions are
below. Questions 7 and 8 include answers agreed during this review and should be
folded into the next story revision.

## Overall assessment

`STORY.md` is substantially stronger than the version reviewed in
`REVIEW01.md`. It now resolves the campaign scale, the three crew arcs,
Calloway's reasoning, Pell's final act, Solberg's function, the Datum battle,
Fleet's arrival, the fate of Severance's crew, and the physical module at the
end.

The main remaining weakness is the Kites' collective decision. The story must
explain why Severance's sixty crew participate in the destruction of Meridian
but refuse the same act against Azimuth. That refusal forces Pell toward Datum,
so it must be as clear as Pell's revenge and Halloran's argument.

The recorder also performs many plot functions. It preserves and authenticates
the evidence, beacons when removed, and apparently provides Meridian's active
access code. The access credential may be clearer as a separate recovered
component.

## Remaining questions

### 1. Did Severance's sixty crew knowingly agree to destroy Meridian?

Pell is explicitly motivated by revenge. Sixty Kites crew the warship that
kills Meridian and its 412 people. The same sixty later refuse to attack
Azimuth because its workers are "four hundred of us."

Questions:

- What distinguishes Meridian's workers from Azimuth's workers in their minds?
- Did Pell tell the sixty what he intended before the strike?
- Did he decide to fire only when Meridian appeared in front of him?
- If the sixty knowingly supported the strike, what changes their position?
- If Pell used them without their informed agreement, why do they remain with
  him until the argument?

The answer should appear before or during the Roost argument. It is the cause
of Pell losing control of Severance's next target.

### 2. Does Pell accept the Datum plan, or only substitute Calloway for Azimuth?

The current answer is that Pell has no political strategy. He abandons Azimuth
because the sixty refuse to crew for it, while Datum still gives him Calloway.
That is enough to explain the change of target, but it does not say whether the
archive changes Pell's thinking.

Questions:

- Does Pell recognize that broadcasting the archive can hurt EWI more than
  another destroyed carrier?
- Does he care whether the broadcast succeeds independently of killing
  Calloway?
- Is his defense of the relay his first commitment to the truth, or only a
  tactical route to Calloway?

Pell need not become a theorist or apologize for Meridian. A clear answer would
show whether Halloran changes his purpose or only removes his available crew.

### 3. Can Solberg legitimately decide for all sixty?

Solberg remains at the Roost while the sixty crew Severance. At the argument,
Solberg answers for them that they will not crew for Azimuth.

Questions:

- Have the sixty already discussed and rejected the attack?
- Is Solberg reporting their decision, organizing their refusal, or exercising
  authority over the ship's crew?
- Are some of the sixty still willing to follow Pell?

The strongest staging may be for the crew to refuse visibly and for Solberg to
give their decision a voice. This preserves Solberg's function without making
the sixty passive again.

### 4. What explicit order does Azimuth refuse?

The ending says Azimuth holds station and does not proceed to the cleared site.
That provides a visible consequence of the broadcast, but it is not yet framed
as an active refusal.

Questions:

- Who orders Azimuth to proceed: Calloway before his death, the EWI desk, or the
  Board afterward?
- Does Azimuth answer, or does it simply hold position?
- Does its crew refuse collectively, or does its captain make the decision?

An order heard by the player, followed by refusal or deliberate silence, would
make the first crack in EWI's practical control unambiguous.

### 5. Should the recorder also be Datum's access credential?

The recorder stores the archive, authenticates it, beacons when removed, and
carries Meridian's code into Datum. A removable flight recorder containing a
reusable station credential may make EWI security look implausibly weak.

Questions:

- Does a recorder normally hold an active vessel identity and authentication
  secret?
- Why does Datum accept that identity from a different physical hull?
- Would an intact Meridian transponder, roster token, or authentication unit be
  a better second recovered object?

A separate credential would preserve the open-roster accounting mistake while
letting the recorder remain focused on the evidence.

### 6. How is the archive authenticated after broadcast?

Tamper evidence, append-only storage, and mandatory certification explain why
the archive survives aboard Meridian. The story does not yet explain how a
remote listener verifies the broadcast.

Questions:

- Does the certified recorder sign its records and live playback?
- Does the Datum relay preserve that signature?
- Do receiving ship recorders store the signed original rather than only an
  ordinary audio transmission?
- Is the physical module still the only authenticated original, or merely the
  source device for many independently verifiable copies?

One rule can answer all four: certified recorders sign every entry, and a relay
broadcast preserves the signed record when receiving recorders append it.

### 7. How does the forty-hour air clock fit the trip to the Roost?

Resolved in this review.

Cutter One is a short-shift work boat. It normally returns to Meridian after a
shift of about twelve hours, so it carries stored oxygen and carbon-dioxide
scrubbing capacity rather than a regenerative life-support plant. Its emergency
stores give the three-person crew about forty hours after the strike.

The tender hull that becomes Nova Protocol is large enough for regenerative
life support. The plant initially has no usable power. The crew must recover
breathing stores to extend the immediate deadline and a cell bank to start the
tender's life-support plant. Once Okoro brings that plant online, the forty-hour
clock ends and the two-day trip to the Roost becomes possible.

Use "regenerative life-support plant" rather than only "oxygen generator." The
crew needs carbon-dioxide removal, circulation, water, and power as well as a
source of oxygen.

This also gives the salvage chapter a clear escalation:

1. Recover enough breathing stores to survive the next shift.
2. Recover the cell bank.
3. Power and seal the tender hull.
4. Bring regenerative life support online.
5. Continue searching for the recorder and weapons without the original air
   deadline.

### 8. What happens in the Calloway bad ending?

Resolved in this review.

The player first gives the recorder module to Parallax. Calloway then confirms
rotation for all three and instructs Cutter One or Nova Protocol to follow the
GOTO and dock at Datum. Once Parallax has the only authenticated original, it
no longer needs to preserve the crew's ship. It fires during the approach,
kills the witnesses, and reports a failed recovery or another ordinary loss
under section nine.

This sequence keeps Calloway's behavior internally consistent:

1. Secure the evidence.
2. Separate the crew from cover and point them toward a controlled destination.
3. Destroy the witnesses after the module is safe.
4. Close the roster and write the loss as procedure.

The story should state how the physical handover occurs, but it does not require
full docking. A retrieval drone, transfer canister, or close-range pickup is
enough.

### 9. How do the beacon and distress call escape the stated radio silence?

The world rule says a small ship can contact only what it can see and that Datum
is the only way out of the silence. The recorder beacon nevertheless reaches
the desk within an hour, and the crew speaks with Calloway.

Questions:

- Is Datum in line of sight from the junk site?
- Does EWI maintain receive-only relays that let workers reach the desk but not
  broadcast to the system?
- Does the recorder beacon use a certified network that ordinary ship traffic
  cannot access?
- Does "silence" mean the crew cannot reach the whole Saturn system, rather
  than that it cannot reach EWI?

The simplest rule is asymmetric reach: small ships can contact a visible local
receiver, while Datum and carriers can retransmit system-wide. The crew can
reach EWI through a nearby company receiver, but cannot make the archive public
without Datum's system relay.

## Review01 coverage

### Fully answered

- Full-length campaign scope with a complete Datum ending and sequel room.
- The captain's starting belief in rotation.
- Halloran's homecoming, guilt, and consequential speech.
- Okoro's resolution through evacuating Severance.
- Pell's final distinction from Calloway.
- Calloway's self-justification and personal involvement.
- Solberg's general role and inheritance.
- Calloway's recovery timing and use of the beacon.
- Datum as an active tactical obstacle.
- Pell's pre-strike transmission as a ritual use of EWI's clause.
- Azimuth's purpose.
- Fleet pursuit and arrival.
- Evacuation of most of Severance's sixty.
- Recovery of the physical module.
- Player responsibility for the Datum operation.
- Additional playable Roost missions.

### Answered but needing clarification

- Pell's objective is revenge, intentionally not the strategic motive proposed
  by `REVIEW01.md`.
- Pell changes target when his crew refuses Azimuth, but his commitment to the
  broadcast remains unclear.
- Azimuth stopping is a meaningful result, but an explicit refused order would
  be stronger.
- The recorder rules are substantially answered, but remote authentication and
  use of the recorder as an access credential need clarification.
- Solberg now has a dramatic function, but the sixty should have visible agency.

## Recommendation

Keep the current plot and character outcomes. Revise the causal chain around
Severance's crew, separate or explain Datum's access credential, and fold the
resolved life-support and bad-ending rules into `STORY.md`. After those changes,
the remaining questions are scene-level clarifications rather than structural
problems.
