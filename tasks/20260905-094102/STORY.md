# Nova Protocol: the story

Source of truth for the campaign story, agreed in pairing on 2026-09-05. `design.html` in this folder is the design document: the same material with the system map, the timeline and the storyboard draft of both comics. It is published as the artifact "Off the Roster": https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4af44c85-e778-494d-80f7-5933e640add3. Where the two differ, this file wins.

## Premise

Everyone at Saturn knows EarthWorks Industrial is rotten. Nobody does anything,
because the company owns the ships, the ice, the contracts, and the way home.
Four years ago it staged an accident to finish a competitor, and its own boat
deck officer was lost trying to save the people it left behind. Today he comes
back with a stolen warship and kills the carrier he served on, while the
three-person crew of a small cutter watches from the junk. The campaign is
about what those three do with the one thing everybody wants: the proof.

Theme in one line: everyone knows, nobody does anything. Pell did something,
the wrong way. The crew finds the something that is not Pell's, and then stands
with him anyway.

Tone: greed, not cruelty. Every EWI evil is a cost saving. The crew is used to
it, so the story never underlines it. The reader, who has seen the prologue,
notices for them.

## The world

### Saturn

The campaign happens in Saturn's rings. The rings are the plate: a flat field
of ice, rock and, after twenty years of small companies, scrap. The shepherd
moonlets are the shadows. A hull parked behind one is dark to everything except
a ship on the same side. Water ice is the product. Whoever owns the ice owns
fuel and water for everything that moves in the outer system, so whoever owns
Saturn's ice owns outer-system traffic.

Radio is the second rule of the place. A small ship talks to what it can see. A
carrier or a station relay talks to the whole system. Everything the crew does
after the strike happens inside that silence, and the only way out of it is
Datum's relay.

### EarthWorks Industrial (EWI)

The monopoly on space mining and ice. It buys every competitor it can and
bankrupts the rest, then clears their sites and puts up its own. The slogan is
on every bay wall: WE ARE EXPANDING.

EWI is not cruel. It is cheap. Every evil in the story is a cost saving: a
demolition charge instead of a purchase, no rescue instead of a search, a
renewal clause instead of a ticket home. Contracts renew at the company's
option. Section nine holds the renewal clause and, under it, the Nova Protocol:
when a site is a total loss, its roster closes and the company owes nothing to
the closed lines. Rights are an Earth thing. Different gravity.

The people who work for EWI know all of this and joke about it. That is the
story's tone: nothing is underlined. On the radio the company is warm. Its
ships and stations carry survey words: Meridian, Azimuth, Parallax, Datum. Its
colour on the page is phosphor green.

### Earth Fleet

Earth's military and the only owner of armoured ships. Cut back for years. EWI
bought its surplus, including the demolition charge that killed the Shelter and
the security ship that became Parallax.

Weeks before the story, Fleet lost the warship Resolute to pirates and told
nobody, for fear of public opinion. Its hunt for the ship is quiet, slow and
far away. That silence is why Meridian had no warning. Fleet is not evil. It is
slow, embarrassed, and interested in the story it can tell afterwards. Its
ships carry virtues: Resolute, Vigilant. Blue.

### Kestrel

A small honest ice miner with a few stations around Saturn, the Shelter the
largest. Kestrel named its ships after birds, Osprey and Harrier, and its
stations after the places birds live: the Shelter, the Roost, the Nest.

EWI finished it four years ago with the charge on the claim next to the
Shelter. Kestrel folded within weeks. Its people were bought with EWI
contracts, settlements and silence clauses, or scattered, or joined Pell.
Kestrel is dead as a company and alive as a way of naming things and a way of
talking off the radio. Bone white.

### The Kites

Pell's people. The volunteers who "died" with him, the Kestrel survivors they
pulled out of the Shelter, renewed workers who walked off EWI ships, drifters
who fell off every roster. They live in one of Kestrel's dead stations, the
Roost, and have spent four years scraping weapons and parts out of the junk.
Weeks ago they stole Resolute from Fleet and renamed it Severance. Until then
they were a joke.

EWI, Fleet and the press call them Scavengers. They call themselves the Kites.
A kite is a hawk that lives on what it finds. The name is Kestrel's way, a
bird, and the insult taken back on their own terms. They keep Kestrel's words
for themselves and their homes and give what they take from a company a company
word: Severance, Rotation. Amber.

### Earth

Home. Where rights apply. Where the rotation home goes. Absent from the
campaign and present in every conversation.

## Characters

### The captain (you)

Pilot of Cutter One. Came from Earth four years ago, in the weeks after the
Shelter, straight into the cutter's seat. Just a cutter captain: no rank on
Meridian, no history at Saturn, no memory of Kestrel except the plaque in the
boat bay. Two renewals signed since, neither by choice.

Four years with Halloran and Okoro, so the three are a crew before page one.
The player is new; the captain is not. The captain is the only one of the three
with no side in the old story, which is what makes them the judge in the new
one. The captain decides twice on the page: at the midpoint, to refuse
Calloway, and at the Roost, to go with Pell on the crew's terms. The third
decision has no line. Nova Protocol undocks.

### Nadia Halloran

Copilot of Cutter One. Was Kestrel's pilot, flying the supply boat Harrier, and
was away on a run the day the Shelter died. Came back to a dead station and a
company story, and signed with EWI because there was nothing else at Saturn to
sign with.

She says what everyone thinks and does nothing about it, because nobody does.
That is her character, not a plan. She believes Pell died a hero. She said
once, four years ago, to nobody in particular, that the explosion was planned,
and has not said it since. She calls Datum the Shelter, off the radio and
sometimes on it, which is a policy violation.

At the midpoint she does the math out loud: the man on the archive has come for
the only copy, and three witnesses are a cost. At the Roost she is the one who
says the true thing to Pell's face: Azimuth is four hundred of us and Calloway
is one. She is the one who knows that the word Rotation's pilot uses is a
Kestrel word, and that nobody alive should be using it on the radio.

### Bastian Okoro

Engineer of Cutter One. An EWI lifer. Junior engineer on Meridian four years
ago, on Brandt's deck, fitting Pell's boats. Saw nothing, did as told, and
mourned Pell as the man who took a tender for strangers. Loyal to people, not
the company, and Meridian's people were his.

He is the one who revives the tender hull, grafts the cutter onto it, feeds it
PDCs off a wrecked tug and paints its name on before the seal holds. He plugs
the module in for a beacon log and gets the archive, and hears the voice from
four years ago and the voice from this morning as the same man: "That is... it
can't be."

At the Roost, when Pell says 412 includes Brandt and is not sorry, Okoro says
nothing. The thought only the reader gets: so he was willing to kill me too. At
the argument his line is a question: the man who took a tender for strangers,
where is he.

### Dorian Pell

Leader of the Kites. Ran Meridian's boat deck four years ago. Refused the
no-rescue order under section nine, took a tender and volunteers toward the
Shelter, and went dark. Nobody searched. The press made him a hero and the
files made him a line, and his name went on a plaque in Meridian's boat bay
next to the WE ARE EXPANDING banner.

He and his volunteers and the Kestrel survivors they pulled out became the
first Kites. He has watched Meridian for four years: the ship that carried the
charge, the ship that left him, EWI's Saturn carrier working the ground he
lives on. Weeks ago he stole Resolute. Today he transmits before he fires, so
the recorder holds his voice, and he kills 412 people, most of whom he knew.

Right about the company, wrong about the carrier. Not sorry. Fleet will take
the ship back within weeks, so he hits Azimuth within days: that is his clock.
He first appears at the Roost, on his own ground. He dies with Severance under
Datum's turrets, in the same minute as Calloway, in the light of the rock they
both wanted.

### Ines Calloway

EWI's regional desk at Saturn. Commanded Meridian four years ago and carried
out the Board's order. Promoted to the desk as the reward. The warm voice on
the radio that every crew at Saturn hears every week: rotation home, recovery
in nine days, costs against remaining term.

He is the only person at Saturn besides the Board who knows what the Shelter
was, and the recorder module holds the only copy of his acknowledgement. He
wants the module first and the crew second, and says so kindly. He fires to
disable while the module is aboard. He dies with Parallax at Datum, and EWI
names him the lone author of everything.

### Solberg

Pell's second. Runs the Roost while Pell flies. One of the Kestrel hands Pell's
tender pulled out of the Shelter four years ago. The village is Solberg's and
the ship is Pell's. After Datum, Solberg is what the Kites have left, with the
proof.

### Yusra Demir

Meridian Control. The voice on the work channel in the first beat, and the
voice on the guard channel the cutter hears only in fragments in the second.
Dies in the strike.

### Aurelio Brandt

Meridian's deck chief. Taught Pell to fit boats, and Okoro after him. Dies in
the strike. 412 includes Brandt: that is the number that lands on Okoro when
Pell says it.

### Captain Vance

Commander of Meridian today. Not the commander of four years ago and no part of
the old story. Dies in the strike with the ship.

### Commander Marsh

Earth Fleet, commanding Vigilant, hunting a ship Fleet never admitted losing. A
competent officer in an incompetent service: the hunt is right, and the orders
around it are slow and quiet. Arrives at Datum when it is over and takes the
credit, because that is what the service needs and the account is already
written.

### The Board

EWI leadership on Earth. Ordered the Shelter finished. Never at Saturn and
never on the radio in its own voice. Named in the module and at the end, and
untouched.

## Locations

### The junk site

The opening location. A ring moonlet under dead hulls and scrap nobody can
name, left by the small miners EWI finished over the years. Nobody knows who
owned any of it, which is the point: a company is a plaque, and then it is
junk. Meridian arrives to clear it for EWI's new stations.

The moonlet's shadow is where Severance waited. The junk is where Cutter One
hides during the strike, where the crew finds the tender hull, and where they
lose Parallax at the midpoint. A security ship's hull and sensors do not go
into it. A skiff does.

### Datum, the Shelter

Kestrel's largest station was the Shelter, on a rock in the rings. It died four
years ago. EWI cleared the rock, built its Saturn headquarters on it, and named
it Datum in the survey scheme. Policy bans the old name. Kestrel hands still
say the Shelter off the radio.

Datum holds the system relay. Whatever plays from Datum, every carrier crew,
every bought survivor and every Fleet picket hears. Its turrets are the only
fixed guns at Saturn. Calloway's desk is here. Parallax and Severance die under
its turrets, and the module plays from its relay: the proof plays from the rock
it is about.

### The Roost

One of a dozen small dead Kestrel stations scattered around Saturn,
unregistered, so EWI never cared which one and Fleet cannot find it. The Kites'
village: Kestrel survivors, renewed workers, the volunteers who died, Severance
at the dock, skiffs around it. Amber windows in a bone-white station. Two days
from the junk behind a skiff. Where Pell first appears, where the argument
happens, and where the story ends.

### Meridian's boat bay

Where the captain reports every shift. The WE ARE EXPANDING banner on the wall
and, next to it, the plaque for Pell. Brandt's deck. Lost with the ship, and
the only place in the story where the company's two faces hang side by side.

## Ships

### Meridian

EWI's Saturn carrier. 412 aboard. Carried the charge four years ago under
Calloway. Today under Vance, at the junk site to clear it. Dies in the second
beat, killed by Severance out of the moonlet's shadow. Its recorder module
survives in the wreck, holding the last hours and the ship's long archive: the
Board's order, Calloway's acknowledgement, the no-rescue under section nine,
Pell's refusal, and this morning's transmission.

### Azimuth

EWI's second carrier, arriving in days with 400 workers and the replacement
stations. Pell's intended second target. Its crew hears the module play.

### Parallax

EWI's own security ship, bought Fleet-surplus when Fleet was cut. Calloway's
ship. Less than a warship, more than a tender. Meets the crew on the Datum
approach at the midpoint, fires to disable, and cannot follow into the junk.
Destroyed at Datum with Severance.

### Cutter One

The crew's unarmed cutter. The tutorial ship: GOTO beacons, tagging, retrieval.
Its cockpit and drive go onto the tender's frame in the third beat.

### Nova Protocol

The crew's ship. A dead tender hull in the junk, owner unknown. Okoro grafts
Cutter One onto it: the cutter's cockpit and drive on the tender's frame. Then
piece by piece from the field: a cell bank, two debris PDCs off a wrecked tug,
a better drive. Every upgrade is scavenged. This is the game's progression and
the story's texture.

The name goes on before the seal holds. Section nine of the EWI contract is the
Nova Protocol, the clause that closes a lost site's roster. The crew names its
ship the Kites' way, with the company's own word for what is about to be done
to them, before it has met a Kite. Nova Protocol is too small for the fight at
Datum and stays out of it. It ends at the Roost, off the roster.

### Severance, was Resolute

The Fleet warship the Kites stole weeks ago and renamed with a company word.
Nobody at Saturn was told a warship was loose. Strikes once and moves, because
Fleet is hunting it: it cannot hold position or wait for small craft. Sixty of
the village crew it. Kills Meridian. Destroyed at Datum with Parallax.

### The skiffs

Small scrap-built ships. Sweep, pick, herd. Each carries a company-word name of
its own. Rotation is the one that finds the crew in the junk at the midpoint,
holds fire, and gives them a Kestrel word and a heading. The rest are named as
the comic needs them.

### Vigilant

Commander Marsh's ship. Heavier than Resolute: Severance can outrun it, not
fight it. Arrives at Datum when it is over and takes the credit.

## The prologue comic: The Shelter

A separate comic in the archive, so "four years ago" and "now" never mix. It
shows the staged order and Calloway's face. The reader knows the crime the crew
shrugs at. It does not show Pell surviving: the tender goes dark and the reader
believes the hero is dead, the same as the crew. Three pages.

### Page 1: For the ice

The Shelter on its rock, bone white, amber windows, Kestrel's largest station.
A supply boat leaves it on a run; the reader does not learn until the main
comic that Halloran was flying it. Meridian on station over the adjacent claim,
phosphor green, the banner in its bay. A charge marked on the survey plot:
DEMOLITION, FOR THE ICE. An EWI tender crew placing it, talking about the ice.
Everyone at working level believes it. The page ends with the charge in place
and the Shelter in the background of every panel.

### Page 2: For the Shelter

The order from the Board, on Calloway's screen: text, no face. Calloway's face,
and the acknowledgement in his voice, the words the recorder keeps for four
years. The blast. The Shelter opens. The tender crew placing the charge is
still beside it and dies with it. The reader sees them and understands that
nobody meant to bring them back.

### Page 3: Nobody searched

The desk, in Calloway's voice: no rescue, no witnesses, a reactor failure at
Kestrel. Section nine applies. On the boat deck Pell hears it and refuses.
Brandt fits the boat. Pell takes a tender and volunteers toward the Shelter.
The tender goes dark. Nobody searches. Press: hero. Files: a line. Kestrel
folds in weeks. Last panel: the plaque for Pell going up in Meridian's boat
bay, next to WE ARE EXPANDING.

## The main comic: Nova Protocol

Eleven beats in three acts. Each beat is one scene of the comic and one
scenario or cutscene of the game.

### Act one: the job

#### 1. An ordinary shift

Meridian at the junk site. Dead hulls, scrap, the moonlet, the banner. Cutter
One and its crew: the captain, Halloran, Okoro, four years together. The
captain walks past the plaque for Pell in the boat bay, as every shift. Section
nine is the standing joke: the renewal clause, and under it the Nova Protocol,
which closes the roster of a lost site. Demir on the radio, Brandt on the deck.

The work: a re-qualification card after yard work, which is the GOTO tutorial.
Tag the hulls. Pull a cache. Nothing is wrong, and the reader who has seen the
prologue knows what ship this is.

#### 2. The strike

Out in the junk on a work channel, the cutter hears Control's guard channel
only in fragments: a voice, not Demir's, transmitting before it fires. A hull
with no fleet code comes out of the moonlet's shadow, kills Meridian, and
leaves at once. Nobody at Saturn was told a warship was missing. Skiffs sweep
the field. The cutter hides in the junk. 412 dead. The captain knew them four
years. The reader does not yet know the voice.

### Act two, first half: forty hours

#### 3. Shelter in the junk

A dead tender hull in the junk, owner unknown. Okoro pressurises it, grafts
Cutter One's cockpit and drive onto its frame, and paints the name on before
the seal holds: Nova Protocol, the clause about to be applied to them. Over the
next shifts the crew feeds it piece by piece from the field: a cell bank, then
two debris PDCs off a wrecked tug. Salvage runs into the wreck for air, cells,
and the recorder module, which also holds Meridian's log archive.

Distress to the desk. Calloway, warm: recovery in nine days, hold position,
costs against remaining term. Air is forty hours. Hold position is section nine
said kindly. Nobody on the crew is surprised.

#### 4. The module

Okoro plugs the module in for the beacon log and gets the archive. Four years
ago: the Board's order, Calloway's acknowledgement in a voice the crew hears
every week, the no-rescue under section nine, Pell refusing. This morning: the
transmission before the shot, the same man. Okoro: "That is... it can't be."
Halloran said once, four years ago, to nobody, that it was planned. She does
not say it again. She does not have to.

#### 5. The run

Scavengers scavenge. The skiffs return to strip the carcass and find a live
tender. The crew runs for EWI's nearest safe harbour, Datum, the company's name
for the rock where Halloran used to live. Calloway's channel says Datum.
Halloran says the Shelter.

Nova Protocol's PDCs against skiffs through the ring junk. First fight. The
skiffs herd more than they hit, and the crew, who have never been shot at,
cannot tell herding from hunting.

### Midpoint

#### 6. Recovery

Calloway's security ship meets them on the Datum approach, early after all, and
asks for the module first and the crew second. Halloran does the math out loud:
the man on the archive has come for the only copy, and three witnesses are a
cost. The captain's call.

Refuse, and Calloway fires to disable, not to kill: the module is aboard. Nova
Protocol dives back into the ring junk, where a security ship's hull and
sensors do not go, and loses him. A skiff finds them there, holds fire, and
speaks on a work channel with a Kestrel word nobody alive uses on the radio:
the Roost, and a heading. The skiffs were herding them away from Datum all
morning. The crew fought the people trying to warn them, to reach the man
trying to kill them. The skiff's name, in company letters on scrap: Rotation.

The other choice is game only. Hand the module over and fly the GOTO to the
station. Parallax fires on the approach. Game over, the record buried. A short,
early bad ending that teaches the player what the company is. The comic does
not show it.

### Act two, second half: the other side

#### 7. The Roost

One of a dozen dead Kestrel stations, two days behind Rotation. A village:
Kestrel survivors, renewed workers, the volunteers who died. Severance at the
dock, the first time the crew sees the ship that killed Meridian. Pell alive,
on his own ground, his first page. Okoro's reunion.

Pell says what he did and why and is not sorry, and 412 includes Brandt. Okoro
says nothing. A thought bubble only the reader gets: so he was willing to kill
me too. Pell's clock: Fleet will take the ship back within weeks, so he hits
Azimuth within days. Sixty of the village crew Severance.

#### 8. The argument

The module is worth more than the ship: proof, with Calloway's voice on it.
EWI's deal is a trap with a warm voice. Fleet would seize the module and lose
it. Pell is right about the company and wrong about the carrier.

Halloran, who has said what everyone thinks for four years, says it to Pell:
Azimuth is four hundred of us and Calloway is one. Okoro: the man who took a
tender for strangers, where is he. The captain decides. Comic and game go with
Pell, on the crew's terms: Calloway and the truth, not the carrier. No fork
here.

### Act three: Datum

#### 9. The plan

Datum holds the system relay. Play the module from there and every carrier
crew, every bought survivor and every Fleet picket hears it. Calloway will be
there: it is headquarters, and he is the one man who cannot let it play.
Severance draws the fight. Nova Protocol goes for the relay.

#### 10. The climax

Severance against Parallax under Datum's turrets. The duel Pell has wanted for
four years. Nova Protocol is too small for it and stays out of it: while the
two ships tear at each other, the crew slips through the debris and docks at
the relay.

The module plays: the order, Calloway's voice, the no-rescue, Pell's refusal,
this morning's shot. The sixty hear their officer from four years ago.
Azimuth's crew hears why. Outside, Parallax and Severance finish each other.
Calloway and Pell die in the same minute, in the light of the rock they both
wanted.

#### 11. Minute zero

Vigilant arrives when it is over and takes the victory: two hostile vessels
destroyed, the pirate leader dead, the relay secured, law and order at Saturn.
The official account has no civilians at the relay. EWI plays dumb: Calloway
acted alone, the Board knew nothing, the company thanks Fleet, and the three
who exposed a rogue officer are heroes, with the rotation home to prove it. The
press prints all of it. Every worker who heard the module knows better.

The Kites have lost their ship and their leader and gained proof and a cause.
The Board is named and untouched. The crew does not stay for any of it. Nova
Protocol undocks while Vigilant is still decelerating and is gone into the ring
junk before the account is written. The praise and the rotation home go out to
a channel nobody answers. Last page: the Roost, three people, one ship, the
module. Off the roster.

### Progression

The crew's ship is built from what they find. Cutter One is unarmed. Nova
Protocol starts as Cutter One grafted onto a scrap hull and grows piece by
piece: cells, two debris PDCs, a better drive. Every upgrade is scavenged.

### Branches

- **Midpoint bad ending (game only).** Trust Calloway, hand over the module,
  fly the GOTO, get blasted on the approach. Short, early, and it teaches the
  player what the company is. The comic does not show it.
- **The argument (no fork).** Comic and game both go with Pell against

  branches.
- **Calloway and Pell** die with their ships at Datum. Neither fate is open.
- **The ending (no fork).** The final scenario ends with the run to the Roost.
  The comic ends on the same page.

## How the company reads on the page

Never a speech. Only habits and paperwork:

- The plaque for Pell next to the WE ARE EXPANDING banner.
- Datum on the radio, the Shelter in the galley. The old name is a policy
  violation.
- Section nine, the renewal clause, as a crew joke.
- Recovery in nine days for a crew with forty hours, with costs against
  remaining term, delivered kindly.
- Survivors "bought": contracts, settlements, silence.
- Fleet-surplus warheads sold to a company for cracking ice.
- "Rights are an Earth thing. Different gravity." A crew line, not a villain's.
- Heroes in the press, a line in the files, a plaque in the bay.

## Rules of the world

- Small-ship radio is local. A carrier or a station relay reaches the system.
  This is why Datum matters and why nobody hears the crew.
- Cutter One is unarmed. Nova Protocol has two debris PDCs. Severance is a
  warship. Parallax is a security ship: less than a warship, more than a
  tender.
- Parallax fires to disable while the module is aboard: Calloway wants the only
  copy intact. Ring junk is the crew's cover from anything bigger than a skiff.
- Severance strikes once and moves. It cannot hold position or wait for small
  craft, because Fleet is hunting it. Skiffs do the small work.
- Fleet lost a warship to pirates and kept it secret, for fear of public
  opinion. Nobody at Saturn knew a warship was loose, so Meridian had no
  warning. Fleet is slow, far, and embarrassed.
- Vigilant outguns Severance. Pell can run from Fleet, not fight it. This is
  his clock.
