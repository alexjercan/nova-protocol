# Nova Protocol: the story

Source of truth for the campaign story, agreed in pairing on 2026-09-05 and tightened the same day against `REVIEW01.md`. `design.html` in this folder is the design document: the who-is-who tables, the system map, the timeline and the storyboard draft of both comics. It is published as the artifact "Off the Roster": https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4af44c85-e778-494d-80f7-5933e640add3. Where the two differ, this file wins. The design page still shows the story as it stood before the review.

## Premise

Everyone at Saturn knows EarthWorks Industrial is rotten. Nobody does anything,
because the company owns the ships, the ice, the contracts, and the way home.
Four years ago it staged an accident to finish a competitor, and its own boat
deck officer was lost trying to save the people it left behind. Today he comes
back with a stolen warship and kills the carrier he served on, while the
three-person crew of a small cutter watches from the junk. The campaign is
about what those three do with the one thing everybody wants: the proof.

Theme in one line: everyone knows, nobody does anything. Pell did something,
the wrong way. The crew finds the something that is not Pell's, and then stands
with him anyway. At the end the institutions absorb the story, as they always
do. What they cannot absorb is the record.

Tone: greed, not cruelty. Every EWI evil is a cost saving. The crew is used to
it, so the story never underlines it. The reader, who has seen the prologue,
notices for them.

Scale: a full-length first campaign. The eleven comic beats are the spine and
reach a complete ending at Datum and the Roost. The game adds missions along
the spine, most of them at the Roost, so the middle is played and not told. The
Board, Fleet's answer, and what the off-roster people do with the proof are
left for a later campaign.

## The world

### Saturn

The campaign happens in Saturn's rings. The rings are the plate: a flat field
of ice, rock and, after twenty years of small companies, scrap. The shepherd
moonlets are the shadows. A hull parked behind one is dark to everything except
a ship on the same side. Water ice is the product. Whoever owns the ice owns
fuel and water for everything that moves in the outer system, so whoever owns
Saturn's ice owns outer-system traffic.

Radio is the second rule of the place. A small ship talks to what it can see. A
carrier or a station relay talks to the whole system. Everything the crew does
after the strike happens inside that silence, and the only way out of it is
Datum's relay.

### EarthWorks Industrial (EWI)

The monopoly on space mining and ice. It buys every competitor it can and
bankrupts the rest, then clears their sites and puts up its own. The slogan is
on every bay wall: WE ARE EXPANDING.

EWI is not cruel. It is cheap. Every evil in the story is a cost saving: a
demolition charge instead of a purchase, no rescue instead of a search, a
renewal clause instead of a ticket home. Contracts renew at the company's
option. Rights are an Earth thing. Different gravity.

Section nine of every EWI contract holds the renewal clause and, under it, the
Nova Protocol. The Nova Protocol is the company's name for writing off its own
people when a site is lost: roster closed, no recovery, the standard letter to
next of kin, costs against remaining term. Everyone at Saturn knows the term
the way soldiers know "missing in action". It was applied to the tender crew at
the Shelter. It is applied to Meridian's crew, and to the three of Cutter One,
with a warm voice. A closed roster puts the loss on the desk's books, so a desk
files it as late as it can.

Every carrier carries a certified recorder: tamper-evident, append-only, and
required for the ship's certification, so the desk can purge everything else
and not this. It beacons when unseated. The rule that kept the crime is the
rule that keeps the proof.

The people who work for EWI know all of this and joke about it. That is the
story's tone: nothing is underlined. On the radio the company is warm. Its
ships and stations carry survey words: Meridian, Azimuth, Parallax, Datum. Its
small craft are pickets. Its colour on the page is phosphor green.

### Earth Fleet

Earth's military and the only owner of armoured ships. Cut back for years. EWI
bought its surplus, including the demolition charge that killed the Shelter and
the security ship that became Parallax.

Weeks before the story, Fleet lost the warship Resolute to pirates and told
nobody, for fear of public opinion. Its hunt for the ship is quiet, slow and
far away, and it hunts the way Fleet hunts: by transmissions. Every second
Severance spends on the air is a second for Vigilant to fix it. Fleet challenge
traffic reaches the junk site in fragments through the whole campaign and
nobody cares, because Fleet is always looking for something. That silence is
why Meridian had no warning. Fleet is not evil. It is slow, embarrassed, and
interested in the story it can tell afterwards. Its ships carry virtues:
Resolute, Vigilant. Blue.

### Kestrel

A small honest ice miner with a few stations around Saturn, the Shelter the
largest. Kestrel named its ships after birds, Osprey and Harrier, and its
stations after the places birds live: the Shelter, the Roost, the Nest.

EWI finished it four years ago with the charge on the claim next to the
Shelter. Kestrel folded within weeks. Its people were bought with EWI
contracts, settlements and silence clauses, or scattered, or joined Pell.
Kestrel is dead as a company and alive as a way of naming things and a way of
talking off the radio. Bone white.

### The Kites

Pell's people. The volunteers who "died" with him, the Kestrel survivors they
pulled out of the Shelter, renewed workers who walked off EWI ships, drifters
who fell off every roster. They live in one of Kestrel's dead stations, the
Roost, and have spent four years scraping weapons and parts out of the junk.
Weeks ago they stole Resolute from Fleet and renamed it Severance. Until then
they were a joke.

The Kites are not one mind. Sixty of the village crew Severance, and Pell
cannot fly it without them. They came to the junk site to strip a wreck, not
to find proof, and they learn what the crew carries from Calloway's guns. They
will not crew for Azimuth.

EWI, Fleet and the press call them Scavengers. They call themselves the Kites.
A kite is a hawk that lives on what it finds. The name is Kestrel's way, a
bird, and the insult taken back on their own terms. They keep Kestrel's words
for themselves and their homes and give what they take from a company a company
word: Severance, Rotation. Amber.

### Earth

Home. Where rights apply. Where the rotation home goes. Absent from the
campaign and present in every conversation.

## Characters

### The captain (you)

Pilot of Cutter One. Came from Earth four years ago, in the weeks after the
Shelter, straight into the cutter's seat. Just a cutter captain: no rank on
Meridian, no history at Saturn, no memory of Kestrel except the plaque in the
boat bay. Two renewals signed since, neither by choice.

The captain believes in rotation. Of the three, only the captain has an Earth
to go back to and still laughs at the section nine joke, because for the
captain the renewal clause is a delay and not a sentence. Halloran and Okoro
stopped laughing years ago. That belief is what the story attacks. Calloway
offers the captain rotation home by name at the midpoint, approved for all
three, effective on docking, in the voice on the archive. The company offers
it again at the end, publicly and sincerely. The channel goes unanswered.

Four years with Halloran and Okoro, so the three are a crew before page one.
The player is new; the captain is not. The captain is the only one of the three
with no side in the old story, which is what makes them the judge in the new
one. The captain decides twice on the page: at the midpoint, to refuse
Calloway, and at the Roost, to go with Pell on the crew's terms. At Datum the
captain stays at the helm while the others go inside: the ship is the way out,
and the player never leaves it. The third decision has no line. Nova Protocol
undocks, and the rotation home goes out to nobody.

### Nadia Halloran

Copilot of Cutter One. Was Kestrel's pilot, flying the supply boat Harrier, and
was away on a run the day the Shelter died. Came back to a dead station and a
company story, and signed with EWI because there was nothing else at Saturn to
sign with.

She says what everyone thinks and does nothing about it, because nobody does.
That is her character, not a plan. She believes Pell died a hero. She said
once, four years ago, to nobody in particular, that the explosion was planned,
and has not said it since. She calls Datum the Shelter, off the radio and
sometimes on it, which is a policy violation.

At the midpoint she does the math out loud: the man on the archive has come for
the only copy, and three witnesses are a cost. She is the one who knows that
the word Rotation's pilot uses is a Kestrel word, and that nobody alive should
be using it on the radio.

The Roost is her homecoming and her accusation. The village is the people who
did not sign, and she signed. Solberg says it to her face. Her answer is her
turn: yes, and she has brought the thing that voids every signature. At the
argument she does not argue with Pell. She talks over him to the sixty who
crew his ship: Azimuth is four hundred of us and Calloway is one. At Datum she
goes inside with the module and plays it from the relay. What she said once to
nobody, said to everyone.

### Bastian Okoro

Engineer of Cutter One. An EWI lifer. Junior engineer on Meridian four years
ago, on Brandt's deck, fitting Pell's boats. Learned the boat deck from Pell:
the deck where the tenders sit, and the boat-deck officer's order that empties
a dying ship, "all hands, tenders away". Saw nothing, did as told, and mourned
Pell as the man who took a tender for strangers. Loyal to people, not the
company, and Meridian's people were his.

He is the one who revives the tender hull, grafts the cutter onto it, feeds it
PDCs off a wrecked tug and paints its name on before the seal holds. He plugs
the module in for a beacon log and gets the archive, and hears the voice from
four years ago and the voice from this morning as the same man: "That is... it
can't be."

At the Roost, when Pell says 412 includes Brandt and is not sorry, Okoro says
nothing. The thought only the reader gets: so he was willing to kill me too. At
the argument his line is a question: the man who took a tender for strangers,
where is he. Pell does not answer.

At Datum Okoro is at the relay console when Severance starts to die, with Pell
at its helm and nobody ordering the crew off. He gets on Datum's channel to
Severance and gives the order himself, in Pell's words: all hands, tenders
away. Pell's last transmission is to his own deck: do as he says. The sixty go
to the boats. The man who gets strangers into the tenders is Okoro. That is
the answer to his question, and he gives it himself.

### Dorian Pell

Leader of the Kites. Ran Meridian's boat deck four years ago. Refused the
no-rescue order under section nine, gave the abandon-ship order against
orders, "all hands, tenders away", took a tender and volunteers toward the
Shelter, and went dark. Nobody searched. The press made him a hero and the
files made him a line, and his name went on a plaque in Meridian's boat bay
next to the WE ARE EXPANDING banner.

He and his volunteers and the Kestrel survivors they pulled out became the
first Kites. He has watched Meridian for four years: the ship that carried the
charge, the ship that left him, EWI's Saturn carrier working the ground he
lives on. Weeks ago the Kites stole Resolute, and Pell had a warship and the
company in front of it. He is revenge, not theory. He has no plan for the
monopoly and no theory of the workers. He read Meridian its own clause over
the guard channel and fired, and he knows it was wrong. "I had to," and he
means it. He did not come for proof. He came for the ship he served on, and
his next target is the next carrier.

He learns about the archive at the Roost, when Okoro plays the module in his
village and he hears his own refusal from four years ago with the sixty
listening. His answer is the theme's line: everyone knew, nobody did anything,
he did. He takes Datum because the sixty will not crew for Azimuth, because he
cannot fly Severance alone, and because Datum still gives him Calloway. He
knows the broadcast hands Vigilant the fix. He goes anyway.

His last act is the tender for strangers. When Calloway turns Parallax on the
relay tower, Pell puts Severance between them and drives it in. He tells his
deck to obey Okoro. A few stay with him by choice. He never apologises for
Meridian. He dies in the same minute as Calloway, in the light of the rock
they both wanted, and not for the same reason.

### Ines Calloway

EWI's regional desk at Saturn. Commanded Meridian four years ago and carried
out the Board's order. Promoted to the desk as the reward. The warm voice on
the radio that every crew at Saturn hears every week: rotation home, recovery
in nine days, costs against remaining term.

He does not think of himself as a murderer. He made an allocation under
emergency policy, and he would make it again. He is the only person at Saturn
besides the Board who knows what the Shelter was. He learns the recorder is
out and live from its beacon, within the hour, and comes himself because
nobody else can be told what it holds. Hold position was meant to kill the
crew by paperwork before he arrived. He has not filed section nine on
Meridian: a closed roster puts 412 on the desk's books before he has the
module, so the dead ship's code is still on Datum's roster. He offers the
captain rotation home by name, and he fires to disable while the module is
aboard.

At Datum he cannot win the duel before the module finishes, so he turns
Parallax on the relay tower: his own station, his own people, a cost against
the record. It is the Shelter decision made a second time, with witnesses, and
it is why EWI can afterwards blame him alone and be believed. He dies with
Parallax. EWI names him the lone author of everything, and he did fire on his
own station.

### Solberg

Pell's second. Runs the Roost while Pell flies. One of the Kestrel hands Pell's
tender pulled out of the Shelter four years ago. The village is Solberg's and
the ship is Pell's.

Solberg is the one who tells Halloran she signed, and the one who answers for
the sixty at the argument: they will not crew for Azimuth. Solberg's voice
runs the Roost defense. After Datum, Solberg is what the Kites have: most of
the sixty back in the boats, the authenticated original, and a cause.

### Yusra Demir

Meridian Control. The voice on the work channel in the first beat, and the
voice on the guard channel the cutter hears only in fragments in the second.
Dies in the strike.

### Aurelio Brandt

Meridian's deck chief. Taught Pell to fit boats, and Okoro after him. Dies in
the strike. 412 includes Brandt: that is the number that lands on Okoro when
Pell says it.

### Captain Vance

Commander of Meridian today. Not the commander of four years ago and no part of
the old story. Dies in the strike with the ship.

### Commander Marsh

Earth Fleet, commanding Vigilant, hunting a ship Fleet never admitted losing,
by its transmissions. A competent officer in an incompetent service: the hunt
is right, and the orders around it are slow and quiet. The broadcast from
Datum is the fix Marsh has waited weeks for. Vigilant arrives when it is over
and takes the credit, because that is what the service needs and the account
is already written. Vigilant's own recorder holds the broadcast, and Marsh
cannot classify it.

### The Board

EWI leadership on Earth. Ordered the Shelter finished. Never at Saturn and
never on the radio in its own voice. Named in the module and at the end, and
untouched.

## Locations

### The junk site

The opening location. A ring moonlet under dead hulls and scrap nobody can
name, left by the small miners EWI finished over the years. Nobody knows who
owned any of it, which is the point: a company is a plaque, and then it is
junk. Meridian arrives to clear it for EWI's new stations. Fleet challenge
traffic reaches it in fragments, and nobody cares.

The moonlet's shadow is where Severance waited. The junk is where Cutter One
hides during the strike, where the crew finds the tender hull, and where they
lose Parallax at the midpoint. A security ship's hull and sensors do not go
into it. A skiff does.

### Datum, the Shelter

Kestrel's largest station was the Shelter, on a rock in the rings. It died four
years ago. EWI cleared the rock, built its Saturn headquarters on it, and named
it Datum in the survey scheme. Policy bans the old name. Kestrel hands still
say the Shelter off the radio.

Datum holds the system relay. Whatever plays from Datum, every carrier crew,
every bought survivor and every Fleet picket hears, and every recorder at
Saturn keeps. Its turrets are the only fixed guns at Saturn, run from control
nodes and sensor masts a small ship can kill, and its automation admits any
hull squawking a code on an open EWI roster. Meridian's code is still open.
Calloway's desk is here. Nova Protocol comes in under a dead ship's name and
silences the guns. Parallax and Severance die under them. Calloway fires on
the relay tower and Pell puts his ship in the way. The proof plays from the
rock it is about.

### The Roost

One of a dozen small dead Kestrel stations scattered around Saturn,
unregistered, so EWI never cared which one and Fleet cannot find it. The Kites'
village: Kestrel survivors, renewed workers, the volunteers who died, Severance
at the dock, skiffs around it. Amber windows in a bone-white station. Two days
from the junk behind a skiff. Where Pell first appears, where Okoro plays the
module in the village, where the argument happens, where the three Roost
missions launch from, and where the story ends. Parallax's pickets find it by
following Rotation home.

### The Nest

A second dead Kestrel station, stripped to the frame. The Kites take Nova
Protocol there with Rotation for the refit: a real drive, a third PDC, a sensor
mast. Where the player learns how the Kites pick a wreck, with the skiffs as
allies for the first time.

### The approach lane

Azimuth's lane in from the outer system. Nova Protocol shadows it with
Rotation, engines cold, and listens to four hundred voices make the renewal
joke. The player hears Azimuth's crew before Halloran speaks for them.

### Meridian's boat bay

Where the captain reports every shift. The WE ARE EXPANDING banner on the wall
and, next to it, the plaque for Pell. Brandt's deck, where Pell gave the order
"all hands, tenders away" against orders and where Okoro learned it. Lost with
the ship, and the only place in the story where the company's two faces hang
side by side.

## Ships

### Meridian

EWI's Saturn carrier. 412 aboard. Carried the charge four years ago under
Calloway. Today under Vance, at the junk site to clear it. Dies in the second
beat, killed by Severance out of the moonlet's shadow. Its recorder module
survives in the wreck, holding the last hours and the ship's long archive: the
Board's order, Calloway's acknowledgement, the no-rescue under section nine,
Pell's refusal, and this morning's reading of the clause. Its roster is still
open days after it dies, because closing it costs, and the open code is what
admits Nova Protocol to Datum.

### Azimuth

EWI's second carrier, days out on the approach lane with four hundred workers
and the replacement installations for the cleared site. It is the thing that
turns a dead miner's claim into a company site under the banner. Pell's
intended second target. The crew shadows it from the Roost and hears its
people. Its recorder holds the broadcast. Afterwards it holds station and does
not proceed to the site. The banner ship stops expanding.

### Parallax

EWI's own security ship, bought Fleet-surplus when Fleet was cut. Calloway's
ship. Less than a warship, more than a tender, and it carries pickets: small
security craft that sweep the junk for Nova Protocol and follow Rotation's
track to the Roost. Meets the crew on the Datum approach at the midpoint, fires
to disable, and cannot follow into the junk. Comes back to Datum at speed when
the module plays, duels Severance, turns on the relay tower, and dies with
Severance in it.

### Cutter One

The crew's unarmed cutter. The tutorial ship: GOTO beacons, tagging, retrieval.
Its cockpit and drive go onto the tender's frame in the third beat.

### Nova Protocol

The crew's ship. A dead tender hull in the junk, owner unknown. Okoro grafts
Cutter One onto it: the cutter's cockpit and drive on the tender's frame. Then
piece by piece from the field: a cell bank, two debris PDCs off a wrecked tug.
At the Roost the Kites give it real parts: a drive, a third PDC, a sensor mast.
Every upgrade is scavenged. This is the game's progression and the story's
texture. It has room for a handful of Kites, which is how the crew goes into
Datum with help.

The name goes on before the seal holds. The Nova Protocol is the company's
term for a closed roster, under section nine of the EWI contract. The crew
names its ship the Kites' way, with the company's own word for what is being
done to them, before it has met a Kite. Nova Protocol flies to Datum under
Meridian's code, kills the turret control, holds the dock while the module
plays, covers Severance's boats into the junk, and ends at the Roost, off the
roster.

### Severance, was Resolute

The Fleet warship the Kites stole weeks ago and renamed with a company word.
Nobody at Saturn was told a warship was loose. Strikes once and moves, and
keeps every transmission short, because Fleet hunts by transmissions. Sixty of
the village crew it, and Pell cannot fly it without them. Kills Meridian. At
Datum it comes in after Nova Protocol has silenced the turrets, to extract the
crew and to meet Parallax. When Calloway turns on the relay tower, Pell drives
it between them. Most of the sixty leave in its boats on Okoro's call.
Destroyed with Parallax.

### The skiffs

Small scrap-built ships. Sweep and pick. Each carries a company-word name of
its own. They come to the junk site to strip Meridian's carcass, find a live
tender with EWI cockpit glass and a fresh cell bank, and read it as a company
survivor with salvage aboard: a witness and a prize. They chase it for real.
When Parallax fires on the tender they were chasing, Rotation's pilot puts it
together: a company boat the company wants dead is carrying something.
Rotation follows Nova Protocol into the junk, holds fire, and gives a Kestrel
word and a heading. From the Roost on, the skiffs fight beside the crew. The
rest are named as the comic needs them.

### Vigilant

Commander Marsh's ship. Heavier than Resolute: Severance can outrun it, not
fight it. Hunts by transmissions. The broadcast from Datum is its fix. Arrives
when it is over, decelerating, and takes the credit.

## The prologue comic: The Shelter

A separate comic in the archive, so "four years ago" and "now" never mix. It
shows the staged order and Calloway's face. The reader knows the crime the crew
shrugs at. It does not show Pell surviving: the tender goes dark and the reader
believes the hero is dead, the same as the crew. Three pages.

### Page 1: For the ice

The Shelter on its rock, bone white, amber windows, Kestrel's largest station.
A supply boat leaves it on a run; the reader does not learn until the main
comic that Halloran was flying it. Meridian on station over the adjacent claim,
phosphor green, the banner in its bay. A charge marked on the survey plot:
DEMOLITION, FOR THE ICE. An EWI tender crew placing it, talking about the ice.
Everyone at working level believes it. The page ends with the charge in place
and the Shelter in the background of every panel.

### Page 2: For the Shelter

The order from the Board, on Calloway's screen: text, no face. Calloway's face,
and the acknowledgement in his voice, the words the recorder keeps for four
years. The blast. The Shelter opens. The tender crew placing the charge is
still beside it and dies with it. The reader sees them and understands that
nobody meant to bring them back.

### Page 3: Nobody searched

The desk, in Calloway's voice: no rescue, no witnesses, a reactor failure at
Kestrel. Section nine applies. On the boat deck Pell hears it and refuses:
"All hands, tenders away." Brandt fits the boat. Pell takes a tender and
volunteers toward the Shelter. The tender goes dark. Nobody searches. Press:
hero. Files: a line. Kestrel folds in weeks. Last panel: the plaque for Pell
going up in Meridian's boat bay, next to WE ARE EXPANDING.

## The main comic: Nova Protocol

Eleven beats in three acts. Each beat is a scene of the comic. The game plays
the same main events and adds missions between them; see The game.

### Act one: the job

#### 1. An ordinary shift

Meridian at the junk site. Dead hulls, scrap, the moonlet, the banner. Cutter
One and its crew: the captain, Halloran, Okoro, four years together. The
captain walks past the plaque for Pell in the boat bay, as every shift. Section
nine is the standing joke: the renewal clause, and under it the Nova Protocol.
The captain still laughs at it. Halloran and Okoro do not. Demir on the radio,
Brandt on the deck. A Fleet challenge crackles on the guard channel in
fragments and nobody cares. Fleet is always looking for something.

The work: a re-qualification card after yard work, which is the GOTO tutorial.
Tag the hulls. Pull a cache. Nothing is wrong, and the reader who has seen the
prologue knows what ship this is.

#### 2. The strike

Out in the junk on a work channel, the cutter hears Control's guard channel
only in fragments: a voice, not Demir's, reading before it fires. What it
reads is the clause, in the desk's own dead language: "Meridian Control.
Section nine. Roster closed. No recovery." The cutter hears "...roster...". A
hull with no fleet code comes out of the moonlet's shadow, kills Meridian, and
leaves at once, because every second on the air is a second for Fleet to fix
it. Nobody at Saturn was told a warship was missing. Skiffs come in to sweep
the field. The cutter hides in the junk. 412 dead, Brandt among them. The
captain knew them four years. The reader does not yet know the voice.

### Act two, first half: forty hours

#### 3. Shelter in the junk

A dead tender hull in the junk, owner unknown. Okoro pressurises it, grafts
Cutter One's cockpit and drive onto its frame, and paints the name on before
the seal holds: Nova Protocol, the clause being applied to them. Over the next
shifts the crew feeds it piece by piece from the field: a cell bank, then two
debris PDCs off a wrecked tug. Salvage runs into the wreck for air, cells, and
the recorder module, which also holds Meridian's long archive. The recorder
beacons when it leaves its seat. The desk knows within the hour.

Distress to the desk. Calloway, warm: recovery in nine days, hold position,
costs against remaining term. Air is forty hours. Hold position is the Nova
Protocol said kindly, and it would have worked. Nobody on the crew is
surprised.

#### 4. The module

Okoro plugs the module in for the beacon log and gets the archive. Four years
ago: the Board's order, Calloway's acknowledgement in a voice the crew hears
every week, the no-rescue under section nine, Pell refusing and calling his
deck to the boats. This morning: the clause read before the shot, the same
man. Okoro: "That is... it can't be." Halloran said once, four years ago, to
nobody, that it was planned. She does not say it again. She does not have to.

#### 5. The run

Scavengers scavenge. The skiffs come back to strip the carcass and find a live
tender with EWI cockpit glass and a fresh cell bank: a company survivor with
salvage aboard, a witness and a prize. They chase it for real. The crew runs
for EWI's nearest safe harbour, Datum, the company's name for the rock where
Halloran used to live. Calloway's channel says Datum. Halloran says the
Shelter.

Nova Protocol's PDCs against skiffs through the ring junk. First fight, and
both sides mean it. Nobody is herding anyone.

### Midpoint

#### 6. Recovery

Parallax meets them on the Datum approach, early after all, and asks for the
module first and the crew second. Calloway, to the captain by name: rotation
approved, all three, effective on docking. Halloran does the math out loud:
the man on the archive has come for the only copy, and three witnesses are a
cost. The captain's call.

Refuse, and Calloway fires to disable, not to kill: the module is aboard. Nova
Protocol dives back into the ring junk, where a security ship's hull and
sensors do not go, and loses him. The skiffs, hanging back out of Parallax's
range, have watched the company fire on its own tender. Rotation's pilot puts
it together: a company boat the company wants dead is carrying something.
Rotation follows them into the junk, holds fire, and speaks on a work channel
with a Kestrel word nobody alive uses on the radio: the Roost, and a heading.
The skiffs learned who the crew were from Calloway's guns. The crew learn who
the skiffs are from the fact that they stop shooting. The skiff's name, in
company letters on scrap: Rotation.

The other choice is game only. Hand the module over and fly the GOTO to the
station. Parallax fires on the approach. Game over, the record buried. A short,
early bad ending that teaches the player what the company is. The comic does
not show it.

### Act two, second half: the other side

#### 7. The Roost

One of a dozen dead Kestrel stations, two days behind Rotation. A village:
Kestrel survivors, renewed workers, the volunteers who died. Severance at the
dock, the first time the crew sees the ship that killed Meridian. Pell alive,
on his own ground, his first page. Okoro's reunion.

Pell says what he did and why. He had a warship and the company was in front
of it, so he read them their own clause and fired. He is not sorry and he
knows it was wrong. "I had to." 412 includes Brandt. Okoro says nothing. A
thought bubble only the reader gets: so he was willing to kill me too. Pell's
clock: Fleet will take the ship back within weeks, so he hits Azimuth within
days. Sixty of the village crew Severance.

The Kites did not know what the tender carried. Okoro plays the module in the
village, and Pell hears his own refusal from four years ago with the sixty
listening. His answer is the theme's line: everyone knew, nobody did anything,
he did. Halloran's homecoming lands the same night. The village is the people
who did not sign, and she signed. Solberg says it. Her answer: yes, and she
has brought the thing that voids every signature.

Between this beat and the next, the game plays the refit at the Nest and the
Azimuth shadow. The comic gives them one page: Nova Protocol growing in a Kite
dock, and four hundred voices on the approach lane making the renewal joke.

#### 8. The argument

The module is worth more than the ship: proof, with Calloway's voice on it.
EWI's deal is a trap with a warm voice. Fleet would seize the module and lose
it. Pell is right about the company and wrong about the carrier.

Halloran does not argue with Pell. She talks over him to the sixty who crew
his ship, and she has heard Azimuth's people, so the line is not abstract:
Azimuth is four hundred of us and Calloway is one. Okoro: the man who took a
tender for strangers, where is he. Pell does not answer. Solberg answers for
the sixty: they will not crew for Azimuth. Pell cannot fly Severance alone.
Datum still gives him Calloway, so he takes Datum. The captain decides for the
crew: Calloway and the truth, on the crew's terms. No fork here.

Between this beat and the next, the game plays the Roost defense: Parallax's
pickets follow Rotation's track home and the village fights them off with the
crew. The comic gives it a page. The clock starts: Parallax knows the
neighbourhood now.

### Act three: Datum

#### 9. The plan

Datum holds the system relay. Play the module from there and every carrier
crew, every bought survivor and every Fleet picket hears it, and every
recorder at Saturn keeps it. Calloway will be there: it is headquarters, and
he is the one man who cannot let it play.

Meridian's roster is still open. Calloway has not filed section nine, because
a closed roster puts 412 on the desk's books before he has the module. So
Datum's automation still admits Meridian's code, and the recorder carries it.
Nova Protocol goes in first under a dead ship's name, kills the turret control
and the sensor masts, and docks at the relay with a handful of Kites aboard.
Then Severance comes in for the extraction and for Calloway. Pell knows the
broadcast hands Vigilant the fix. He goes anyway.

#### 10. Datum

The approach. Nova Protocol under Meridian's code through the turret envelope.
Control nodes and sensor masts die before the code stops matching, and Datum's
pickets launch when it does. Dock at the relay. Signal Severance.

Inside, unseen. Halloran, Okoro and the Kites go in with the module. The
captain stays at the helm: the ship is the way out. Halloran plays the module.
What she said once to nobody, said to everyone. Okoro takes the relay console
and Datum's channels.

The duel. The captain holds the dock against pickets while the module plays.
Parallax comes back at speed. Severance meets it under the turrets Nova
Protocol silenced. The archive is on every channel at once, Parallax's
included: the order, Calloway's voice, the no-rescue, Pell's refusal, this
morning's reading. The sixty hear their officer from four years ago. Azimuth's
crew hears why.

Calloway's turn. He cannot win the duel before the module finishes, so he
turns Parallax on the relay tower: his own station, his own people, a cost
against the record. The Shelter decision, a second time, with witnesses.

Pell's answer. Okoro, on Datum's channel to Severance, gives the boat-deck
order Pell taught him: all hands, tenders away. Pell's last transmission is to
his own deck: do as he says. Then he puts Severance between Parallax and the
tower and drives it in. A few stay with him by choice. The rest take the
boats. Both ships die in the light of the rock. Calloway and Pell in the same
minute, and not for the same reason.

Extraction. The crew back aboard with the module. Nova Protocol and the skiffs
cover the boats into the junk. Vigilant on the horizon, homing on the
broadcast, decelerating.

#### 11. Minute zero

Vigilant arrives when it is over and takes the victory: two hostile vessels
destroyed, the pirate leader dead, the relay secured, law and order at Saturn.
The official account has no civilians at the relay. EWI plays dumb: Calloway
acted alone, and he did fire on his own station, so the story holds. The Board
knew nothing. The company thanks Fleet, and the three who exposed a rogue
officer are heroes, with the rotation home to prove it. The press prints all
of it.

What the account cannot touch: every recorder at Saturn holds the broadcast,
Azimuth's, the pickets', Vigilant's own, append-only and impossible to purge
without grounding the ship. The rule that kept the crime keeps the proof.
Azimuth holds station and does not proceed to the cleared site. The banner
ship stops expanding. The Board is named and untouched.

The Kites have lost their ship and their leader and kept most of the sixty,
the authenticated original, and a cause. Solberg has the village. The crew
does not stay for any of it. Nova Protocol undocks while Vigilant is still
decelerating and is gone into the ring junk before the account is written. The
praise and the rotation home go out to a channel nobody answers. Last page:
the Roost, three people, one ship, the module. Off the roster.

## The game

### Two media, one spine

The comic tells the main events in eleven beats. The game plays the same
events and has more missions than the comic has pages. Every cutscene beat is
a comic page. The three Roost missions are game content that the comic covers
in a page each, or shares between two. Nothing that happens in a mission may
contradict a page, and every event on a page happens in the game.

### Progression

Cutter One is unarmed. Nova Protocol starts as Cutter One grafted onto a scrap
hull and grows: cells and two debris PDCs in the junk, then a real drive, a
third PDC and a sensor mast at the Roost. Every upgrade is scavenged and every
one shows on the hull. The Kites' parts make it a ship that can carry a
handful of them into Datum.

### Branches

- **Midpoint bad ending (game only).** Trust Calloway, hand over the module,
  fly the GOTO, get blasted on the approach. Short, early, and it teaches the
  player what the company is. The comic does not show it.
- **The argument (no fork).** Comic and game both go with Pell against
  Calloway. EWI's deal and Fleet's protection are arguments in the scene, not
  branches.
- **Datum is on the player.** The duel belongs to Pell and Calloway. Whether
  the broadcast finishes belongs to the player: the dock holds, or the
  scenario fails and restarts.
- **Calloway and Pell** die with their ships at Datum. Neither fate is open.
- **The ending (no fork).** The final scenario ends with the run to the Roost.
  The comic ends on the same page.

## How the company reads on the page

Never a speech. Only habits and paperwork:

- The plaque for Pell next to the WE ARE EXPANDING banner.
- Datum on the radio, the Shelter in the galley. The old name is a policy
  violation.
- Section nine, the renewal clause, as a crew joke.
- Recovery in nine days for a crew with forty hours, with costs against
  remaining term, delivered kindly.
- Rotation approved, all three, effective on docking, from the man on the
  archive.
- Meridian's roster still open days after the ship died, because closing it
  costs.
- Survivors "bought": contracts, settlements, silence.
- Fleet-surplus warheads sold to a company for cracking ice.
- "Rights are an Earth thing. Different gravity." A crew line, not a villain's.
- Heroes in the press, a line in the files, a plaque in the bay.

## Rules of the world

- Small-ship radio is local. A carrier or a station relay reaches the system.
  This is why Datum matters and why nobody hears the crew.
- Cutter One is unarmed. Nova Protocol has two debris PDCs, three after the
  Roost. Severance is a warship. Parallax is a security ship: less than a
  warship, more than a tender. EWI's small craft are pickets, the Kites' are
  skiffs.
- Parallax fires to disable while the module is aboard: Calloway wants the only
  copy intact. Ring junk is the crew's cover from anything bigger than a skiff.
- Severance strikes once and moves, and keeps every transmission short,
  because Fleet hunts by transmissions. Skiffs do the small work.
- Fleet lost a warship to pirates and kept it secret, for fear of public
  opinion. Nobody at Saturn knew a warship was loose, so Meridian had no
  warning. Fleet is slow, far, and embarrassed.
- Vigilant outguns Severance. Pell can run from Fleet, not fight it. This is
  his clock. The broadcast from Datum is Vigilant's fix.
- Kestrel's dead stations are scattered and unregistered. Nobody knows which
  one the Kites live in, until Parallax's pickets follow Rotation home.
- Forty hours of air for three people sets the clock of act two.
- The recorder is tamper-evident, append-only and required for certification.
  It beacons when unseated. It holds the last hours and the ship's long
  archive. Every recorder that receives the broadcast keeps it.
- A closed roster puts the loss on the desk's books. Meridian's stays open
  until Calloway has the module, so its code still opens Datum.
- Datum's automation admits any hull on an open EWI roster. Its turrets run
  from control nodes and sensor masts a small ship can kill.
- Sixty crew Severance. Pell cannot fly it alone.
- Nova Protocol carries a handful of Kites to Datum. The captain never leaves
  the helm.
- Consumables, PDC ammunition, docking and the fuel clock are scenario
  presentation until the engine has them.
