# Review 03: the book as ground truth

Read at `59b83b49`, as a stranger with only the book: the introduction and
the nine world pages. The people, places, paper and timeline pages are drafts.
The question is whether the spine holds on the rules the world pages set, and
what would make it hold better.

## Verdict

The spine holds on its mechanics. Every beat has a rule in the world pages
that makes it happen: the one antenna, the open-roster lag, the recorder that
cannot be purged and beacons when unseated, Fleet that finds ships by their
transmissions, a Board that never speaks and always finds a scapegoat.

It is weak in two joints and has one hole.

- The strike is under-motivated. Pell refused an order to save thirty-one
  strangers. The Kites kept "cargo, never crews" for four years. Then he kills
  412, many of them working level and some of them Kestrel hands under
  contract. The book says the sixty knew it would be first blood. It does not
  say what changed, or why this ship.
- Calloway's greed is undefined. The book gives him a motive to erase the
  record (his voice is on it) but nothing he wants, so "killed by his own
  greed" has nothing to kill him with.
- The recorder is the object of the season and the book never handles it:
  what exactly it is, why three people with forty hours of air pull it out of
  a wreck, who can read it, and why it survived four years under Calloway's
  desk.

## Where the pages disagree

Small faults. None breaks the spine.

- Power page: "Until seven weeks before Season 1, these were the only guns at
  Saturn." Resolute was stolen seven weeks before and arrived four weeks
  later. Say three weeks.
- The dead companies: eight bought, five bankrupt, one finished. The named
  entries do not sort. Oriel Water's owners "took EWI contracts", which is
  neither. Decide, and let the six unnamed carry the remainder.
- Kestrel: 1,400 members at peak, 610 aboard the Shelter, 31 out, about 400
  signed, about 100 vanished, "the rest went home, or died". The arithmetic
  works only if peak was years before Y-4. State Kestrel's headcount in Y-4.
- Earth page: the press made a hero of a boat-deck officer "because the
  company handed it one". The Kites page has that officer refusing an order.
  The company cannot say both. The plaque page must give the official story
  of Pell, word for word.
- The Roost: two hundred at peak, three hundred Kites now. Say how it grew.
- The Board's order is text. Exposure needs the text to be attributable. Say
  that relay text is signed by the sending desk and kept by the receiving
  recorder like anything else.

## What the spine needs from the pages not yet written

- **Pell.** That he never knew the charge was aimed. What changed seven weeks
  before Y0. What the sixty are to him and he to them. Whether he can fly
  Severance with a handful. His words: Kestrel's, learned from the
  thirty-one.
- **Calloway.** What he wants. What he knows the record holds. That the
  Board's "acknowledge by voice" made him the designated scapegoat four years
  ago, and that he knows it. How his kindness sounds when it is a threat.
- **The captain.** From Earth, believes in rotation, and leads the flight at
  the end. The page must make the second follow from the first.
- **Halloran.** Harrier's pilot is nameless on the Kestrel page and came back
  to a dead station. That is a hook with her name on it, if the owner wants
  it.
- **Okoro.** Knew Pell on Meridian's deck. Recognises the voice.
- **Solberg.** The village's voice. The person the crew actually ally with.
- **Vance.** Whether he knew. Whether he dies in the strike.
- **Marsh.** A competent method inside an incompetent mandate, and the
  account Fleet writes afterwards.
- **The paper.** Section nine. The plaque. The standard letter. And the Y-4
  record itself: the order and the acknowledgement, word for word, since the
  whole season turns on what it says.

## Proposals

1. **Give the strike a material cause beside revenge.** Clearing sector M is
   clearing the Kites' larder, and the sites Azimuth builds put company
   installations two days from the Roost. The sixty fight for the Roost. Pell
   fights for the Shelter. Meridian tags and Azimuth builds, so Azimuth is the
   real target and Meridian the first blow. That makes the turn away from
   Azimuth the natural climax instead of a debate.
2. **Add the trigger.** Shortly before Y0 the company kills Kites for the
   first time, cheaply, and files it: a Parallax picket fires on a skiff at a
   cache, or the desk lets an off-plate ice party die when a tug was an hour
   away. The company broke "never crews" first. Pell answers in the company's
   own arithmetic, and the sixty follow him because of the dead, not because
   of him.
3. **Handle the recorder.** It is Meridian's certified recorder. The crew
   pull it because a dead ship's recorder is the only thing that gives its 412
   names on Earth, and boat crews are drilled to recover it. It beacons on the
   certified channel when unseated, and the desk's antenna hears the beacon
   because Cutter One is on the open roster: the roster that keeps them alive
   is what tells Calloway they have it. Reading needs a certified seat. The
   Roost has one, from Harrier or Osprey, so the record is first heard at the
   Roost, in Pell's village.
4. **Define Calloway's greed.** He wants the record back, not destroyed. The
   Board's text and his voice are on it together, and it is the only insurance
   he has against the people who made him the scapegoat in advance. That is
   why he talks before he shoots, why the crew live long enough to reach the
   Roost, and how he dies: the Board reads his delay for what it is and
   applies the company's method to him. Datum's roster closes with him on it.
   "Killed by his own greed" becomes literal, and in the book's accounting
   voice.
5. **Make Pell's death a payment, not a redemption.** The book's motif is
   costs against remaining term. Pell mantles the crew's boat with Severance
   while the record goes out through Datum's relay. Most of the sixty have
   left in the boats. The reader is not asked to forgive him, only to watch
   him pay.
6. **Make Fleet's arrival Pell's doing.** Fleet finds ships by their
   transmissions. Pell reads section nine over the guard channel before he
   fires. That is the fix Vigilant has hunted seven weeks for, and the clock it
   starts runs the whole season. Fleet arrives late because it was always
   going to, and takes the credit for a kill Pell made.
7. **Give the ending its temptation.** The company offers the crew rotation
   home, by name, over the relay, in the warm voice. The crew's answer is the
   "Why nobody does anything" section read backwards. Nothing else needs to be
   said.

## Risks the book should watch

- Sympathy for Pell after 412 dead. The mitigations are in proposals 2, 3
  and 5: the company struck first, the crew never ally with him, and his death
  is a payment. Name at least one of Meridian's dead, such as the Oriel owner
  on the deck.
- The recorder as a magic key. Keep "it opens no doors". The open roster and
  Cutter One's transponder are what get the crew into Datum.
- The Board as an abstraction. It never gets a face, so its one act must be
  visible: the moment its method turns on Calloway.
