# Story review 01

## Scope decision

The campaign should combine these two directions:

- A full-length campaign, with more time among the Kites and more operations
  before Datum.
- The opening chapter of a larger campaign, where Datum ends this campaign but
  the broadcast and the crew going off-roster create room for a later campaign.

The story should become longer where that length explains character turns,
causality, factional conflict, and gameplay. It should still reach a complete
ending at Datum and the Roost rather than stop on an unresolved half-story.

## Overall assessment

The story has a strong backbone:

- The central crime is simple and understandable.
- EWI has a clear identity: evil expressed through contracts, accounting, and
  routine.
- The naming systems reinforce the factions without exposition.
- The Shelter/Datum transformation is an effective image.
- Pell is both justified and indefensible.
- The midpoint reversal is strong: the crew fought the people trying to save
  them.
- The ending preserves the theme. The truth becomes public, but the
  institutions absorb it and continue.

The largest weakness is not the premise. It is the space between the major
events. Several emotional and mechanical turns are stated as facts rather than
caused on the page. The current structure could support a short, focused
campaign. It does not yet feel large enough for a long campaign.

## The most important unresolved questions

### 1. What does Pell want?

We know his immediate plan:

1. Destroy Meridian.
2. Destroy Azimuth.
3. Keep doing as much damage as possible before Fleet recovers Severance.

We do not yet know what he believes this will accomplish.

Possible answers:

- **Revenge:** EWI killed the Shelter, so he kills EWI carriers.
  - Consequence: He is emotionally clear, but not much of a political leader.
- **Economic sabotage:** Without carriers, EWI cannot operate Saturn.
  - Consequence: His attacks have a strategic purpose, but the story must
    explain how two carriers affect the monopoly.
- **Revolt:** He expects carrier workers to defect once EWI appears vulnerable.
  - Consequence: Meridian becomes a terrible contradiction because he kills
    the workers he hopes to liberate.
- **Forced exposure:** He attacks in ways that force EWI and Fleet into public
  action.
  - Consequence: His transmission and the eventual relay plan become part of
    one strategy.

The recommendation is a combination of economic sabotage and failed revolt.
Pell believes destroying EWI's Saturn transport capacity will break the
monopoly and force workers off the company roster. He has convinced himself
that carrier crews are participants because they continue serving EWI.
Halloran punctures that belief when she calls Azimuth "four hundred of us."

That would give the argument a precise target. At present, Halloran persuades
him to change plans, but we do not know which belief she defeats.

### 2. Why does Pell accept the crew's plan?

This is currently the most important missing dramatic turn.

Pell has just killed 412 people. He is not sorry. He intends to kill another
400. Then the crew argues with him, and the story proceeds with their plan.

Something must change.

Options:

- **Pell is persuaded morally.**
  - Consequence: This gives him a redemption arc, but conflicts with "not
    sorry."
- **Pell is persuaded strategically.**
  - Consequence: He remains hard and unrepentant, but accepts that the archive
    can hurt EWI more than destroying Azimuth.
- **The Kites choose against Pell.**
  - Consequence: Pell must follow or lose his people. This gives Solberg and
    the Kites agency.
- **The crew makes cooperation conditional on control of the module.**
  - Consequence: Pell cannot execute the Datum plan without them, but this can
    feel merely transactional.

The recommendation is to combine the second and third options. Halloran and
Okoro challenge Pell, but Solberg and the Kites make the decision
consequential. Pell realizes that if he attacks Azimuth, his own people will
not follow him. He accepts the relay plan because it can sever EWI's control
more effectively than another massacre.

That would also give Solberg a real dramatic function.

### 3. What changes when the archive is broadcast?

The story begins with:

> Everyone knows, nobody does anything.

At the end, everyone hears proof. EWI denies institutional responsibility,
Fleet claims victory, and the Board remains untouched.

That is thematically coherent, but it risks making the entire campaign produce
no meaningful change. The document says the Kites gain "proof and a cause,"
but they already had Pell, the Shelter survivors, a village, and a stolen
warship. What becomes possible after the broadcast that was impossible before?

Some possibilities:

- Azimuth's crew refuses orders or mutinies.
- Other EWI crews begin defecting to the Roost.
- Settlements and silence agreements lose their power.
- Fleet personnel preserve copies despite official orders.
- EWI retains legal control but loses practical control of Saturn.
- The proof becomes the founding event of a wider resistance that drives later
  campaigns.

The proof does not need to defeat EWI. It should alter the balance of power.
Otherwise, Pell's accusation that knowledge changes nothing may be proven
correct.

The recommendation is that Azimuth does not become friendly immediately, but
it refuses Calloway's final order. That is the first visible crack. The
official account remains a lie, while workers begin acting on what they already
suspected.

## Character arcs

### The captain

The captain is called "the judge," but currently has almost no internal
position to judge from. The player makes two nominal decisions:

- Refuse Calloway, unless choosing the explicit bad ending.
- Join Pell under fixed terms.

The silent departure is presented as a third decision, but the player has no
meaningful alternative.

The captain needs a starting belief. It need not be a detailed backstory. For
example:

- Keep your head down and finish the contract.
- Institutions are bad, but alternatives are worse.
- Survival is the only responsibility a cutter captain can afford.
- Earth is still home, and rotation is still the goal.

Then each act can attack that belief. Without this, Halloran and Okoro have
arcs while the protagonist mainly pilots between them.

### Halloran

Her arc is the clearest:

- She recognized the truth.
- She stopped saying it.
- She lived under EWI's replacement of her home.
- At the Roost, she finally says the necessary thing to someone who can act.

The missing piece is a consequential action. Her speech should change Pell's
plan, divide the Kites, or cause Azimuth's crew to respond. Otherwise, her arc
resolves internally but does not affect the plot.

There is also a powerful unresolved question: how does she feel about the
Shelter survivors who joined Pell while she signed with EWI? She may experience
the Roost as a homecoming, an accusation, or both.

### Okoro

Okoro has the most emotionally difficult position and currently receives the
least resolution.

He learns:

- His employer murdered Kestrel workers.
- Pell, whom he admired, murdered Meridian's crew.
- Pell knowingly accepted that Okoro might have been aboard.
- Brandt helped Pell rescue strangers and later died by Pell's hand.

His question to Pell is strong:

> The man who took a tender for strangers, where is he?

But we do not yet get Pell's answer, Okoro's conclusion, or an action that
expresses Okoro's choice. He builds the ship, but that happens before his
conflict with Pell.

He should do something at Datum that resolves this tension. For example, he
could:

- Save Severance crew despite hating Pell.
- Refuse to arm Nova Protocol for an attack on Azimuth.
- Preserve or transmit Brandt's part of the archive.
- Choose the relay over an opportunity to avenge Meridian.
- Retrieve the module while Pell knowingly covers the escape.

### Pell

Pell has a compelling history but currently a flat present-tense arc:

- He was once heroic.
- He has become willing to kill workers.
- The crew tells him he is wrong.
- He accepts another plan.
- He dies.

The missing scene is the point at which the old Pell appears again, if only
once. His death should follow from a choice that distinguishes him from
Calloway. Otherwise, their simultaneous deaths risk implying simple
equivalence.

He does not need forgiveness or regret. He could remain unapologetic about
Meridian while still choosing to protect Azimuth or Nova Protocol. That
contradiction would be stronger than a clean redemption.

### Calloway

Calloway works well as the voice of institutional violence. However, he is
almost entirely a function of the plot. A few questions could sharpen him:

- Does he genuinely believe the Shelter decision was necessary?
- Does he think he saved EWI's Saturn operation and therefore thousands of
  jobs?
- Does he regard Pell as proof that rescue was a mistake?
- Why does he personally take Parallax instead of delegating recovery?
- Is his priority the archive, the crew, Pell, or his own survival?

His lie should be internally consistent. The most fitting version is that he
does not think of himself as a murderer. He thinks he made necessary
allocations under emergency policy.

### Solberg

Solberg is described as the person who will inherit the Kites, but has no
stated part in the argument or climax. This makes the ending feel
administratively true rather than dramatically earned.

Solberg could represent the Kites who owe Pell their lives but reject what he
has become. That would place the faction's future inside the central argument.

## Plot and world-logic gaps

### The recorder module

This is the largest technical plot device and needs firmer rules.

Questions:

- Why does Meridian retain a four-year-old Board order implicating EWI?
- Why was it not purged?
- Why is it stored in the same removable module as recent flight records?
- Why can Okoro access it by plugging it into a salvaged tender?
- Why is it the only copy?
- How can listeners authenticate it?
- After it is broadcast, can recipients record and duplicate it?

A useful answer might be that the recorder is tamper-evident, append-only, and
legally required for carrier operations. EWI deleted ordinary records but
could not alter this one without invalidating Meridian's certification.
Calloway believed it would remain inaccessible inside an operating carrier.

Without an answer, the archive can feel like a convenient box containing
exactly what the plot needs.

### Calloway's recovery timeline

Calloway says recovery will take nine days even though the crew has forty hours
of air. Then Parallax reaches them within that survival window.

This can work as a trap, but the story should establish:

- Whether Calloway already knows the module survived.
- How he locates Nova Protocol.
- Why he initially tells them to hold position.
- Why he approaches personally.
- Why the crew does not realize that an early arrival is suspicious before
  Halloran explains it.

### Datum's defenses

The climax needs a tactical pass.

Datum is EWI headquarters with the system relay and Saturn's only fixed guns.
Yet:

- Severance and Parallax fight under its turrets.
- It is unclear who controls the turrets.
- Nova Protocol docks at the relay.
- The crew encounters no described station security.
- The archive plays without anyone cutting power or disabling the transmitter.
- Vigilant later "secures" the relay.

The station itself should probably be an active obstacle. Otherwise, the most
important installation in the system behaves like scenery.

### Pell's transmission before the strike

The document says Pell transmits before firing so the recorder holds his
voice. Why?

If he wants secrecy, this is a mistake. If he wants the attack attributed to
him, what does he say and what purpose does attribution serve? If the
transmission is a warning, does Meridian have time to respond? If it is a
declaration, it should reveal something about his ideology.

### Azimuth's arrival

Azimuth arrives "with 400 workers and the replacement stations." Replacement
for what? It was already inbound before Meridian died, so it cannot be
replacing Meridian unless the wording means replacement installations for
cleared claims.

Its purpose matters because Pell's desire to attack it depends on what it
represents:

- Another carrier full of ordinary workers.
- The force that will occupy newly cleared sites.
- A mobile company town carrying replacement labor.
- The means by which EWI erases another small miner.

### Fleet's arrival

Vigilant arriving in the exact minute after the climax risks feeling convenient
unless its pursuit is visible earlier.

Possible foreshadowing:

- Fragmented Fleet challenge traffic during the junk missions.
- Severance must leave transmissions brief because Vigilant triangulates them.
- Pell knows Datum will expose Severance's position.
- The relay broadcast gives Vigilant its final fix.

That makes Pell's death a cost he understands rather than a scheduled ending.

### The fate of Severance's sixty crew

The text says sixty Kites crew Severance. It appears that Severance is
destroyed with all sixty aboard, but the ending only says the Kites lost their
ship and leader.

If sixty people die, that is a major event. It should shape the ending and
Solberg's inheritance. If they evacuate, the story needs to explain how.

### The module at the end

The crew docks, plays the module, escapes, and ends at the Roost with the
module. This is plausible but not stated. The climax should make retrieval
explicit, especially if the relay is under attack.

Also, once broadcast, the physical module is no longer the only copy of the
information. It may remain the only authenticated original. That distinction
should be established.

## Campaign and gameplay concerns

The current outline contains roughly five gameplay sequences:

1. Tutorial work.
2. Salvage and ship construction.
3. Skiff pursuit.
4. Escape from Parallax.
5. Relay insertion.

That can support a compact campaign, but likely not a long one. Most of the
factional and character material appears in cutscenes, especially at the Roost.

The final gameplay also gives the decisive combat to Pell and Calloway. The
player slips through debris and activates the relay while the two important
ships destroy each other. That is thematically suitable, but it may feel
passive in a space shooter.

The player does not need to defeat either ship, but player actions could decide
whether the broadcast succeeds:

- Disable Datum's turret control.
- Hold a transmission corridor.
- Protect relay antennas.
- Rescue Severance escape craft.
- Carry authenticated segments between damaged relay nodes.
- Choose between preserving the archive and helping Pell.

The midpoint choice is also not a true moral fork. By that point, the player
knows Calloway ordered the old crime, arrived early for the only copy, and asks
for the module before the crew. Handing it over is an explicit failure button.
That is acceptable as a bad ending, but it should not be presented as the
captain's major ambiguous decision.

## Structural diagnosis

The story currently has three strong reveals:

1. EWI deliberately destroyed the Shelter.
2. Pell survived and destroyed Meridian.
3. The institutions will absorb the truth and protect themselves.

The second reveal is delayed effectively. The first is only a reveal for the
crew because the prologue tells the reader. The third is an ending statement
rather than something the protagonists can contest.

The section that most needs expansion is the Roost. It currently carries too
much work in two cutscene beats:

- Pell's reveal.
- Halloran's homecoming.
- Okoro's betrayal.
- The Kites' explanation.
- The moral judgment of Meridian.
- The Azimuth deadline.
- The debate over the archive.
- The formation of the final plan.
- The crew's choice to become fugitives.

That likely needs either several scenes or playable time at the Roost.

## Campaign-scale options considered

### Option A: A compact campaign

Approximately five to seven substantial missions over several in-world days.

Consequence: The current backbone fits. The existing scenes would need to be
deeper, with clear causality and no subplots that demand more time.

### Option B: A full-length campaign

Approximately ten to fifteen missions, with more time among the Kites and more
operations before Datum.

Consequence: The story needs a larger middle. The player should meet Kites,
experience divided loyalties, prepare the relay operation, and see Azimuth's
approach affect the system.

### Option C: The first chapter of a larger campaign

Datum is the end of an opening arc. The wider campaign follows the consequences
of the broadcast and the crew becoming off-roster.

Consequence: The current ending becomes a strong launch point, but it should
not imply that the conflict with EWI is resolved.

### Decision

Combine B and C. Build a full-length first campaign whose Datum ending resolves
its immediate conflict and transforms the situation at Saturn. Leave EWI, the
Board, Fleet's institutional response, and the growth of an off-roster
resistance available for a later campaign.
