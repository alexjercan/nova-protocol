# Add enemy, neutral, and allied ship encounters to the open world

- STATUS: OPEN
- PRIORITY: 60
- TAGS: v0.15.0,world,encounters,design

## User facts (2026-10-02 grilling)

- The owner wants enemy, friendly, and neutral ships in the open world and a clear implementation plan for the next feature. This is a design session, not approval to implement.
- Record each owner decision and unresolved unknown in this file as the session progresses. Do not treat proposed defaults or code findings as owner decisions.
- Correction: ship spawning/placement stays as it is in the PR #96/#99/#101/#103 stack. The suggestion to add separately placed live encounter groups or tune their spawn density was wrong. Do not add new ships, candidate slots, or cluster frequency in this task unless the owner changes this decision.
- Patrol behavior must include stationary dwell periods. The owner chose a local route with seeded 30-60 second stops at every safe waypoint.

## Goal

Let existing intact generated ships in the streamed world become enemy, neutral, and allied AI encounters at their already-planned slots, including local combat zones the player can fly into. For this first release, draw each civilization's stable player-facing combat allegiance (Player, Enemy, or Neutral) independently of living/extinct status; only intact living ships use it as their starting combat side. Start with a playable example, then judge whether this makes the world better. Dynamic standing, trade-driven reputation, relations between civilizations, and station jobs belong to a separate backlog task. Do not start implementation yet.

## Decisions from 2026-10-02 grilling

- Activate existing intact generated ships from living civilizations as AI-controlled Enemy, Player-aligned, or Neutral ships at their current slots. Keep existing derelicts inert and Neutral. Ship counts, positions, and clearance are unchanged; add no new candidate slots or density draw. A cluster or area may lack a side, with combat zones only where existing local opposing sides meet. The separate controlled playable example proves all three sides together. This replaces the earlier proposal for separate live encounter groups.
- Each civilization draws its starting side independently from a dedicated stable CivilizationId seed aspect, with equal 1/3 chance for Enemy, Player-aligned, or Neutral. Do not balance assignments world-wide. Uneven regional side counts and missing sides are valid. Keep this draw independent of role, advancement, appearance, and living/extinct status. This changes sides of existing intact ships, not ship counts.
- Each intact generated AI ship follows a local patrol and station-keeps for a seeded 30-60 seconds at every safe waypoint before moving to the next. Combat interrupts a pause; after disengagement it returns to its local routine. No inter-cluster travel or change to its spawn slot. Existing Patrol has no timed dwell, so waypoint-state ownership, seeded timing, and safe route geometry require a code-backed gate.
- Industrial AI ships may retain their optional working Mining sections, but do not select asteroids, fire Mining beams, or collect ore autonomously in this encounter task. Their calm behavior is patrol/dwell. A damaged unarmed Neutral (Civilian or Industrial) remains noncombatant: no pursuit, fire, or threat marker. Damage still counts and it becomes boardable if later neutralized; do not add flee AI.
- Civilian and Industrial generated hulls have no combat weapons in #103; Scavenger and Armored do. An unarmed Enemy keeps its Enemy allegiance and contact marker but patrols without chase or attack. Like any active Enemy, it refuses physical docking and trade. Armed Player-aligned ships still acquire and attack it based on allegiance; do not add a special unarmed-target exemption. Do not add guns, reroll its role or side, ram, or implement flee AI. Unarmed ships of ANY allegiance, generated or authored, become neutralized and boardable when they lose their LAST working thruster, provided they previously had at least one; losing just one of several does not neutralize. Keep the current armed-ship weapon/computer rule. Once neutralized, any generated or authored ship may be docked with and looted for remaining cargo/credits, regardless of starting side (subject to a surviving port and existing inventory rules). This supersedes the earlier proposed computer-loss rule for unarmed ships; avoid false neutralization during spawn or for thrusterless authored hulls. No general cargo-drop-on-destruction rule is approved.
- Newly materialized armed Enemy ships get a short 5-10 second engagement grace via the existing AI delay. An incoming hit ends grace immediately. Choose and verify the exact delay in a live flight test; do not delay neutral/friendly interaction or change spawn placement.
- Use existing allegiance-colored HUD/map contacts and visible combat to signal nearby fights. A Neutral ship that is retaliating against the player shows as a threat to that player only while it has the player as its active target; underlying civilization side stays Neutral. A Neutral fighting another AI stays Neutral-colored to the player. Do not add encounter-zone markers or entry/outcome messages in the first pass. Inspect rendered flight; reconsider only if real fights are easy to miss.
- Allow docking/trade with CALM Player-aligned and CALM Neutral intact ships. Refuse new docking while a Player-aligned ally is actively engaged, while an armed Neutral is retaliating against any attacker, or while an Enemy is active. A ship already docked stays docked under third-party fire per the separate hit policy. Once ANY intact ship is neutralized, allow docking as boarding and Take its remaining stock/credits, not Buy/Sell, regardless of its previous side. Existing derelict looting remains. Pause the docked partner's patrol and offensive AI and hold the pair steady until undock; incoming damage still applies. On third-party damage, a docked Neutral records the latest shooter but does not move/fire or auto-undock; trade continues and retaliation waits for undock if the attacker remains in territory. If the player damages ANY docked Neutral, armed or unarmed, force undock and end trade. An armed Neutral then retaliates against the player; an unarmed one stays noncombatant and does not display a threat marker. Gate the docking teardown, atomic transfer boundary, and hit ordering so a partial trade cannot occur. The current generated-ship Buy/Sell path does not check allegiance; gate docking authorization, neutralized-vs-active policy, docked helm/AI ownership, and inventory action choice. Do not reroll stock or credits.
- An ARMED struck Neutral live ship retaliates only against the identified ship that actually damaged it, whether Player or AI, while that target is alive and inside its territory. The most recent valid damaging shooter replaces the current target; no threat list or queued attackers. It drops ship-local hostility on target loss or leash break and returns to its Neutral patrol; another hit may restart retaliation. No civilization-wide change or timed cooldown. The exact hit signal, attacker identity, and relation/AI override need a code-backed gate.

## Decisions and open gates

- For the first live encounter, represent allies as `Allegiance::Player` (the player's existing combat side); use `Allegiance::Enemy` and `Allegiance::Neutral` for the other two starting sides. Seed a stable player-facing allegiance for each civilization, independent of its role, advancement, appearance, and living/extinct draw. Carry that value into each existing intact generated ship's AI construction at its planned slot; keep civilization identity separate from combat side. Existing derelicts remain inert and Neutral. No new `Allied` variant or dynamic reputation is implied.
- The first playable encounter example uses the generated-hull prototype from the regional ship PR stack. Do not place authored encounter hulls in the open world as a temporary exception; no new open-world ship placement or count is in scope.
- An actual hit from any ship makes only that struck ARMED Neutral live ship retaliate against the identified shooter. Its civilization's seeded starting allegiance does not change, other ships of that civilization do not change sides, and no lasting reputation is granted. On target loss or leash break it returns to Neutral patrol. The target-specific AI/relation interface is a code-backed gate; `Allegiance::Neutral` alone cannot express retaliation under today's `relation()` resolver.
- Shooting a Player-aligned allied encounter ship does not change its side or make it retaliate; it remains allied. Damage may still occur under the existing damage rules. There is no group alert or reputation change.
- First playable example: one controlled local scene using generated hulls, with an armed Enemy engaging, a Neutral staying out until actually damaged, and an armed Player-aligned ally engaging the Enemy. Prove it without adding any ship to streamed-world placement.
- Keep existing deterministic generated ship slots, counts, positions, and clearance rules. Intact ships from living civilizations become AI at those slots; derelicts remain inert. Side, controller and local patrol must not change placement or depend on visit order or player position. No encounter-density draw or new candidate ID is needed.
- First-version lifetime: after sector retirement, defeated live ships regenerate at their same seeded slots on revisit, with original stock and credits. Repeated fighting/looting is a known temporary limit, not persistent defeat or claim-once loot. Document it for players. Durable lifetime is owned by `20260925-190156`.
- Implementation still needs a code-backed gate for independent civilization side draw and propagation into existing intact ships; Neutral's ship-local target override across sensing, firing and HUD; route safety and seeded waypoint dwell; active/neutralized docking and trade authorization; docked hit/undock ordering; last-thruster neutralization for unarmed ships; and sector-retirement reset. Keep current generated positions, counts, derelict Neutral state, and the existing contact UI. Use a controlled example to prove combat before generalizing to streaming. No dynamic reputation or station job board.
