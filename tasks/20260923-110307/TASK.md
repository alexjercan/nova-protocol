# Add regional ship variety to the open world

- STATUS: OPEN
- PRIORITY: 55
- TAGS: v0.15.0,world,ships,content

## Goal and boundary

Vary ship designs and appearances across the open world. Human activity already weights a damaged tender versus wreck plating; explore industrial, scavenger, and other readable role silhouettes using current fields or an additional field only if the evidence needs it. Ship variety should include more than the same two hulks. World ships must be generated from eligible sections; mods can add sections and GLB models, but not complete authored world-hull designs. The player ship remains the authored exception for now. Do not assume that a new skin alone makes a new ship role.

`20260925-190131` owns live enemy, neutral, and allied encounter spawns and their AI. This task owns what ship designs and looks are eligible in which places; it does not itself authorize a full faction system or new combat behavior.

## First-slice boundary

- Static ship selection has finite 3D influence reach, not an infinite nonzero tail. Civilization advancement rises with 3D distance from the world origin; static ships usually appear farther outward than their civilization centroid, but inward appearances remain possible within reach. Live moving visitors belong to `20260925-190131`. Discovery must not alter generation: the same world seed and content produce the same ships regardless of visited sectors.
- The first visible slice should include randomly generated full ships for living civilizations, driven by their advancement. They are powered, intact, unpiloted world ships placed at seeded initial poses: no movement orders, hostility, retaliation, or new AI in this task. Do not introduce a special kinematic/anchor exception here; `20260925-182711` owns making unpiloted ships and other mobile bodies obey the future static-well gravity/orbit policy. Materialize all intact generated ships with Neutral combat allegiance regardless of civilization or fighting role; installed weapons stay idle without AI. Extinct wrecks have no combat controller. Each seeded civilization has a fixed living or extinct status drawn independently of advancement and distance. Start the three-seed diagnostic at 70% living / 30% extinct, then tune the observed regional and ship mix before runtime integration. Select the civilization by influence first, then its status determines intact ship versus generated derelict. Some regions may contain only one form. Extinct civilizations leave generated derelicts. The ship-generation prototype starts world-ship holds empty and does not implement loot interaction. Keep normal installed weapon magazines. A later approved item-loop glue rule may seed generated wreck contents when the loot stack is ready; decide role cargo, claim-once lifetime, and persistence there. World ships must be assembled by a seed-driven algorithm from section facts. WFC is a comparison point, not a mandatory solver; use another algorithm if it gives better valid shapes.

## Decisions

- This task owns regional faction identity only for ship-design and appearance selection. Identity must not imply hostility or alliance. Live relations, neutral-until-attacked behavior, and reputation remain separate gates with `20260925-190131` and its follow-ups.
- Use overlapping, seeded areas of faction influence so overlap can mix ship identities. A separate unclaimed design pool is not wanted. The prototype maximum selection reach is 320 km in 3D space; exact curve parameters remain open. There is no discovery-gated generation.
- The world seed creates distinct faction identities as centroids with readable display names composed deterministically from a small authored syllable inventory, not a finite authored faction enum or catalog. Show sample names and collisions in the three-seed diagnostic. Represent stable machine identity as the structured world seed plus signed 3D centroid-lattice node; generated hulls retain it for provenance, without combat allegiance or reputation. Display names may repeat and must not serve as IDs. Derive a short UI token from the full identity only where a collision needs disambiguation; never replace the full identity with a truncated hash. Show the generated civilization name when the player inspects or targets one of its ships, not as an always-on distant label. For a wreck, also label it Derelict and show its former role; this does not imply lootability or a faction relation. Treat each identity as a civilization, not a single exclusive ship-role kind. The first slice has exactly four closed generator roles (civilian, industrial, scavenger, armored); mods contribute eligible parts/models but cannot author new placement/validation role policies yet. A civilization's advancement and role mix determine which of industrial, civilian, armored, and scavenger ships it actually fields; high-advancement civilizations may have no scavenger fighters. Identity does not imply alliance even where civilizations share ship roles.
- Advancement is one continuous value fixed for each seeded civilization, not a location field or separate propulsion/weapons/construction axes. It follows a bounded gradient by full 3D distance from the world origin, with seeded faction variation, and eventually saturates within available content; prototype saturation at about 1,000 km (3D radius), subject to diagnostic and travel review. It imposes a hard build ceiling on parts and ship size. Normalize at least one viable connected hull, flight computer and main-drive set to the minimum advancement floor so even a near-origin living civilization can build a simple flyable civilian/industrial craft. Missing that minimum set fails content validation; do not silently turn the civilization into a no-ship region. Derive section eligibility from intrinsic kind and per-kind normalized capability, factoring relevant performance, footprint, and resource costs where applicable, calibrated against the pinned validated base+mod catalog with a viable basic floor. Maximum advancement can use the strongest valid mod parts; a changed catalog requires a new session and can change relative scores. Do not require mod-authored generator tiers. Invalid or non-finite capability stats fail content lint; valid but out-of-ceiling parts remain ineligible. For the advancement-map prototype, start with a clamped linear 0-1 ramp across roughly 0-1,000 km plus seeded variation of +/-0.1 (also clamped to 0-1); render a smoothstep comparison before choosing the shipped curve. Exact variation bounds, per-kind formulas, and normalization remain a code-backed gate. Scavengers are rough, low-tier fighting ships that may also carry utility equipment; they are not dedicated salvage vessels. Lower-advancement or pirate-like civilizations may field more scavengers than armored ships. Scavenger frequency declines with advancement but has no global hard cutoff: an advanced pirate-like civilization may still field one, while another may have zero through its seeded preference. More advanced factions can build larger ships and thrusters, more capable weapons, better-finished hulls, and clearer ship roles. A seeded civilization-level fleet-role preference (including pirate-like scavenger-heavy mixes), its advancement, and existing local material/volatiles/human-activity fields set the role mix in the first slice; start the diagnostic with moderate typical preference spread but permit specialized extremes and zero-weight roles, then show role frequencies before fixing the distribution; do not add an independent industry/conflict noise field without rendered evidence. In the diagnostic, material-rich places favor industrial hulls, high human activity favors civilian traffic and some armored escorts, and volatile-rich places favor industrial utility work. Scavenger frequency comes mainly from seeded civilization preference and lower advancement, not an invented danger field. Show actual role counts before fixing multipliers. Local conditions change role frequency, not capability. Captured/salvaged technology exceptions require a separate rule; exact eligibility and weights remain open.
- Generate exactly one faction centroid per node of a separate coarse, seeded jittered lattice, provisionally spaced about 240 km with +/-40 km jitter per axis for the first diagnostic. The conservative nearest-node bound is sqrt(3) * (120 + 40) km, about 277 km, below the 320 km reach; prove coverage and tune irregularity from maps. Derive stable identity from world seed and lattice node; sample neighboring nodes so influence is independent of sector visit order. Bound jitter so every location has a centroid within the 320 km prototype maximum. Inspect a diagnostic influence map before fixing density or jitter.
- Prototype a roughly 160 km influence core and a 320 km maximum selection reach, with influence declining smoothly through the core and tapering continuously to zero only at the reach edge; every centroid inside the reach has a positive weight and no hard territorial border. Inspect maps and rendered travel before treating distances or weight curve as balanced. Place ship candidates near rocks/worlds in multiple anchored cluster types, driven by activity and clearance. Draw a primary civilization per cluster; start with a 10% per-additional-ship chance to draw a different reachable civilization, weighted by its local influence, and otherwise use the primary. If no second civilization is within reach, use the primary. Tune the observed mixed-cluster rate from three-seed maps. This does not imply alliances or coordinated AI. Do not spawn solitary ships from empty-cell background scatter in the first slice. Apply a smooth 3D outward weight to pure selection of seeded intact ships and extinct-civilization derelicts here: locations farther from the world origin than their civilization's centroid are favored; inward locations within reach remain possible. Start the diagnostic with about 4:1 outward-to-inward preference at matched influence distance, then count actual placements across seeds before locking the curve. Do not impose a fixed per-cluster cap on intact ships in the first prototype. Derive a finite, deterministic candidate-attempt budget from the place's activity/geometry, place candidates only when clearance permits, and count named skips. Never change population based on machine speed or frame rate; exact density still needs tuning. Live moving visitors remain under `20260925-190131`.
- Do not add faction-specific presentation in the first slice: no unique color, style, or shape profile solely to distinguish civilizations with the same advancement and role. Faction color on plating/greebles is a later design question.
- Every intact generated ship must be structurally connected, have a working flight computer and main drive, and clear installed weapon/engine lanes, even while unpiloted. Civilian and industrial-role ships are always unarmed and must not display weapon-looking mounts, even empty ones; exclude recognized weapon/mount section categories and inspect rendered mod art for ambiguous geometry. Civilian ships are intended NPC craft. Industrial ships are intended miners and each intact industrial hull requires a usable cargo intake, including at minimum advancement; no missing mining/NPC behavior is implied. An intake is optional on civilian, scavenger, and armored ships. All hulls have inventory space; ships without an intake can still transfer at a dock but cannot pick up or jettison canisters in free space. Scavenger and armored roles are fighting ships. Intact scavenger and armored ships each require at least one usable weapon within their civilization's advancement ceiling. Exclude a fighter role before the role draw when the civilization's advancement has no eligible weapon; this is valid low-tech behavior, not a silent ship fallback. A selected fighter role with no eligible weapon, or no buildable role at all, fails loudly rather than producing an unarmed fighting hull. It should be usable by future encounter AI without replacing its design. A derelict remains a ship design, not a separate object kind or spawn-time damage flag. Extinct civilizations select a former role from their same role mix and advancement ceiling, then generate a separate connected ruined design from that role's structural grammar; an armored wreck stays visibly armored, while a civilian wreck stays unarmed. It needs no working drive/controller and cannot contain disconnected fragments inside one ship design. Seed full 3D orientations for derelicts; keep intact ships level in their local place frame with seeded yaw. Use the outward radial vector from the cluster's planetoid center as local up when anchored to a planetoid, otherwise world +Y; yaw intact ships around that up axis. Validate the radial frame and rotated hull clearance. Operational hardware may remain visibly installed in a derelict, but every active system (drive, weapon, radar, flight computer, intake and other utilities) starts disabled. Physical sections remain collidable and damageable; no thrust, firing or active utility behavior occurs. Activation/repair needs a separate rule. Generate visible holes by omitting noncritical outer sections while retaining one connected structural body. Expose internal framing and use available valid weathered/damaged visual parts so a wreck cannot read as an intentional sparse new ship; report missing usable assets in the rendered prototype. Remaining sections start at normal health; do not invent spawn-time section damage or zero-Health wreck pieces. Any future repair/salvage activation belongs to a separate decision.
- World ship generation considers all valid base/mod section prototypes and GLB models, rather than selecting complete authored ship designs. The player ship is the only authored exception for now. The algorithm owns placement rules and reads a section's existing type, footprint/shape, and link points; sections and mod authors must not declare generator-specific placement metadata. A valid part may be excluded by current role, advancement, geometry, or links without invalidating the mod; absence of a required part category must fail loudly. Choose generator-owned functional/shape variants before drawing an eligible prototype. Give base content and each eligible mod pack equal source weight, independent of each pack's number of near-identical entries; then choose eligible prototypes within that source. Many separate mod packs may collectively make base parts rare, and that is accepted. Choose a preferred source/palette per hull from its role and stable hull seed, not per civilization. Prefer source coherence on civilian, industrial, and armored designs; allow scavengers to mix eligible sources more freely. The generator owns this role-dependent selection, with cross-source functional parts allowed when needed; mods do not author style-placement rules. Exact within-source weights remain to tune. A single centered 5x5x3 thruster at the stern can preserve a symmetric overall ship when built on a centered backbone; the algorithm may also choose a valid paired-drive layout by seed. Generation failure must fail loudly, not substitute an authored hull.
- Derive ship placement clearance from each generated hull's bounds rather than the current fixed 400 m `SECTOR_SHIP_CLEARANCE` (`crates/nova_world/src/generation.rs:224`). Change the owning manifest/placement contract; a hull that does not fit seeded terrain/ship clearance or its owning 32 km cell is skipped for a named geometry reason, never clipped, shrunk, or split across sector ownership. Player position must never change the seeded manifest: if the player overlaps a valid ship's spawn bounds when its sector activates, delay only its materialization until clear and diagnose the delay, without moving or dropping that ship. Impose a 400 m prototype maximum hull clearance radius (the existing fixed reservation's radius), while computing each actual hull's smaller clearance from its bounds. Exceeding this maximum fails validation rather than silently shrinking the ship. This keeps the cluster halo bounded; advance the maximum allowed hull clearance radius continuously from a validated minimum flyable envelope to the 400 m ceiling; actual role/seed layouts may be smaller and remain grid-discrete. Tune the world maximum only with a separate reviewed geometry/halo change.
- Unknown section/style IDs and empty eligible part sets must fail loudly. For a valid catalog but an unsatisfied seeded layout, use a fixed bounded set of deterministic attempts, then fail that sector build with the seed, civilization, role, and failed constraint. Only spatial clearance conflicts may skip a candidate with a named diagnostic; never hide an unbuildable selected role by reducing population. Never retry without a bound or fall back to an arbitrary hull.
- Derive a generated ship's stable ID and its random stream from world seed, cluster node, and planned candidate slot before clearance/solver checks. A skipped candidate leaves an ID gap but cannot renumber later ships. Derive each design-local section ID from its planned generator slot (station/side/fitting position), not final list order; optional omissions leave gaps and duplicate IDs fail validation. Selection and ship construction must be deterministic for a world seed, coordinate, and content identity. The prototype accepts current pristine-sector regeneration: a destroyed generated ship reappears after sector retirement/revisit. Document this as temporary; do not claim persistent destruction or claim-once loot before `20260925-190156` and the item-loop integration decide their lifetime. Carry each unique generated `ShipDesign` inline with the stable ship identity in a validated sector description; its sections refer to validated prototype IDs. Do not register one-off hulls as catalog designs. Pin a validated content snapshot for the live session and refuse continuation against a changed catalog rather than silently redesigning a sector. Canonicalize source and prototype IDs before hashing or drawing so mod load order cannot alter ships for the same effective catalog; conflicting IDs fail at load, not by order-based precedence. Durable save migration belongs to `20260925-190156`.
