# Round 7: story craft research for the Nova Protocol campaign

Ran 2026-09-04. Owner request. Craft research only: techniques and
citations, never copied text. Feeds task 20260905-094102 (the story of Nova
Protocol). The outline it was first written for did not survive; its
recommendations for that outline are not kept.

## Research questions

- How does routine work create attachment before a disaster?
- How does an ordinary crew become involved in a political conflict without
  becoming chosen heroes?
- How can recovered evidence reveal a conspiracy through gameplay or scene
  structure, one item at a time, each item changing belief or action?
- How can factions remain understandable without exposition dumps?
- How can a short campaign end at worker scale while leaving a larger setting
  alive?
- How is a "silent channel" or an unanswered call used as a dramatic device?
- How do stories handle a final choice about WHO receives the truth
  (broadcast vs institution)?

## Sources

### Leviathan Wakes

1. **Reference** - James S. A. Corey (Daniel Abraham and Ty Franck), Orbit,
   2011. Reviewed: the opening ice-hauler chapters, the first and second
   broadcasts, and the evidence chain through the middle of the book.
   - https://www.redfuturesmag.com/publications/the-expanse-expanded/work-horror-and-the-expanse
   - https://snarkknight.com/2023/08/26/the-expanse-leviathan-wakes-1st-book-spoilers-only/
   - https://expanse.fandom.com/wiki/Leviathan_Wakes
2. **Craft problem studied** - Moving a working crew into a conflict larger
   than them, with incomplete evidence and competing public readings.
3. **Technique** - The book opens on a long, dull, low-status haul. The crew
   is skilled but nobody important. Their first pages are about a worn ship,
   an injured coworker, and what the company owes him. A distress beacon is an
   obligation the crew resents, not a call to adventure. The loss of the big
   ship happens while the small party is away, so they watch it from a
   distance and can do nothing. The lead then transmits raw findings with one
   implied conclusion. Every polity reads the same facts through its own
   interest, and violence follows. Later evidence arrives as a chain: each
   recovered hull or dataset names the next place to go and revises the last
   belief. A second transmission tries to correct the first, which shows the
   cost of speaking early. The authors have said the working-crew framing
   was deliberate and drew on the commercial-crew tradition in film.
4. **Why it works** - The crew never chooses politics; the wreck brings the
   politics to them. Their skills stay the tools of the plot (piloting,
   repair, salvage). The reader learns three factions by watching each one
   react to a single message rather than by hearing them described.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Treat the ordinary recovery job as the beacon
   obligation: the crew is away because a checklist said so. Let the three
   recorders in chapter 2 form a chain where each one revises the reading of
   the last (a navy hull is not the same as a navy order; a company log can
   show prior knowledge). Give the crew's first instinct (tell everyone) a
   visible local cost at the relay station in chapter 3: someone acted on the
   partial version. Make the final channel choice the corrected second
   message, sent by people who now know what a first message did.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - The lead's idealism turns into lecture. The
   crew's message triggers a system-wide war, which breaks worker scale.
7. **Originality boundary** - No exotic agent or alien technology. No
   detective co-lead. No planetary tribes with coined demonyms. The crew must
   not become the pivot of a solar war; their consequences stay within a few
   ships and one station.
8. **Question for the outline** - Which recorder overturns the crew's first
   belief, and what did they already say or do on the strength of that belief?

### Alien (1979)

1. **Reference** - Dir. Ridley Scott, screenplay Dan O'Bannon (story with
   Ronald Shusett), 20th Century Fox / Brandywine, 1979. Reviewed: the first
   act aboard the towing vehicle before the creature appears.
   - https://blog.kinolime.com/articles/alien-1979-analysis
   - https://www.gradesaver.com/alien/study-guide/summary-part-1-aboard-the-nostromo
   - https://filmschoolrejects.com/alien-movie-labor-exploitation/
2. **Craft problem studied** - Making a commercial crew and its ship feel
   inhabited through hierarchy, pay talk, and maintenance language before
   any danger.
3. **Technique** - The first act is a shift, not a mission. The crew wakes,
   eats, runs systems, and argues about pay. The two lowest-paid members
   complain that the split is unfair and that a rescue is not in their
   contract. A clause turns a signal into an obligation with total forfeit
   as the penalty. The landing takes many pages of procedural trouble
   (fires, terrain, damage) and establishes stakes with no monster in view.
   Rank is asserted and questioned in ordinary terms: who decides, who
   complains, who gets overruled. The script hides who the protagonist is
   for a long time, so the audience watches the group. The ship reads as a
   workplace: dirty, loud, half-broken, and owned by someone absent.
4. **Why it works** - Money and rank are the most legible human stakes. A
   crew that argues about overtime is a crew the audience believes in. The
   procedural landing teaches the audience the ship's limits, so later
   failures need no explanation.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Open chapter 1 with the acceptance check and the
   guidance checks as a real checklist with real friction. Give the copilot
   and the engineer a small disagreement (hours, credit for the repair, the
   unauthorized orbit) that the new helm captain must settle. The
   "documented donut" is the crew's minor rebellion; because it is
   documented, it becomes a liability later, which is how a small workplace
   act ties to the larger plot. Show the crew's competence by what breaks
   and gets fixed on the job, not by praise.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - The pay dispute becomes a running gag and the
   crew a set of types. Danger arrives as horror beats instead of loss.
7. **Originality boundary** - No hidden company directive planted in the
   crew. No onboard computer as an authority figure. No contract clause that
   forces investigation of a signal. No creature, no cat, no self-destruct.
8. **Question for the outline** - What is the one ordinary disagreement the
   three crew have before the loss, and who does the destruction prove
   right?

### Planetes

1. **Reference** - Makoto Yukimura, serialized in Morning (Kodansha),
   1999-2004; anime by Sunrise for NHK, 2003-2004, dir. Goro Taniguchi.
   Reviewed: the debris-collection episodes, the corporate setting, and the
   critical view of the late escalation.
   - https://timeldred.com/planetes/
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetes
   - https://www.uk-anime.net/articles/The_success_and_failure_of_Planetes.html
2. **Craft problem studied** - Orbital labor as a complete social world, with
   procedure exposing values and institutional pressure felt as budget and
   staffing.
3. **Technique** - The crew is a low-status division inside a large company.
   Each episode is a job with a procedure (locate, approach, secure,
   dispose) that collides with a personal value: remains in orbit, a risk
   rule, a colleague's illness, a career ambition. Company indifference is
   shown by staffing and equipment, never by a speech. The wider politics
   (a wealth gap between nations, a militant movement, a flagship deep-space
   mission) are seen only through how they change a job or a coworker. The
   anime adds the office layer and workplace comedy; the manga stays with
   inner life. Critics agree the work is strongest when procedure is
   respected and weakest when it escalates to sabotage and grand plots.
4. **Why it works** - Procedure is a moral instrument: the moment a rule and
   a value conflict, the character shows who they are with no speech. The
   company stays real because it is felt as a missing part or an unfilled
   post.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Give each chapter one job the crew knows how to do,
   and put the story in the moment the procedure and a value conflict:
   recovery rules say tag and leave a recorder, salvage rules say log the
   find, and the crew must decide who owns the record of their dead. Show
   the company at the relay station as a staffing table and a budget line.
   Let the drift crews be ex-workers who left, seen through what they
   salvage and what they refuse to salvage. Keep any militant faction off
   stage; the drift crews are not it.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - Late escalation into sabotage, hacking, or a
   grand terror plot. A worker who becomes a savior.
7. **Originality boundary** - No debris-collector premise. No anti-rich
   militant faction as the villain. No deep-space-dream protagonist arc. No
   half-staffed-unit nickname or office-comedy cast.
8. **Question for the outline** - Which written procedure does the crew
   break in chapter 2, and what does the log of that breach later prove?

### Outland (1981)

1. **Reference** - Written and directed by Peter Hyams, The Ladd Company /
   Warner Bros., 1981. Reviewed: the company outpost, the drug scheme, the
   isolation of the investigator, and the local stakes.
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outland_(film)
   - https://popcult.blog/2021/06/23/movie-review-outland/
   - https://www.popmatters.com/outland-peter-hayms-film
2. **Craft problem studied** - An isolated industrial community where the
   employer holds all power and workers are vulnerable through material
   dependence.
3. **Technique** - A remote outpost where the company supplies everything,
   including the illegal. Deaths are filed as accidents and suicides. The
   workers know and stay silent because the shuttle comes once a week and
   the company is the only ride home. The manager assumes every official has
   a price because the last ones did. The investigator is offered a price,
   then isolated, and only one person (a physician with nothing to gain)
   chooses to help. Stakes are material and local: a shipment, a shuttle
   timetable, a locker, a corridor. Production design says function over
   comfort. The individual wins a local fight; the system is unchanged.
4. **Why it works** - Silence is rational, not cowardly. Every worker's
   calculation is legible: speak and lose the ride home. The film never
   needs to explain the company; the schedule explains it.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Make the relay station in chapter 3 a company-owned
   outpost with a skeleton crew who already know and stay silent because the
   company owns their berth, their fuel, and their transfer home. Make the
   stakes concrete: a docking permit, a resupply slot, a shift roster with a
   name struck out. One staffer helps with one small act that cannot be
   traced; the rest look away. The station is not a villain; it is a place
   where silence has a price list.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - The station becomes a shootout. The cutter is
   unarmed and must not win a gunfight. The lone-hero-versus-corrupt-boss
   shape swallows the crew.
7. **Originality boundary** - No productivity-drug scheme. No marshal or law
   officer. No countdown to hired killers. No frontier-western shape with
   a showdown.
8. **Question for the outline** - What does the relay staff stand to lose if
   they relay the recorders, and what one concrete thing can one of them
   give the cutter without being seen?

### Battlestar Galactica miniseries (2003)

1. **Reference** - Written by Ronald D. Moore, dir. Michael Rymer, Sci Fi
   Channel / Universal, 2003. Reviewed: the routine before the attack, how
   the attack reaches the characters, and the first decisions after.
   - https://en.battlestarwiki.org/Naturalistic_science_fiction
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlestar_Galactica_(miniseries)
   - https://musingsofamiddleagedgeek.blog/2021/04/21/reflecting-on-the-reimagined-miniseries-of-battlestar-galactica-2003-part-1/
2. **Craft problem studied** - Sudden destruction changing routine command
   relationships; a surviving group choosing an immediate purpose; absence
   and silence making loss comprehensible.
3. **Technique** - The opening routine is a ceremony of obsolescence: an old
   ship being retired, which gives every character a task on the day. The
   attack arrives as fragments: printouts, radio reports, a contact dropping
   off a screen, a count that keeps growing. Loss is shown as absence: a
   blank display, static where a broadcast was, a compartment vented and a
   roster shortened. Command shifts because of who survived and what was
   not connected, not because of merit. Civil and military authority both
   claim the survivors and clash at once. The first purpose is small and
   immediate: run, count, gather. A larger story is invented by a leader to
   keep people moving. Moore's production rules reject stock heroics and
   magic technology and demand that damage persists.
4. **Why it works** - Fragments force the audience to assemble the disaster
   with the characters. Absence is cheaper and more credible than
   spectacle. Command by survival, not by rank, makes every later order
   feel provisional.
5. **Nova adaptation** - End chapter 1 with the carrier's loss reaching the
   cutter as sensor dropout, a silent channel, a missing beacon, a debris
   cloud on a scope. No explosion in the player's face. The first command
   choice is small: return, hide, or count. In chapter 2 the crew counts
   recorders and names. The copilot and engineer take on roles the carrier
   used to hold, because the chain of command above them is gone. No
   invented grand purpose; the purpose is that someone must say who died.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - Mythic quest language. Speeches. Leaders
   instead of three coworkers.
7. **Originality boundary** - No lost-homeland quest. No infiltrator plot.
   No military-versus-civilian leadership duel. No decommissioning
   ceremony or museum-ship framing.
8. **Question for the outline** - What is the last routine message from the
   carrier before silence, and which of the three crew hears it?

### Chernobyl (HBO, 2019)

1. **Reference** - Created and written by Craig Mazin, dir. Johan Renck,
   HBO / Sky, 2019. Reviewed: the structure of withheld truth across
   levels, the delayed explanation, and the cost to the teller.
   - https://tvobsessive.com/2019/06/10/the-cost-of-lies-in-hbos-chernobyl/
   - https://slate.com/culture/2019/06/chernobyl-finale-hbo-miniseries-craig-mazin-interview.html
   Justification: the clearest recent model of institutional silence, and
   of a recorded record as the counter-instrument to it.
2. **Craft problem studied** - Institutional truth suppression, testimony,
   and the delay of explanation until suffering has been seen.
3. **Technique** - Truth is withheld at each level because admission costs
   pride, career, or a state's image. The recorded record (tapes, a
   testimony) becomes the counter-instrument. The technical explanation of
   what happened is delayed to the last episode; the writer says the
   audience must first see the suffering to be ready to hear the cause.
   Workers are abstracted by administrative language, and the show cuts
   from the word to the body it hides. Telling the truth costs the teller
   and does not fix the system. The writer held himself to changing
   details only when the story needed it, and published his changes.
4. **Why it works** - Delaying the cause keeps the audience in the workers'
   position: they lived it before anyone explained it. The gap between the
   official word and the person is the moral engine, and needs no speech.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Company and navy both know in chapter 3; each
   silence is a career, a contract, or a budget line, never malice. Delay
   the bridge recorder (the "why") until after the player has seen the wreck
   and counted. Put administrative language in company and navy messages
   (asset, incident, personnel loss) against the crew's use of names. Let
   the final choice carry cost for the crew and leave the institutions
   mostly unchanged.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - A closing monologue that explains everything.
   Officials as villains instead of people protecting positions.
7. **Originality boundary** - No trial. No suicide-note framing. No
   scientist hero. No radiation-style invisible hazard as a game system.
8. **Question for the outline** - Which institution's message uses the
   wrong word for the dead, and when does the player first read it?

### Hardspace: Shipbreaker (2020)

1. **Reference** - Blackbird Interactive, published by Focus Entertainment,
   early access 2020, release 2022. Reviewed: narrative delivery between
   shifts, corporate framing, and the coworker cast.
   - https://www.thegamer.com/hardspace-shipbreaker-review/
   - https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/hardspace-shipbreaker-review/
   - https://www.thegamer.com/hardspace-interview/
   Justification: the closest game precedent for worker-scale industrial
   space labor with a company that is felt but never met.
2. **Craft problem studied** - Delivering corporate framing and coworker
   character through work loops, radio voice, and paperwork.
3. **Technique** - The company is felt through numbers: an opening debt,
   daily fees, deductions, and a supervisor voice that enforces policy. The
   owner is never met. Coworkers are voices between shifts, each holding a
   stance toward the company: a believer, a frightened underperformer, and
   two who have stopped hoping. Company pressure produces the collective
   response it was meant to prevent. Story is told in the gaps between
   jobs, and the jobs themselves reinforce the theme (you make value that
   flows upward). Reviewers found the arc predictable but honest.
4. **Why it works** - Paperwork and voice cost nothing in scene budget and
   read as truth. A cast defined by stance toward the employer gives every
   conversation a position without exposition.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Deliver faction texture between jobs as radio
   voices and documents: an invoice, a shift notice, a routing order. Give
   the three crew distinct stances toward the company and toward the navy.
   Show the drift crews through why they left and what they will not
   salvage. The company's presence on the cutter is a voice and a form,
   never a face.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - Satirical posters and on-the-nose organizing
   speeches. A debt meter that reads as parody.
7. **Originality boundary** - No debt-servitude premise. No clone or
   genetic-rights clause. No salvage-for-quota loop. No union newsletter
   as a story device.
8. **Question for the outline** - What is the company's first message to the
   cutter after the loss, and what form does it take (invoice, notice,
   condolence, routing order)?

### Return of the Obra Dinn (2018)

1. **Reference** - Lucas Pope, 3909 LLC, 2018. Reviewed: the evidence model,
   the ledger, the confirmation rule, and chapter ordering.
   - https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/for-lucas-pope-i-return-of-the-obra-dinn-i-was-a-bunch-of-appealing-design-problems
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn
   Justification: the strongest design precedent for evidence recovered
   one item at a time where each item changes belief.
2. **Craft problem studied** - Evidence as gameplay: fragments, inference,
   commitment, and confirmation without hand-holding.
3. **Technique** - Every piece of evidence is a fixed moment, partial by
   design. A record book auto-fills the basics and leaves the player to
   name the who and the how. The player commits inferences in writing. The
   game confirms only when a set of three claims is correct, so guessing
   fails and reasoning is rewarded. The story is grouped into chapters by
   disaster, not by clock time, a structure the designer adopted after
   testers were lost in nonlinear order. The designer favors rigid core
   rules and constraints, and says the story can only be told at points of
   loss.
4. **Why it works** - Commitment turns belief into an action the player
   owns. Batch confirmation protects ambiguity from brute force. Chapter
   grouping gives shape without spoiling order.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Treat the three recorders as three lenses: what was
   sent (distress relay), what the ship did (engineering log), what was said
   (bridge recorder). Keep a crew ledger of claims: who fired, when, who
   knew. Let the player commit a claim and revise it later. Confirm a claim
   only when two or three recorders agree. Gate chapter progress by claims
   made, not by items collected, so belief drives action.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - The campaign becomes a puzzle game. Ambiguity
   reads as vagueness if the lenses do not overlap on some hard facts.
7. **Originality boundary** - No magical viewing device. No identity matrix
   of a full crew. No 1-bit look. No frozen tableau death scenes.
8. **Question for the outline** - What is the minimum claim set the player
   must commit to before the chapter 3 run, and which claim is wrong?

### Deepwater Horizon (2016)

1. **Reference** - Dir. Peter Berg, screenplay Matthew Michael Carnahan and
   Matthew Sand, Summit / Lionsgate, 2016. Reviewed: the routine opening,
   the pressure-test talk, the schedule as antagonist, and the ending.
   - https://www.deepfocusreview.com/reviews/deepwater-horizon/
   - https://www.npr.org/2016/09/26/495467460/deepwater-horizon-director-on-the-bp-oil-spill-and-the-addictive-dance-for-fuel
   Justification: a worker-scale industrial disaster where corporate
   pressure is a schedule number and procedure is the drama.
2. **Craft problem studied** - Industrial disaster told through procedure,
   with corporate framing carried by a schedule and a face.
3. **Technique** - The film opens at home, then commutes to the rig. Workers
   are introduced by what they check and what they argue about. Corporate
   pressure is a number of days behind schedule and one visiting manager
   who wants a test skipped. The disaster is a chain of readings and
   decisions, and the audience can follow it because the tests were taught
   earlier. The ending names the dead with photographs. Critics note the
   film avoids the corporate and environmental aftermath, which limits it.
4. **Why it works** - Teaching the test before it fails makes the failure
   legible. A schedule number is the least dramatic and most credible form
   of pressure.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Use the acceptance check in chapter 1 to make the
   player fluent in one measurement the later story depends on. Let a
   schedule pressure explain why the carrier was where it was. End with
   names, but do not avoid accountability: give it at worker scale, as the
   question of who gets to name the dead and where.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - Heroics and a victim list with no system in
   view. Family cutaways used as structure.
7. **Originality boundary** - No rig-blowout sequence. No on-site company
   villain. No home-and-family framing. No real-people postscript.
8. **Question for the outline** - What schedule pressure put the carrier at
   that position at that time, and does any recorder show it?

### Spotlight (2015)

1. **Reference** - Dir. Tom McCarthy, screenplay Josh Singer and Tom
   McCarthy, Open Road Films, 2015. Reviewed: the records procedural, the
   decision of when and what to publish, and the ending.
   - https://www.deepfocusreview.com/reviews/spotlight/
   - https://www.thehaughtyculturist.com/films/spotlight-2015-explained-conspiracy-of-silence/
   Justification: the clearest model for who controls the record and for a
   deliberate choice of what a disclosure should target.
2. **Craft problem studied** - Institutional silence, sealed records, and
   the ethics of the moment and target of disclosure.
3. **Technique** - The film is a procedural of records: sealed documents,
   directories, clippings. The team could publish early against
   individuals and chooses to wait until the pattern shows the institution.
   The newspaper's own past silence is part of the record, and the film
   refuses to exempt its protagonists. The ending withholds triumph: a list
   of more places where the same thing happened, and a story still going.
   Restraint is the style; no scene is bigger than the fact in it.
4. **Why it works** - The choice of target (persons versus system) is the
   drama, not the discovery. Complicity inside the protagonists' own house
   makes the final disclosure cost something.
5. **Nova adaptation** - Frame the final choice as target, not volume: the
   navy channel names culprits and controls the story; the company channel
   settles and seals; the open channel exposes the system and controls
   nothing. Put the crew's own complicity in the record: the undocumented
   orbit, the check they skipped, the fact they were away. End with the
   names and a setting that is still open.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - Journalism ethics grafted onto three
   engineers. A ringing-phones triumph beat.
7. **Originality boundary** - No newspaper, church, or reporter roles. No
   postscript list of cities. No sealed-court-document subplot.
8. **Question for the outline** - What does each channel do with the
   recorders within the story's time, and what does the crew give up under
   each?

### Das Boot (1981)

1. **Reference** - Dir. Wolfgang Petersen, Bavaria Film, 1981, from the
   novel by Lothar-Gunther Buchheim. Reviewed: confinement, procedure under
   attack, sound and silence, and distant command.
   - https://anempireofwords.wordpress.com/2017/01/22/das-boot-1981/
   - https://www.rogerebert.com/far-flung-correspondents/the-crushing-suspense-of-das-boot
   Justification: the model for a confined crew whose only sense is sound
   and whose command is a distant, unreliable voice.
2. **Craft problem studied** - A confined crew, procedure as the only
   agency, and silence as the main instrument of tension.
3. **Technique** - Long boredom alternates with short terror. During an
   attack every crew member has a role and does it; the audience learns the
   roles by repetition. The boat's only sense is sound, so the film makes
   the audience listen: pings, hull noise, and the crew's own silence.
   Command is a distant radio voice that sends orders late and knows less
   than the crew. The political regime is kept understated so the crew is
   read as workers. The ending empties the effort of meaning without a
   speech.
4. **Why it works** - Sound demands attention where images would show too
   much. Roles under pressure characterize with no dialogue. A distant
   command that is wrong makes the crew's judgment the only authority.
5. **Nova adaptation** - The chapter 2 escape from the cleanup group is run
   quiet: cut emitters, listen, hold in the wreck's shadow, move when the
   contact turns. Each crew member has one role in the escape and the
   player learns it by doing it twice. The navy is a channel that speaks
   late or not at all. Keep the politics understated; the crew is trying
   not to be seen, not fighting a war.
6. **Risk or failure mode** - Submarine cliches. The cutter behaves as a
   warship.
7. **Originality boundary** - No depth-charge beats. No sing-along or
   barracks humor set pieces. No war-regime frame or ironic homecoming.
8. **Question for the outline** - Which sensor does the cutter give up to
   stay hidden, and what does the crew hear instead?

## Cross-source findings

- Attachment comes from shift-scale work: a checklist, a pay split, a tool
  that fails and gets fixed. Alien, Leviathan Wakes, Planetes, Deepwater
  Horizon, Hardspace.
- A small grievance before the loss makes the loss legible; afterward the
  grievance is both petty and precious. Alien, Leviathan Wakes, BSG.
- The loss reaches the crew as fragments and absence: a contact gone from a
  scope, a channel with no reply, a shortened roster. BSG, Leviathan Wakes,
  Das Boot.
- Obligation pulls the crew in, not choice: a clause, a beacon, a
  procedure. Alien, Leviathan Wakes, Planetes.
- Evidence works as a chain in which each item corrects the last, and the
  player or crew commits to a belief before the next item arrives. Obra
  Dinn, Leviathan Wakes, Spotlight, Chernobyl.
- Factions are learned by watching them react to one message or one job,
  never by description. Leviathan Wakes, Planetes, Hardspace.
- Institutional silence is locally rational: a career, a ride home, a
  budget line. Nobody needs to be evil. Outland, Chernobyl, Spotlight.
- Administrative language against the names of the dead is the cheapest
  and strongest moral contrast available. Chernobyl, Deepwater Horizon,
  Spotlight.
- Command changes because of who is left, not who deserves it; every later
  order feels provisional. BSG, Das Boot.
- Distant command is a voice: late, wrong, or silent. The unanswered call
  is a scene in itself when the crew keeps working through it. Das Boot,
  BSG, Outland.
- Worker-scale endings give names and a list, and leave the system
  standing. Spotlight, Chernobyl, Outland, Das Boot.
- Escalation kills the grounded story: critics of Planetes and Deepwater
  Horizon fault the moves toward grand plots or plain heroics.
- The choice of who receives the truth is a choice of target and control:
  everyone at once (Leviathan Wakes, with cost), the system rather than
  persons (Spotlight), inside the institution at personal cost (Chernobyl),
  or the collective instead of the employer (Hardspace).

## Not used

- Apollo 13 (1995): procedure as drama and a re-entry radio blackout, but
  the crew is a national heroic mission, not workers; the blackout device is
  covered by Das Boot and BSG. https://talkfilmsociety.com/articles/a-successful-failure-ron-howards-apollo-13-1995
- Her Story (2015): evidence by database query; too solitary and too
  detached from a working crew; Obra Dinn covers commitment and revision.
- The Wire: who controls the record across institutions, but a season-long
