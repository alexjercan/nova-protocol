1

One card, two states

520 px instead of 360, so the buttons sit in a row and the field note keeps its two lines without wrapping to four. It is the same card for everyone — only the emphasis and the button order change between a fresh profile and a returning one.

2

The menu row comes out

The corner card is the handbook's only door, so the menu card goes back to six rows and the eye has one place to look for lessons instead of two.

That forces one thing: the card can never leave the screen. Not now demotes it to State B below, it does not remove it — a card that could be dismissed for good would take the handbook with it.

State A — fresh profile

Training — new pilot STOP is an order, not a brake. The flight computer burns until your speed reads zero.

State B — after Not now, or after flying

Training STOP is an order, not a brake. The flight computer burns until your speed reads zero.

State B is permanent, and with the menu row gone it is load-bearing: it is the only route to the lessons. It keeps the rotating field note, which is the reason to glance at the corner at all; only the pilot-facing pitch and Start Basic Training go away.

2 · Lesson page

one tip, one screen

Page navigation is gone. A lesson is a loop and the words that go with it, so Prev, Next and Page N of M come out entirely, and each of today's three-page placeholders becomes three separate tips.

3

Rows lose their summary line

With the whole tip in the text box, a summary under every row said the same thing twice. Title and state badge only: about eleven rows visible at once instead of six, which matters more once one lesson per page doubles the list to roughly 24 tips.

4

The loop sets the split

A 16:9 frame at the full width of the pane lands at almost exactly three quarters of its height on a 16:9 monitor, so the quarter you asked for at the bottom falls out of the aspect ratio rather than being a number I picked.

Where a tip has no loop yet, the frame stays and holds the art to come placeholder — the page must not reflow between an authored lesson and a pending one.

5

Everything else lives in the box

The whole tip, the binding chips inline in the sentence that needs them, and a thin action strip: Practice this on the left, the wiki path on the right. Roughly 45 words is what fits at the floor without scrolling — that is the real cap on lesson copy, and Phase 2 writes to it.

3 · The 640 × 600 floor

supported narrow window

Same two plates at the smallest window the game supports. This is where the split stops being free: a 16:9 loop across a 300 px pane is only about half its height, so the text box takes the rest.

6

The card cannot get wider here

The menu card is 280 px at right: 40, which leaves 280 px between it and the left edge. The card takes 256 of them and stacks its two buttons. The 520 px width is a desk-monitor width with a percentage cap under it.

7

Loop shape at the floor

Two ways to go, and this is the one real layout decision left: keep 16:9 and let the text box grow to about half the pane, or hold the loop at three quarters and letterbox it with bars at the sides.

The drawing shows the first. It keeps every loop the same shape on every machine, which is what an authored animation wants.

Open questions

  1. Settled: the card is the only door. The Training menu row comes out, so the card can never be dismissed away — only demoted to State B. Open underneath it: does Not now still earn its place when all it can do is change the card's emphasis? Drawn: keep it, so a first-time player has a way to say “read it, leave me alone” and get the quieter corner.

  2. Does the pause menu need the same door? Mid-flight there is no corner card, so a player who wants a tip has to quit to the main menu for it. Drawn: nothing — outside this schematic. Say the word and the pause overlay gets the entry instead.

  3. Do lesson rows keep their one-line summary? Drawn: no. Title and badge, so more of a longer list is visible at once and the text box is the only place the tip is written.

  4. At the floor, 16:9 loop with a taller text box, or a fixed three-quarter loop with side bars? Drawn: 16:9 everywhere. Pick the other and every authored loop needs a safe area inside the frame.

  5. Roughly 45 words per tip as the hard cap? That is what the box holds at the floor without scrolling. Say a number and Phase 2 writes to it.
