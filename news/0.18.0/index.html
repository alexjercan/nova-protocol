v0.18.0 - The world answers back

v0.17.0 was the release where the world comes to you. Traders flew to stations, traded and posted jobs. But the world said little about what you did in it. A docking clamp closed with no sound. A railgun slug landed with the same click as any other penetrating hit. No station cared what you carried, and a scenario could hear a job accepted but not a job finished.

v0.18.0 makes the world answer. A clamp latches with a sound, a light and DOCKED <NAME>. Every sound, your own ship's included, now plays from its place in the world, at one even loudness. Stations refine ore into alloy plate, and the plate builds a second tier of sections. A stranded ship asks for goods. Lawful stations do not trade contraband. An escort's raiders grow with the region, and turrets lead a ship that burns. Station Control speaks about your first job. The key row shows only the keys that apply, and a gamepad can dock. And scenarios can read what an event carries, follow a job to its end, and pay, give, take, post jobs and set stances.

Cargo that matters

Refine ore into alloy plate

Some stations now offer Refine. Docked at one, click an ore a recipe uses in the Inventory pane, then Refine beside Sell. The form counts batches and states each one whole: 2 Iron ore + 1 Carbon ore + 30 cr -> 1 Alloy plate. Confirm takes the ore from your hold, adds the plate and pays the fee to the station. A refine that cannot run changes nothing and says why.

One batch takes 2 iron ore, 1 carbon ore and a 30 cr fee, and gives 1 alloy plate. At base prices iron ore sells for 12 cr, carbon ore for 6 cr and an alloy plate for 68 cr. A batch sold as ore earns 30 cr; the same batch refined and sold as plate earns 38 cr after the fee. A plate costs 90 cr to buy. A hold of 100 iron ore and 40 carbon ore covers 40 batches: a 1,200 cr fee, 40 plates and 20 iron ore left over. Sold as ore that hold earns 1,440 cr. Refined and sold, it earns 1,760 cr. A generated trader moves each price by its stock, its cluster and its civilization, so the numbers at a real station differ.

Refineries are out in the more advanced civilizations. A generated station refines only when its civilization is at advancement 0.25 or more, and a New Game starts among civilizations well below that. Your start station does not refine: the form there reads Refused: <station> does not refine. Plan a trip, and carry the ore with you.

A civilization's advancement rises with the distance of its centre from the world origin, slowly at first, and reaches 1.00 at 4,000 km. Each civilization reads up to a tenth of that value over or under it. A station reads its civilization's advancement.

Every living station of a civilization at 0.25 or more refines. A section is different: each civilization knows a section its advancement opens on one even draw, so about half of them build it.

A second tier of sections

An alloy plate is a part, and five new sections spend one. Each is the second tier of a section that had one variant. Its recipe is the first tier's parts plus one alloy plate, at one and a half times the credits.

The Dart

dart_torpedo_section loads the Dart, a third torpedo type. It flies the Lance's straight run at a cruise of 450 m/s against the Lance's 350. Over a 3 km run-in it arrives in 7.9 s where a Lance takes 9.3 s. Point defense solves it as it solves a Lance. The bay opens at advancement 0.25, and its recipe is the torpedo bay's plus one alloy plate.

Supply jobs

A station board can now post a Supply job. A neutral ship drifts 10 km out and asks for goods: dock with its caller with the whole stack in your hold, and the dock takes it. The goods name the job. Medkits make it a rescue (Rescue a stranded crew with 3 Medkit), rations a resupply, ammunition a rearm, hull plates a repair, and cheap ore or parts a supply run. A board never asks for contraband. The count fits the smallest start hold, and the reward covers the goods at ask plus half. Generated stations sell medkits, rations, plates and ammunition. A Distress call is unchanged: dock with the caller and it is done.

With the hold short the dock takes nothing. The HUD says what is missing, such as JOB-1: SHORT 1 MEDKIT, and the job stays open: dock again with the rest.

Contraband and lawful stations

Half the civilizations of a generated world are now lawful. The market of a living lawful station does not deal in Contraband: it buys none and sells none. Open a Sell of the item there and the form reads Refused: <station> does not deal in contraband, with Confirm disabled. The station still lets you dock with the item in your hold, and its storage keeps it. Sell the item at a station that is not lawful.

The base game has one such item, Unmarked cores: 10 kg, with a 300 cr ask and a 240 cr bid. Scavengers deal them. Half the intact scavenger ships carry one unit.

Fights that fit the region

Escort raiders grow with the region

The raiders that wait for an escort now come from the advancement of the civilization that posted it. Near the start, one small raider waits: about the size of your start ship, with one turret. Further out they come in twos and threes, and in the advanced core three of the civilization's own scavengers wait. Each raider adds 150 cr to the reward. In v0.17.0 the hauler's cargo set the count: one raider, plus one per 700 cr of cargo, up to three.

A raider below 0.75 mounts turrets only: no torpedo bay and no railgun. The chart scope above shows how far out each advancement lies. The hauler now carries a spare pair of drives beside its flight computer, so one cut through its hull does not leave it adrift.

Turrets lead a ship that burns

Turrets now lead a target's acceleration as well as its course. A ship that burns or turns under fire no longer pulls out from under the rounds: the solve aims where the hull will be if it keeps accelerating as it does.

A turret also no longer holds fire on a small mark far out. One turret of a raider 2 km away looks smaller than the last tremor of a settled barrel, so in v0.17.0 your guns waited, with the trigger down, for an aim they could not reach. A barrel settled on its aim now fires. Half a degree off still holds. See Turret.

The ship tells you

A clamp you can hear

Every docking port of a station now wears a beacon: a cold light that breathes on a 3 s pulse. It burns amber while a ship holds the port and is dark on a dead station. The beacons follow the Flash lights setting: Off, which the Low preset uses, lights none.

When the clamp closes, your port plays the latch, a cold light blinks on the collar, and the HUD posts DOCKED <NAME>. Letting go plays the release and posts UNDOCKED. A dock that a Load rebuilds is not a new clamp, and a dock that damage ends has no release, so both stay quiet. The loop at the top of this post shows the sequence.

A railgun hit you can see

A railgun slug landing is now its own event. The entry face flashes the slug's steel blue, throws a dense burst of spall and plays the lance's own impact sound. It happens once per shot, at the first body the slug meets, however many cells the corridor crosses.

The kill stinger and the camera jolt now confirm any hull your own fire defeats: PDC rounds, a railgun slug, a torpedo blast or a ram. In v0.17.0 only a torpedo kill confirmed.

A tick for every pickup

When your intake takes a canister, a pickup tick prints above the key row: one +<COUNT> <ITEM> line per stack, such as +4 HULL PLATE, for 1.5 s. At most three lines show. The intake blinks warm at the same moment. A take by another hull shows nothing.

Sector crossings

Crossing into another sector of a generated world now posts SECTOR (x, y, z) on the HUD notice, with - FIRST VISIT for a sector your chart had not recorded. A New Game or a Load posts nothing for the sector you start in.

Every sound in its place

Your ship sounds from where it is

Your own ship's sounds now play in the world like every other ship's. A world sound plays at the section or body that made it, fades with its distance from your ship and pans with the camera. A hit on your plate is heard where it landed, and a gun on your far side sounds from that side.

One loudness, one table

Every sound file is measured when it loads and evened out to one loudness. Then each kind of sound, from a gun to a door, an alarm or a menu click, has one level, one reach and one repeat rate, set in one table. The interface sits under the world: a click is as loud as a nearby gun, never louder than a launch or the drive.

Each sound plays at its row's level, relative to the loudness every file is measured to. A world sound plays at full level out to its first distance, then falls as that distance over yours, eases to silence over the last quarter before its second distance, and is silent there. An interface sound plays flat.

The torpedo bay door no longer blasts over the guns. It is quiet ship machinery now, and one ship's doors share one voice however many bays open at once.

Sliders that fade evenly

The volume sliders now fade evenly in decibels over their whole travel. A slider no longer does most of its work in the last few notches. The World slider is now Effects, and the unused Music slider is gone. Your saved levels carry over, and the console takes volume effects.

Keys that apply

The key row shows what you can do

In v0.17.0 the flight key row kept one fixed slot per verb and drew a verb dark when it did not apply. Now the row shows only the chips that apply. Five core chips always sit left of the centre line: INTERFACE, DRIVE, STOP, RCS and RADAR. The rest join right of it while there is something for them to do:

  • GOTO while you hold a travel lock, ORBIT near a gravity well and CANCEL while the autopilot is engaged.
  • DOCK while your lock carries a docking port or you are docked, and HELM while you are docked.
  • MINE on a ship with a bound mining beam, and AIM on a ship with a turret, a torpedo bay or a railgun.
  • FIRE, TORPEDO and RAILGUN while the weapons are hot.

A shown chip you cannot press right now still draws dark. That includes RCS on an empty magazine, which v0.17.0 drew lit.

The row on an armed hull that can fly, with the default keyboard keys. A chip in brackets is dark; a chip with a star is inverted, for a state the ship is in.

On a gamepad the same chips draw pad buttons, and DOCK and HELM draw two caps: L3 then Y, and L3 then A.

Dock and take the helm from a gamepad

DOCK and HELM had no default gamepad button. They are now chords on the RCS button, like MATCH: hold L3 and press Y to dock or let go, hold L3 and press A to take or hand back the helm. The key row draws both caps on the DOCK and HELM chips.

New hulls put the weapons on free buttons. A torpedo bay takes LB, a railgun takes View, and a ship's first mining beam takes RT as well as V. LB is also free look and View is also the HUD toggle, so while the weapons are hot those two do nothing on a pad; Alt and the grave key still work. RT is also FIRE: with the weapons cold it only mines. See Keybinds.

Scenarios hear more and do more

Station Control speaks

Station Control now speaks in the open world, three times, each once per world. When the work of your first job is done, it sends you back to the station that posted it. On your first hand-in it pays a 50 cr bonus over the reward. And when the world takes a job you hold, it calls the contract void.

The base game writes those lines with the same events and actions a mod gets. This is the hand-in handler of the Open World scenario:

(
    name: OnJobHandedIn,
    once: true,
    actions: [
        GrantCredits((
            ship: "player",
            amount: 50,
        )),
        NarrativeCue((
            speaker: "Station Control",
            text: "First delivery logged. A 50 cr bonus is on your account. The board has more work.",
        )),
    ],
),

Jobs, stances and sectors as events

OnJobAccepted came with v0.17.0. Three more events now follow a held job to its end: OnJobDone when its work in the world is finished, OnJobHandedIn when the reward is paid, and OnJobDropped with a cause of Lost or Abandoned. All four carry id, kind, station, reward_cr and post_key. A procurement has no work in the world, so it raises no OnJobDone.

OnStanceChanged fires once per step of a civilization's stance toward the player, with civilization, from and to. OnSectorEntered fires when the player crosses into another sector, with sector, x, y, z and from. See Job events.

Read what an event carries

An expression can now read a field of the event its handler runs for. In RON it is Event("field"); typed in the editor it is event.field, as in event.price_cr > 100. It works in a handler's filters and in its own VariableSet and TimerStart. Lint refuses a field the event does not carry and names the fields it has.

Three new queries read the world: entity("id").credits, entity("id").items("Item") and scenario.sector. See Event fields.

Five actions that change the world

  • GrantCredits grants credits to a ship or a station, or charges it with a negative amount.
  • GiveItems and TakeItems put items into a hold or take them out, all or nothing.
  • PostJob posts a procurement on a generated station's board under a key. The job has no time limit, a world save keeps it, and the job events carry the key as post_key.
  • SetStance puts a civilization on Ally, Neutral or Enemy.

No author can write the id of a generated station or civilization, so PostJob and SetStance take a binding, $id or $other_id, that the event fills in. Under OnDocked, $id is the station the player docked with:

(
    name: OnDocked,
    once: true,
    actions: [PostJob((station: "$id", key: "ore_run", item: "IronOre", count: 4, reward_cr: 250))],
),

An action that cannot run changes nothing and raises the new OnActionRefused event with a reason, such as insufficient_credits, hold_full, short_items or duplicate_job. See Ship state.

The new blocks in the editor

The Events canvas of the mod editor has blocks for the new events and actions. The Query block is now picked, not typed: one chip lists every read, a read of a ship adds a chip that lists the scenario's objects, and Items adds a chip that lists the items the mod sees.

More in v0.18.0

  • The editor binds new weapons to free pad buttons. A new torpedo bay takes LB and a railgun takes View, in place of LT (raise weapons) and R3 (interface). A ship's first mining beam takes RT as well as V.
  • Ports and lances carry their own sounds. DockingSectionConfig gains optional clamp_sound and release_sound, and RailgunSectionConfig gains optional impact_sound. The base port and the base lance author all three.
  • Refining and scenario grants name their cause. OnCreditsChanged gains the causes Refine and Scenario.
  • Chips stay above the key row. A job, objective or beacon chip pinned to the bottom edge now stands above the key row and the notice box, not over the RCS, RADAR and AIM keys.
  • Weapon effects follow the weapon. The railgun wake, the PDC muzzle flash, the torpedo launch puff and the mining sparks follow the weapon on every heading, and a railgun slug is drawn from the muzzle, not a step ahead of it.
  • The Problems tab checks the right world. The mod editor checks a scenario on the world its file states, so a generated-world scenario no longer lists SpawnStartRole as an error or OnResume as a warning.

For modders

Run content lint on every bundle before you publish for v0.18.0. Four changes break content that v0.17.0 accepted.

  • (breaking) Medkit is a base role item. It is Repair, 2 kg, with a 60 cr ask and a 45 cr bid, and a Supply job that asks for it is a rescue. A mod item with the id Medkit replaces it and must keep the category Repair.
  • (breaking) A job event needs a generated world. A scenario with no generated world that names OnJobAccepted, OnJobDone, OnJobHandedIn or OnJobDropped is a lint error and does not load: no station there posts a job board.
  • (breaking) A reserved pad or key source fails lint. A player input_mapping source that the flight or camera rig holds is now an error, not a warning. A thruster on Space or RB and a torpedo bay on LB are shared defaults and pass. A torpedo bay on LT does not: move it to LB.
  • (breaking) PlaySound names a kind of sound and a place. It drops route and volume and takes a sound category and at: Ui, or Body("<id>") with a scenario object id, $id or $other_id. The category's row sets the level and the reach. Lint refuses a world category at Ui, an interface category at a Body and a loop category. See PlaySound.
  • Sound files must play. Lint decodes and measures every sound file a bundle declares in resources. A file the game cannot decode, or a silent one, is an error. See How sounds play.
  • Generated worlds only. SetStance, PostJob, OnStanceChanged, OnSectorEntered and scenario.sector are lint errors on a scenario with no generated world.
  • GiveItems and TakeItems raise no hold event. OnPickedUp reports a canister and OnTraded a trade, and these are neither. Read the result with entity("id").items("Item").
  • Refine is content. Item gains optional refine: Some((inputs, fee_cr)), and station services take Refine. Lint refuses a recipe whose inputs and fee cost less than its output's bid. The authored waystation refines, for a scenario that spawns it; a New Game world does not. See Refine.
  • Contraband is a category. Item category takes Contraband: the market of a lawful generated station does not buy or sell it. Base adds UnmarkedCores and a Scavenger Contraband price rule; a pack needs no new rule of its own. An authored station is never lawful.

Check Medkit, job events, bindings and PlaySound, and start a new world

A mod item with the id Medkit outside the category Repair, a job event in a scenario with no generated world, a player input_mapping source the flight or camera rig holds, or a PlaySound with route or volume fails lint in v0.18.0. A world save belongs to the release that wrote it, so a v0.17.0 world save is refused: start a new world.
