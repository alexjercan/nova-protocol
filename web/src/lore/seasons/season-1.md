# Season 1: The water-station arc

**Status:** Shared production outline for the planned comic and mainline
campaign. This is not a released comic season or a playable campaign.

**Spoilers:** This reference includes the intended first-season ending.

This document remains an unpublished production reference. Its approved beats
are adapted into comic scenes and pages, and into campaign mission briefs and
scenarios, not published verbatim at `/lore/`. Keep it for continuity as those
adaptations develop. The [publication policy](../README.md#production-and-publication)
governs when released events enter public article histories.

The approved direction is recorded below. Working proposals and decisions still
needed are listed separately. These stages are not episode boundaries; the
season title, episode count, and mission list have not been chosen.

## Shared story contract

The comic and mainline campaign tell the same story, with the same major events,
character decisions, and outcomes. Missions can expand the work between those
events. They cannot establish a contradictory history or an alternate viewpoint
that replaces the central story.

The player acts through the whole ship. The captain is an authored character,
not an on-foot player avatar. Game dialogue stays restrained. The comic can show
longer conversations and interiors; the game can present those events through
comms, cinematic exterior shots, and points of interest. There is no requirement
for walkable interiors or a second, novel-style telling.

The progression is from useful service, through trusted companions and an
alliance, to an independently controlled home. It is not a market simulation or
a campaign about earning enough money to buy a station.

## Starting situation

The story takes place in Saturn's developing settlement network, roughly 10-15
years after the first permanent station. The central tension remains: Saturn is
being sold as a permanent home, but administered as a temporary worksite.

The protagonist is Jonah Mercer, the established, employed captain of
[Kaveri](../ships/kaveri.md). Jonah is already competent at leadership, is not a
newcomer to Saturn, and does not own the ship or the employer. The initial
responsibility is to the crew, not to lead a political movement.

The employer is Clearwell Waterworks, a small company founded at Saturn by
former EarthWorks staff. It has no Earth parent. [Baikal](../places/baikal.md),
its water-processing station, is its principal business; whether it has any
other operations remains open. The company is genuinely decent, offers decent
working conditions, and wants to repair its problems and continue operating
independently.

The water station is both workplace and home to a small permanent community.
It is an industrial installation with residents, not another major station town.
Its eventual loss threatens a real community, not just a company balance sheet.

Ordinary civilian and industrial ships are unarmed. The opening centers on
useful work, flight, and a noncombat rescue. There is no pirate pursuit during
this sequence. Weapons and more complex command responsibilities enter later.

The three established places are [Keystone](../places/keystone.md), the original
construction-and-supply hub; [Aquila](../places/aquila.md), Farspan's freight-hub
town; and [Baikal](../places/baikal.md), the small water-processing community.
Clearwell Waterworks remains the company's name, distinct from the station's.

The first named ship cast is not a complete inventory of the region's fleets:

Name every deliberately authored story ship. Establish recurring vessels through
ordinary work, port calls, recognizable crews, and relationships before using
loss or capture for impact. Their importance comes from what they mean to
people, not simply their size. Do not assign deaths just to complete a dramatic
roster.

Redress and Windfall are the two ships involved in the independent attack on
Gantry. They recur later, even though the opening does not show the attack or
need to speak their names. Their articles hold the approved vessel identities;
their participation in Gantry's attack stays here until that event is released.

Regular ports are not current berths. Episode outlines must establish where
ships are at each major turn, what journeys brought them there, and what their
absence or loss changes. Exact dock arrangements, transfer areas, routes, and
travel times remain open. Ship designs and crews beyond Kaveri and Gantry have
not yet been chosen. Company articles hold their approved naming traditions.
Redress and Windfall use names chosen for their pirate identities, rather than
retaining old registrations. The [former working outfit](#former-working-outfit)
section tracks their trial origin and ship financing. Names and operators before
that business, and individual crew histories, remain open. These two pirate
names do not establish a general renaming rule after every sale or capture.

Foundation and Altair supply large-ship presence without turning the local
conflict into a war between sovereign fleets. The state's capital warships
remain a separate level of power, and military intervention does not arrive to
solve this season.

## Character directions

Kaveri has five cross-trained civilian crew members. Their introductory
identities and relationships live in the character articles:

- [Jonah Mercer](../characters/jonah-mercer.md): captain.
- [Leila Haddad](../characters/leila-haddad.md): chief engineer.
- [Tomas Vega](../characters/tomas-vega.md): pilot and navigator.
- [Rina Okafor](../characters/rina-okafor.md): work operations lead.
- [Samir Bell](../characters/samir-bell.md): systems technician and first-aider.

These are narrative responsibilities, not five player classes or a staffing
simulation. Player input represents the operation of the whole ship. There is
no initial military specialist or dedicated ship's doctor.

### Jonah Mercer

The captain expects to return to Earth after the posting. Earth offers a
credible, settled future, not a waiting-partner or family subplot. A real
opportunity to leave must remain available. Staying at Saturn is ultimately a
choice, not the result of financial entrapment.

The character grows from responsibility for the crew to responsibility for a
wider community and coalition. This is not a story about an incompetent captain
learning basic leadership. Restrained dialogue leaves room for player
imagination without making the captain an empty role.

### Leila Haddad

The captain's closest crewmate is Leila Haddad, a trusted engineer who has made
a life at Saturn. Professional trust comes first. Their different expected futures create
quiet tension without making either person's choice unreasonable.

Leila is sociable off duty and runs a fiercely competitive station game night.
Keep that wider life present; the character is not merely a source of repair
exposition.

### The rest of the crew

Tomas wants to complete the posting and leave. Precision and concern for the
crew's consent are not cowardice. Rina's personal ties to residents make helping
a concrete obligation rather than an abstract cause. Their disagreement gives
Jonah real responsibilities to weigh, not an obviously correct voice and a
wrong one.

Samir notices discrepancies and trusts records over rumors. Evidence still has
to be obtained and understood; this habit is not an automatic answer to the
conspiracy. First-aid responsibility does not make Samir a full medical service.

The later outcomes for Tomas, Rina, and Samir remain open. Do not assume every
crew member must choose the same permanent future as Jonah.

### The employer's people

The founders and workers demonstrate that lawful, independent, decent companies
already exist. The company's later destruction does not mean its principles
were foolish or worthless.

[Elena Ward](../characters/elena-ward.md) is Clearwell's co-founder, Baikal's
resident manager, and the opening's shore contact. The character article owns
Elena's approved identity, practical and approachable temperament, dry humour,
and professional trust in Jonah.

Elena authorizes company spending on the rescue, backs Jonah's decision, and
handles Foundation's delivery complaint. Clearwell bears the costs rather than
passing blame to the crew. Elena accepts the financial loss without pretending
it is painless or setting aside anger about EarthWorks' double standard.

Elena's finer background, ownership share, and later fate remain open. Other
founders and supporting workers still need identities and roles.

### EarthWorks personnel

EarthWorks is the principal corporate antagonist, but its entire workforce is
not part of the conspiracy. Senior managers commission the operation. Ordinary
employees and most security personnel do not know the plan.

Its genuine history of infrastructure development makes its later claim to
restore order credible. The crime exploits that reputation; it does not erase
every honest achievement or make every employee an enemy.

#### Opening business contacts

Two different EarthWorks staff handle the rescue expense and the water order:

| Contact | Workplace | Opening responsibility and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daniel Reed, EarthWorks Operations contact | Keystone | Decides whether to fund Kaveri's rescue diversion; refuses despite receiving Gantry's survival estimate. |
| Mara Chen, Foundation's supply coordinator | Aboard Foundation | Handles the expected water delivery; complains to Clearwell about the delay and insists on the contractual penalty despite knowing its cause. |

Daniel appears through comms. The opening does not visit Keystone or need a
separate establishing scene there. Mara complains to Clearwell, and Jonah hears
the complaint through Elena. Both exchanges stay within Kaveri's crew viewpoint.
No new station is introduced, and Foundation does not become EarthWorks'
regional headquarters.

Mara is genuinely relieved that Gantry's crew survived while still insisting on
the delivery terms. Keep that contrast rather than making Foundation's whole
crew resent the rescue. The exact dialogue remains open. Daniel and Mara are
minor working contacts, not another main ensemble or a reason to add side
stories. Their biographies and any involvement in the later conspiracy remain
open.

### Gantry's crew

Gantry has three cross-trained civilian crew members, not another five-person
main ensemble:

- [Nadia Sen](../characters/nadia-sen.md): captain and pilot. Professional and
  composed, Nadia expects EarthWorks to meet its obligations. One manager's
  failure does not immediately erase that belief.
- [Owen Park](../characters/owen-park.md): engineer. Injured during the opening
  attack, Owen still helps Leila understand the damage. Evacuation requires
  assistance, not a miraculous medical intervention.
- [Ivo Marin](../characters/ivo-marin.md): work operations technician. Ivo keeps
  the surviving compartment secure and helps Owen, and is more openly angry
  about the company's abandonment than Nadia.

They remain working colleagues with useful judgment during the rescue, not
three helpless passengers. All three are evacuated aboard Kaveri. Their lives
after the opening, and Gantry's eventual hull fate, remain open.

### The pirates

The pirates are culpable but likeable. Grievances, revenge, loyalty, competence,
and dependence on supplies make them understandable without excusing their
choices. Their initial raids are independent, not secretly commissioned from
the beginning.

They later accept corporate assignments. Becoming dependent on corporate
clients constrains their choices but does not remove their agency. An eventual
alliance with their victims is not proof that they were innocent all along.

Adrian Shaw is the pirate captain and a co-founder of the
[former working outfit](#former-working-outfit), not a later arrival who took
over the pirate group. Pride in what they helped build, loyalty to its people,
and responsibility for the business contribute to the choice to keep it
together through robbery. Money alone does not explain that choice. Adrian
retains the working name used before piracy, rather than adopting a pirate alias.

The broader business origin below remains a trial direction, not yet part of
the public ship histories.

## Season progression

### Work and a costly rescue

The opening's causal chain is approved:

1. **Useful work.** A failed circulation-pump assembly has shut down one of
   Baikal's processing lines safely. Residential supplies and life support
   remain protected, but reduced output threatens Ebro's water order for
   EarthWorks, to be delivered to Foundation.
   Kaveri's crew collects a replacement assembly at Aquila to restore output
   before that load is due. A brief working encounter with Nadia introduces
   Gantry before its later distress call.
2. **Confirmed survivors.** An independent pirate attack leaves Gantry's workers
   needing rescue. Samir establishes that people are alive. EarthWorks declines
   to fund Kaveri's emergency diversion despite receiving Gantry's survival
   estimate.
3. **An informed decision.** Tomas calculates the cost of diverting. Rina argues
   for the workers. Leila establishes a workable recovery plan. Jonah commits
   Kaveri with Clearwell's support. The decision is not a contest between a
   compassionate captain and an uncaring employer.
4. **Rescue, not victory.** Kaveri docks directly with Gantry and evacuates all
   three crew members. The pirates are already gone and do not return or
   pursue Kaveri. The campaign version emphasizes ship handling, rendezvous,
   and recovery, with no combat. Gantry's eventual hull fate is not decided by
   the rescue of its people.
5. **Success with consequences.** The crew brings the survivors to Baikal, but
   the delayed repair means Ebro's water load for Foundation is late. EarthWorks
   complains despite knowing that the diversion saved its own employees.
   Clearwell pays the delivery penalties and rescheduling costs rather than
   blaming the crew. EarthWorks later exploits the weakened commercial position.

The primary cost is the diversion and return journey, which delay the repair
and cause the missed delivery. Baikal's replacement assembly remains intact
aboard Kaveri. The cost does not depend on an artificially slow transfer, a
newly wrecked Kaveri, or a sacrificed critical spare.

#### Baikal's pump and Ebro's delivery

EarthWorks is the buyer of Ebro's opening water order, and Foundation is the
receiving vessel. The late resupply is a commercial problem, not an immediate
life-support emergency aboard Foundation.

The failed assembly serves industrial water production, not the residents'
last working life-support system. The rest of the plant continues safely at
reduced output. This opening does not ask Jonah to endanger Baikal's residents
to save Gantry's three crew members; the immediate threat to life is aboard
Gantry.

Replacing the pump assembly restores production sooner than a lengthy on-site
overhaul. Kaveri is doing a useful, ordinary cargo job, not bringing a miraculous
invention to save the station. The replacement is expected to restore enough
output to prepare Ebro's contracted water load on time.

The diversion delays that recovery. Ebro's load is late, and Clearwell absorbs
the financial consequences from its operating margin. It is a painful but
recoverable setback, not immediate bankruptcy. The later sabotage destroys
the company, not this single humane decision.

The delivery deadline is contractual, not an unavoidable orbital window.
EarthWorks knows why the load is late, but complains about the missed schedule
and applies the delivery penalties. Clearwell saved the company's own workers
after it declined to fund the diversion, then faces penalties from that same
company for the resulting delay. This is not merely a failure to tell the buyer
what happened.

The complaint comes from Mara Chen aboard Foundation, not Daniel Reed, who
refuses funding at Keystone. It does not establish that Foundation's crew as a
whole resents the rescue. Neither contact is automatically assigned a role in
the later conspiracy.

Foundation's delivery location, the quantity, detailed contract terms, and
timing remain open. Its regular association with Keystone does not fix its
berth for this order. No market simulation or player-facing financial ledger
is required to show the consequences.

#### Aquila pickup and first contact

Kaveri collects the replacement pump assembly at
[Aquila](../places/aquila.md), Farspan's freight hub. Gantry is also there on an
ordinary supply pickup for EarthWorks. The visit uses the established freight
relationship between the companies; it does not change Gantry's regular regional
port from Keystone to Aquila.

Before the ships leave on their separate jobs, Kaveri's crew has a brief
professional exchange with Nadia. Stay with Kaveri's viewpoint for this encounter.
Introduce a working colleague and a recognizable vessel, not an impending victim
or an invented old friendship. When Gantry's distress call arrives during
Kaveri's return to Baikal, its captain is a familiar voice.

The exchange's dialogue, cargo-handling arrangements, and exact departure order
remain open, as do Gantry's specific supplies and destination. Choosing the
pickup port does not fix the intercept geometry, travel times, or ship designs.

#### Gantry's damage and endurance

The independent pirates wanted cargo, not to destroy Gantry. Redress supplies
the armed force in the attack that disables Gantry's main propulsion and damages
its main electrical distribution. Windfall collects and carries the stolen
loads. Both crews knowingly participate in the robbery and leave people in
danger; Windfall is not an innocent carrier outside the attack.

Gantry's crew isolated the damage and sheltered in a surviving pressurized
compartment. Emergency power still supports communications, limited life
support, and attitude control. The ship is stable but stranded, not an
uncontrollably tumbling wreck.

Redress and Windfall have finished the raid and left before Kaveri arrives.
They do not return or pursue the rescuers. The immediate tension comes from
reaching the survivors in time, approaching the damaged ship safely, and
evacuating an injured worker, not a second attack.

One usable docking port remains accessible. Emergency reserves cannot cover
the wait for the scheduled recovery. Exact endurance and arrival times must be
chosen with the route and distances, not supplied as an arbitrary countdown.
The pirates remain responsible for using violence to take cargo and leaving
people in danger.

#### The refused diversion

Kaveri itself is the available rescue option. Elena asks Daniel Reed at
EarthWorks Operations, Keystone, to cover the diversion while the crew prepares
the intercept. The request does not hold up rescue preparations. Daniel refuses
the emergency expense despite receiving Gantry's survival estimate; Elena backs
Jonah's rescue at Clearwell's expense.

Samir has a concrete record: the request, confirmation that people are alive,
and the refusal. It establishes this abandonment, not the later conspiracy.
The opening raid remains independent of the subsequent corporate contracts.
No separate emergency rescue fleet is introduced to explain the refusal.

#### Direct docking and evacuation

1. Kaveri changes its planned return trajectory to intercept Gantry.
2. The crews establish the condition of the surviving docking port.
3. Kaveri matches motion and docks directly.
4. Rina coordinates the transfer. Samir receives Owen and provides first aid.
5. Nadia, Owen, and Ivo leave aboard Kaveri. Gantry's hull remains a separate
   recovery problem.

The player handles the intercept and approach through the whole ship. The crew
transfer can be presented through comms and short exterior shots. This rescue
does not require an EVA evacuation or a walkable interior. Kaveri's eventual
design must support temporary carriage of all three survivors, including
assisted boarding for Owen.

Exact equipment, port arrangements, intercept conditions, the delivery
commitment, and timing still need definition. These beats do not establish
episode boundaries or claim that docking and evacuation interactions are
already implemented.

#### Opening point of view

The opening stays with Kaveri's crew. The audience learns about the attack
alongside them through Gantry's distress call, the crews' exchanges, the visible
damage, and the rescue. There is no independent cutaway to the pirate attack,
the pirates, or the stranded crew.

This is a crew viewpoint, not a captain-only or bridge-only camera. The comic
can follow Kaveri's crew during station work and the evacuation. The decision
applies to this opening, not automatically to every later sequence in the season.

#### Teaching ship handling through the job

The working gameplay direction is a controls-teaching, noncombat opening. Put
low-pressure practice in the ordinary work before Gantry's distress call, then
let the rescue apply handling the player has already practiced. Steering,
thrust and braking, matching motion, and a controlled approach are the proposed
teaching sequence; exact inputs and prompts still need design.

The player may be new, but Jonah and the crew are already competent. Guidance
should teach the player without turning professional crew exchanges into
lessons for an inexperienced captain. The comic shows the same useful work and
rescue without reproducing interface instructions.

This does not claim that cargo handling, docking, evacuation, or tutorial
interactions are implemented. Check runtime support before selecting playable
steps; crew transfer can still use comms and exterior shots. These plans do not
replace or redesign separate training content.

### Pressure on the independent operator

EarthWorks wants control of the water station. It is much larger than the local
operator, but has not been established as the largest company on Earth.

The station's value comes from useful processing and supply, not from ownership
of uniquely scarce ice. Its independence matters in a growing network where
power, equipment, processing capacity, transport, and imported precision
components limit usable supply.

Commercial pressure develops into a covert operation. The initial independent
pirate raids remain separate from the later commissioned attacks.

### Coordinated collapse and occupation

The commissioned attacks target the smaller company's ships, security,
leadership, and ability to function. They preserve the useful water station.
The pirates occupy it under contract.

The target is the collapse of this local operator, not the destruction of
EarthWorks or a general war between all of Saturn's companies.

### False liberation

EarthWorks presents its intervention as the restoration of a frontier facility
that the smaller operator could not protect. Its infrastructure-building
reputation supports the claim.

The intervention also eliminates witnesses, including workers and pirates.
The pirates discover that their corporate employer treats them as disposable.
This betrayal does not cancel their responsibility for the occupation or their
earlier victims.

### Escape and an uneasy alliance

Worker and pirate survivors escape together after the takeover. Cooperation
comes from shared danger and reciprocal actions, not instant forgiveness or a
speech that settles every grievance.

The takeover can be an evacuation mission with achievable rescue goals. It
should not promise a winnable defense and then discard the player's success.
The people saved and the means of escape must fit the shared story in both
media.

The pirate leadership's survival and succession are not yet fixed.

### Return, reclamation, and defense

The survivors return with allies to reclaim and hold the water station.
Cooperation has to become reliable enough to defend a community, while the
pirates' past actions remain a source of mistrust and accountability.

The captain's responsibility has grown beyond the original crew. A credible
opportunity to return to Earth remains part of this arc. The captain chooses
to stay; the exact placement and form of that choice remain open.

### An independent home

The survivors establish a worker-controlled successor with rights protected
structurally, especially residency independent of employment. Their home is
won through action and cooperation, not financed as a purchase reward.

Holding the station is not, by itself, a legal transfer of ownership. The ending
needs a credible recognition or ownership settlement. Its precise mechanism
has not been chosen.

State military intervention remains a looming threat. It does not arrive to
solve the first season. The resolution removes the immediate grounds for that
intervention without making the community sovereign or immune to public law.

The original company's loss is real. Its decent principles survive in a new
structure that gives the residents stronger protection.

## Campaign development needs

These are story-driven design directions, not claims about shipped systems.
Recheck the implementation before selecting missions or promising interactions.

- Useful work interactions and control guidance for the noncombat opening,
  with routine flight preparing the player for the rescue.
- The opening's direct-docking evacuation, with approach under player control
  and crew transfer represented without an on-foot system.
- Ship inventory and finite carried ammunition and repair reserves.
- Hailing and limited choices where a decision matters to the mission.
- Multiple independently acting sides for the occupation, betrayal, and alliance.
- Partial-success debriefs and continuity between missions.
- Comms and cinematics for conversations that the comic shows inside the ship
  or station.

Mission variation must preserve the shared major events and character outcomes.
Additional objectives can change how a mission is experienced without giving
the comic and campaign contradictory histories.

Full boarding simulation, walkable interiors, a market economy, and strategic
fleet management are not prerequisites for this story.

## Working proposals

These details are favored options or possibilities, not established facts.

- **Foundation's history and later role:** it may have helped build Keystone and may
  later support EarthWorks' intervention. Neither its individual construction
  history nor its deployment in the takeover is established yet.
- **Altair and Earth:** its passenger accommodation and an Earthbound departure
  may provide Jonah's concrete opportunity to leave. This remains a proposal.
  Do not destroy the only way home to force the captain to stay.
- **Bastion's crew:** showing legitimate escort work before the crisis would
  establish its people independently of senior management's conspiracy. Their
  later orders, loyalties, and outcomes remain open.
- **Commercial escalation:** beyond the approved Foundation order, EarthWorks
  may be a major water customer, not the sole customer. A demand for priority
  supply and control for an important expansion, followed by Clearwell's
  refusal, may lead from commercial coercion to sabotage. The broader customer
  relationship and specific demand remain proposals.
- **Competing pirate clients:** the pirates may secretly take assignments from
  rival companies whose managers each believe they have favorable or exclusive
  access. No second client has been selected. Farspan is not assigned a
  conspiracy role by its existing commercial relationship with EarthWorks.
- **Pirate succession:** the pirate captain may die during the false liberation,
  leaving an established deputy to become the workers' ally. The captain's
  survival remains an alternative.

### Former working outfit

**Working direction, approved to try:** The group behind Redress and Windfall
began as a small, lawful escort-and-freight outfit founded at Saturn, with no
Earth parent. It has a shared commercial past, rather than first coming together
for raids. Independence did not mean starting without capital or financing.

The outfit financed the purchase of an older commercial escort against expected
contract income. That vessel later became Redress; it was acquired for lawful
work, not first obtained after the turn to piracy. It earned fees protecting
customers' ships, rather than being an expensive extra guarding only the
outfit's own freight. Windfall handled freight jobs during that legitimate
business. Its acquisition arrangements remain open.

Treat Redress as a modest commercial escort, not an expensive frontline fighter
or warship. Its design and equipment remain open. A used ship can cost less to
buy without being cheap to operate.

The business initially covered its costs. Irregular work and maintenance bills
then gradually consumed its reserves. A substantial completed contract went
unpaid when it was already struggling. That nonpayment was the final blow,
not the sole cause of its failure.
The group chose robbery to keep the outfit together rather than accept its
failure. This does not make piracy the only means of personal survival or an
inevitable outcome of independent business.

The unpaid client was another company, not EarthWorks. Do not turn that
nonpayment into an earlier EarthWorks operation. EarthWorks' later assignments
can appear to offer steadier income, rather than requiring the pirates to
return to the customer that failed to pay them. Its exploitation of their
