# v0.16.0 - The world keeps score

v0.15.0 made a place. New Game took a seed and built rock fields, planetoids,
wrecks and the ships of the civilizations that left them, streamed in around
one line warship. You could mine it, loot it, trade with it and fight it. But
the place had no memory and no opinion. A sector you left was generated again
from the seed when you came back. Nobody out there wanted anything from you.
Nothing you did survived closing the game.

v0.16.0 is the release where the world starts keeping score. Civilizations
build stations, and a station is somewhere to dock, trade, store, refit and
take work. Every station posts jobs, and every hand-in or kill moves what its
civilization thinks of you. New Game asks who you are before it asks where you
start: six roles, each with a hull, a kit and a goal. The Map grows a chart
that remembers every sector you entered and every station you found. And on
the desktop build the world saves itself as you fly, so a run is something you
come back to.

## New Game asks who you are

New Game is two steps now. The first takes a name for the world and a seed.
Under the seed, before the world exists, a 3D chart draws the seed's sectors
around your starting station with YOU at the start; right-drag turns it and
the wheel zooms. The second step lists six start roles. Select one and its
hull turns in 3D beside a card: hull name, length, section count, weapons and
drives, then the kit of credits and cargo, the role's goal and a line of lore.
Create puts you in that hull, docked at your **starting station**: the nearest
station of a civilization that begins as your Ally. A seed with no allied
station in reach is refused on the seed field, with the reason, so you pick
another.

### Six ways in

Every role is content: a `StartRole` with an icon, a hull, a kit, a job mix
and a goal. The base game ships six. Each kit is worth 3,000 cr at the
reference ask before ammunition, so no role starts richer than another; it
starts differently equipped. A hull with point-defense mounts also carries
1,000 PDC rounds in its hold.

### The goal is a charter, not an ending

Each role plays toward one finite milestone, read off the saved world every
frame. Every goal opens with the same first line, **Stations owned**, which
needs one station you own, live or frozen in a sector you left. Then the
role's own line follows: ore handed in, wrecks recovered, distinct stations
you have worked for, hulls on a kill list, patrols flown, or, for the pirate,
one civilization your own kills turned Enemy and six more of its intact hulls
taken while it stands so.

The chart's OBJECTIVES block and the Jobs pane both show the next unmet line
with `have / need`. The frame both lines are met, the save records the play
time and the block reads `Goal reached at` that time. The world does not
change. There is no completion screen. A met goal stamps the save and the
sandbox plays on.

## Stations

The open world places stations itself. Every civilization keeps its stations
at the clusters nearest its centroid: a living one keeps one, plus one per
half of its advancement, so one to three; an extinct one left exactly one,
dead and lootable. A cluster holds at most one station, and the choice is a
function of the seed and the civilization alone, so a cluster shows the same
station from every cell and in any visit order.

A station is built from the loaded parts, never picked from a catalog. Each
civilization draws one of three archetypes and builds one design from it at
its advancement, so all of its stations share a silhouette.

Advancement sizes everything. A backwater station has two docking ports and
four turrets, a tonne of storage and 10,000 cr. A core-world station has
eight ports, sixteen turrets, four tonnes and 40,000 cr. Two new station
parts make the shapes possible: a truss hull and a solar panel. Both end in
`Bare` link points, which still mate but take no skin plate or greeble when
left open, so a spoke or a wing tip stops clean instead of growing a plate.

### A station is a body

A station is a dynamic body like a ship. Gravity and rams move it. In a
cluster with a planetoid it rides a circular orbit; in one without, it stands
at rest 500 m clear of a rock. Shove it off its post and its bridge levels it
and rides back on RCS, docked hulls and all. It docks one ship per port and
turns with them as one body.

A living station aims its turrets. An Enemy station fires on hostile sides. A
Neutral one only fires back, with the same patience a Neutral ship has. The
guns hold on a docked ship and point defense never fires through one. Fight a
station down to its last turret or its last flight computer and it turns dead
in place: services stop, its hold opens to Take, and a docked player gets the
helm. Past its collapse threshold it comes apart like a ship and drops its
hold and storage as canisters.

### Who lets you in

A station flies its civilization's stance as its side, and the dock reads the
side alone. An Ally or Neutral station admits you. An Enemy station refuses
with a `<NAME> REFUSES DOCKING` HUD notice and a sound. A dead station admits a
dock on any side, as a derelict does, and hands you the helm at once; so does
a ship or station you neutralized.

The three-sided relation model under all of this has not changed. What is new
is that a whole civilization now holds one of those sides toward you, and
moves it.

### Rebuild, refit, add, take off

Docked at a living station, the Ship pane does the work a dock is for. A
section you lost is rebuilt with its old code and key, paid by its build
recipe: credits and parts, taken from your hold first and bought from the
station for the rest. At a station with **Refit**, the footer gains a
**Catalogue** button that opens the parts gallery, priced. Select a section
and every part of the same kind and size offers **Swap**. Click a part and
its ghost snaps to the nearest open point under the pointer, green when it
fits and red with the reason when it does not; R rolls it a quarter turn and F
mates it by its next socket, the editor's own controls. Click to fit it. The
view eases onto the new part and it takes its kind's key.

Delete takes a section off for good and gives back its strip share of parts;
**Salvage** lists them before you press. A delete that would split the ship
paints the sections that would come off the dock red while the section is
selected. An armed ship keeps one gun. The station and the ships at its other
docks show as dim outlines, and a part that would hit them is refused.

A station design lists the sections it **builds**. Swap, add and rebuild
refuse the rest, and the Catalogue dims those tiles with the reason on hover.
An All/Available switch lists only what you can place right now.

### Ships for sale, escorts for hire, a station for the right price

Docked at a living station, a fifth pane, **Station**, lists what it sells. A
**Refit** station parks a hull for sale at each port but one, its card turning
the hull slowly with its name, length and price. Buy at full price and you
take its controls; your old ship stays parked, yours to **Board** back for
free, and its hold shows as storage while you are there. **Info** on any card
opens that hull in the Ship pane, read-only: turn it, zoom it, click a section,
then **Close**.

The same station hires out one to three armed escorts that coast beside it.
A hire costs half again what the hull would, joins your side where it waited
and comes to you. It flies on its own drive, 200 m off your side and 100 m
behind, follows you across sectors, fights what attacks you and saves. A
third hire is refused: two fleet slots, **STBD** and **PORT**.

And you can buy the station. Only an Ally civilization's station sells itself
or hires out escorts. The price is twice what its sections, hold and credits
are worth at ask, and the credits go to its civilization. It leaves that
civilization for your side: its guns fire on your foes, it admits you on any
side, its hold sells to you at bid, and it wears **OWNED**. It earns nothing
yet. Owning one is the first line of every role's goal.

## Jobs

Every living generated station posts a board of three jobs. The board is a
function of the world seed, the station, its slot and your start, so a Load
shows the same offers and two boards never pay for the same wreck or hull. An
offer stands for 20 minutes of play, 60 for an escort, then its slot posts a
fresh one under a new id. A job you hold is pinned to its slot and never
expires. Hand one in and the slot renews at once. At the posting station you
can pay 100 cr to delete an offer you do not hold and post a fresh one in its
place; dropping a held job is always free, and its slot waits out the normal
window.

parts and plates in a derelict field. Which kinds a board leans to follows
your role. A trader's boards post procurements and escorts, a scavenger's
recoveries, a hunter's distress calls and bounties. Your starting station's
board lists only the jobs your hull can do: a hold for the stack, a docking
section, or a weapon. Every other board lists them all and greys Accept with
the reason.

### The escort is a fight you can lose

An escort's hauler does not leave until you are undocked and within 1 km of
it. Then it runs its 10 km leg at 100 m/s. Its raiders, one plus one per 700
cr of cargo up to three, lie 2.5 km off the leg three quarters of the way
along it and engage inside 3 km. They hunt the hauler and turn on whoever
shoots them for as long as that ship keeps shooting. A hauler neutralized,
destroyed or left without drives drops the job and its bodies. The reward is
paid at the hand-in, not at the mark.

### The Jobs pane

A fourth pane, **Jobs**, holds your work. Docked, **Jobs posted here** lists
the board with each offer's kind icon, title, reward, a meter of the time left
and Accept. **Your jobs** lists what you hold from any station, docked or not,
with a `have / need` meter, **Hand in**, and an X to drop. At the hand-in
station the row reads HAND IN HERE. Click a row and the inspector reads the
job in full, with **Show on Map**, which opens the chart on the job's mark.
Over the lists sits your role's next milestone. An empty list reads EMPTY.

In flight, the body a held job sends you to wears a gold `JOB-n` chip with
what to do and the distance; off screen it pins to the edge. The moment the
work is done, a HUD notice names the station that pays, and the chip moves
there. Jobs that hand in at one station share its chip. A hand-in reports the
payout, the new balance and, when it steps a civilization, the stance.

## A market with reasons

v0.15.0 priced every item the same on every ship. In v0.16.0 a generated
station or ship prices each item at the moment you trade, from three factors
on top of the reference price: its **scarcity**, the stock it holds against
the stock it spawned with; its **cluster**, the type of space it stands in;
and its **civilization**, what its ships do. Ore is cheap where asteroids are
many and dear among planets. A civilian world pays more for rations. An
armored one pays more for ammunition and plates.

The Inventory pane shows the arithmetic. Each row carries the **Price** of one
with a **Deal** badge of the percent off the reference, green when it favours
you and amber when not. Select an item and the inspector reads Reference,
Pays and Asks with the three factors that made them and their total, marked
`clamped` when the price hit its limit. A **MARKET** strip under the columns
names what the trader buys for most and sells cheapest, its restock gap, and
REFUSED with the minutes left while its civilization will not deal with you.
Buy all and Sell all trade the whole stack in one click, and a double-click on
any row does the same.

<p>A trader's ask is the reference ask times a scarcity factor, a cluster factor and a civilization factor; the bid is the same product over the bid side. Scarcity runs from 1.5 on the ask and 1.25 on the bid at no stock, through 1 at the stock the trader spawned with, down to 0.9 and 0.6 at three times that stock. An item the trader never spawned with pays 0.9 of the reference. Raw material is 0.6 in an asteroid-rich cluster, 0.8 among bare rock and 1.5 near planets; parts are 0.7 and repair material 0.85 in a derelict field. An industrial civilization prices raw material at 0.8, a civilian one food at 1.25, a scavenger one parts at 0.8, and an armored one ammunition at 1.25 and repair material at 1.15. The product is clamped between 0.25 and 3.0. With every factor's bid at most its ask, a trader never pays more than it charges.</p>
</div>

A trader's stock stands still while its sector is frozen and drifts back
toward its spawn stock by the play time it stood frozen, 30 minutes of play
from empty to full. A reload alone restocks nothing. A trader you sold a glut
to is still glutted when you come back a minute later, and recovering when
you come back an hour later.

## Civilizations have opinions

Every living civilization holds one stance toward you: Ally, Neutral or
Enemy. Never a number. The seed draws it with an even chance of each, the same
for every role, and your deeds move it.

- **An offence** is neutralizing an intact generated hull or station of a
  civilization, by your ship or your fleet escort. Three offences inside an
  hour of play step it one down. The first two are a grace count, and an
  offence older than the window is forgotten.
- **A merit** is handing in a job one of its stations posted. Three merits
  step it one up.
- **A step down travels one hop.** Two living civilizations whose centroids
  lie under 340 km apart are neighbours, and their edge is seeded Ally,
  Neutral or Enemy. When an offence steps a civilization down, an Ally
  neighbour follows one step down and an Enemy neighbour warms one step up. A
  Neutral edge carries nothing, and nothing reaches a neighbour's neighbour.
  Every neighbourhood holds at least one rival, so there is always somebody
  who likes it when you misbehave.
- **A provocation** is faster than an offence. Fire or ram a Neutral or Ally
  hull past its patience, the same 30 hit points in quick succession that
  make a Neutral ship answer, and its whole civilization reads Enemy for 15
  minutes of play.
- **An Enemy you made thaws.** With no new offence for two hours of play it
  returns to Neutral. A seeded Enemy never does.
- **Or you settle.** Select a charted station on the chart and the STANCES
  block lists every neighbour of it that stands Enemy to you, with its fine
  and **Pay fine**, which works only while you are docked there. The fine runs
  from 1,000 cr at a backwater to 10,000 cr at a core world, goes to the
  station, and returns that civilization to Neutral with its offences cleared.
  No neighbour moves.

What the stance does is plain. An Enemy civilization's stations refuse your
dock and its ships fight you. Only an Ally's station sells itself or hires out
escorts. A Neutral one trades and posts jobs. A station you bought, an escort
in your fleet and a hull you neutralized keep their side whatever its
civilization decides. And every generated crew now holds fire for eight
seconds after its cell streams in, whatever its side, so a civilization you
turned Enemy arrives with the same grace a seeded one gets.

## The chart remembers

The Map pane has a second view. Press C and it draws your chart at sector
scale: a grid of 32 km cells on the focused Y layer, Z and X step the layer,
and Reframe returns to yours. A cell you entered is a textured tile, one
layer per cluster type it holds: an asteroid speckle, a rock scatter, a ringed
planet, cracked derelict slivers. A cell you never entered is an outline. The
stations and wrecks your sensors found stand on their tiles as `STN-n` and
`WRK-n`, numbered in the order you found them. A dead station fades, one that
refuses you wears a red ring, and one with open jobs adds their count to its
code.

Click a cell or a mark and the view centres on it and the panel opens a
SECTOR block: its contents as icon rows, when you entered it, every find there
with its state, the services, stock and storage you saw at your last dock at
each station there, and the jobs you hold that point there. A station you
never docked at says so. While nothing is selected the panel shows the
OBJECTIVES block, the discovery log of your last twelve finds with relative
times, and STANCES.

Every job you hold is marked `JOB-n` in the sector of the body it sends you
to, and a procurement in the sector of its poster. A scenario handler can mark
a generated rock or derelict too, and the mark outlives a retire and a thaw.

## The world keeps what you did

In v0.15.0 a sector that streamed out and back in was generated again from the
seed. Now it freezes as you left it and thaws that way: mined rocks and the ore
still owed by a cut, damaged, looted and neutralized ships, canisters, wrecks
and a docked partner. Only the planetoids regenerate from the seed. Within a
session, a wreck you emptied stays empty and a ship you destroyed stays
destroyed.

On the desktop build the world also saves itself. New Game names its world and
writes a save on every sector crossing and on leaving. A play clock runs only
while you fly unpaused, and every frozen sector records the time it froze, so
a trader's restock and an offer's expiry read the clock and not the wall. The
main menu gains **Continue**, which opens the newest world and says how long
ago it was saved, and **Load**, which lists every saved world with its role,
play time and open objectives and draws the save's chart. Dying shows DEFEAT
with **Load last save**. The pause menu's Retry becomes Load last save too.

## Ships and parts

### A new base fleet

The base hull list is new. Six start hulls, a training picket for the
handbook, a line warship, a freighter derelict and a debris plate replace the
block fleet of v0.15.0. The salvage hauler and the light freighter fly five
stern drives each, down from eight and nine, with a simpler aft silhouette.

Generated ships changed shape too. They are longer and thinner, cap at 2,000
sections, and fly capital drives that push 75, up from 25. They grow with
distance: the first four civilization rings field small hulls only, medium
hulls appear near 2,000 km out, and the largest past 3,000 km. Every one
carries a cargo intake, on top or below, clear of its docking ports. A ship
carries only ammunition its own weapons reload from: no torpedoes without a
bay. Torpedo bays are rare and railgun lances need mid advancement, so the
ships near your start shoot PDC rounds at you.

### The skin is rebuilt

The hull skin is a new system. Flat plates at half or a whole cell with
chamfered rims, folds and skirts at hull corners, and stairs clad as one ramp.
A plate under a greeble flattens to fit it. A style now places greebles by
`greeble_rules`: link types, greebles with four link points, `near` terms and
caps. Six `station_*` greebles and a `station` style dress the new station
parts.

### A destroyed ship spills its cargo

A destroyed ship drops its hold as cargo canisters, and a destroyed section
drops its `strip_yield` share of each recipe part, rounded down. Both freeze
and save. Dock with a derelict instead and the Inventory pane's **Strip**
first loots its hold, late cargo too, then takes it apart one section at a
time, farthest from your port first and the port last, each part of each
recipe times its yield times its health. The HUD chip over your ship reads
`STRIP 40%` while you fly on docked. A hold without room holds the strip at
that section until you make room.

### Mining without a lock

The mining beam no longer needs a travel lock. It cuts the nearest asteroid
its ray meets within 100 m of the emitter face, and a nearer barren rock
blocks the ore behind it. A refused pulse now plays the section's
`refusal_sound` at the emitter, once per pulse, so a silent beam means
nothing is in front of it and a buzzing one means a check is failing.

## Flight and combat

- **A scrape is free.** A contact under 10 m/s of closing speed, up from 5,
  costs nothing, so a glancing touch or a drift out of STOP no longer damages
  what it touches.
  <!-- SAFE_CONTACT_SPEED crates/nova_gameplay/src/integrity/core.rs:69. -->
- **Docked hulls do not grind.** Two docked hulls no longer collide, so skin
  plates and greebles past a port face no longer wear both hulls down while
  they sit docked, and undocking no longer shoves them apart. A berthed hull
  stands one cell off its port, and the dock gate carries a little slack past
  capture distance for float noise.
- **Neutrals have patience.** A Neutral ship answers a ship only after it
  deals 30 hit points in quick succession, fire and rams alike. One PDC burst
  provokes it. A stray round or a scrape does not.
- **GOTO takes the lock on sight.** A GOTO's target takes your empty travel
  lock the moment it comes into sight, with no dwell, so a Map GOTO set out of
  range is locked for DOCK as you arrive. Docked, a Map GOTO waits and flies
  once you undock. G or CANCEL drops it.
- **The predicted path bends for the target.** GOTO's path curves for a
  coasting or tumbling target, gravity included, and hides while the target
  changes course or spin; the guide stays.
- **RCS has exhaust.** Every push throws gas puffs off the hull. Another
  ship's RCS hiss carries at any push, and generated ships voice their RCS and
  cockpit cues.

  `1,200 cr`.

## More in v0.16.0

- **Starts are docked.** `SpawnStartRole` spawns the role's hull beside the
  starting station and docks it there, so the first thing you do in a world
  is open the Station pane.
- **The menu duel ends fast.** The rival comes apart once a fifth of its hull
  is gone, the victor's PDCs hit for more, and the siege torpedo kills the
  victor in one hit.
- **Hold events fire once.** `OnMined`, `OnPickedUp`, `OnJettisoned`,
  `OnTraded`, `OnCreditsChanged` and `OnRepaired` fire once per occurrence;
  a settlement fires `OnCreditsChanged` with cause `Settle`.
- **Stations know they are stations.** `OnDocked` and `OnUndocked` carry the
  partner's `type_name`, so a filter can ask for a `living_station`.
- **Generated rocks have names.** The target inset names a generated asteroid
  by its kind, as `Rock asteroid`, not its internal id.
- **The Sandbox can spawn a station.** A ship's Station box spawns it as one;
  Station Check names the first fault and Play refuses a hull with a thruster,
  no port or no controller.
- **Credit cheats.** Armed, `credits give` and `credits set` change a ship's
  credits, never below zero, and fire `OnCreditsChanged` with cause `Cheat`.
- **A debug window.** Debug builds open Nova Debug on F11 and freeze flight:
  overlay checkboxes, cheats, a command prompt, entities by sector and type,
  and the raw inspector.
- **The editor's planet hit box matches the drawn planet.** It was far
  larger, so F framed a scenario from too far away to see its ships.
- **Lists stop leaking clicks.** A click or hover under a scrolled list no
  longer reaches a row hidden past the list's edge.

## Modding and compatibility

v0.16.0 changes more content formats than any release since v0.13.0. Run
`content lint` on every bundle and read the [station](/create/stations/),
[start role](/create/start-roles/) and [price table](/create/prices/) pages.

- **`role: OpenWorld` is gone.** A scenario declares `world:
  Generated((seed: None))` for the New Game seed, or a fixed seed the
  Scenarios list shows. It spawns exactly one player ship. A `bounds` sphere
  spawns as a scenario area and raises `OnExit` when the player leaves it.
- **`SpawnStartRole` takes only `id`.** `position` and `rotation` fail at
  lint; the ship spawns beside the starting station and docks there.
- **A world save carries its scenario.** Load fires `OnResume`, not
  `OnStart`; a `seed: None` scenario needs a handler for it.
- **Items are content.** Mods add `Item` entries. Stock keys are quoted,
  `{"HullPlate": 8}`, and unknown ids fail at lint and at load. Two mods that
  define one item id are both refused unless one depends on the other. Base
  must define the ten item ids the game uses.
- **Every section needs a `build` recipe** of credits and `Parts` items, and
  its `base` authors `advancement` and `draw_weight`. Every civilization
  builds the parts at advancement 0 and knows a seeded subset of those above
  it.
- **`Station` content** holds its hull, services, stock and credits under
  `design`, spawns from `Prototype(id)` or `Inline` with a side, and lists the
  sections it `builds`. Its `initial_velocity` must be circular in a well or
  zero outside.
- **A `StartRole` kind** names a New Game start. Its `goal` is `OreDeliveries`,
  `Recoveries`, `StationNetwork`, `KillList`, `Patrols` or `Outlaw`; a zero
  count fails lint, and so does a hull with no intake or a part above
  advancement 0.
- **A `PriceTable` kind** sets the scarcity curve, cluster and role demand,
  the clamp, the restock recovery and the two job offer windows. The base
  states one; a mod that states one replaces it whole.
- **Styles place greebles by `greeble_rules`.** `placement` is gone.
- **A design must name a style the catalog holds.** An unknown style fails at
  lint and stops the game at spawn instead of leaving the hull bare.
- **Ids are stricter.** A scenario object id or a ship section id may not
  contain `/`, two sections of one ship may not share an id, and an asteroid
  `lock_signature` must be finite and above zero.
- **Mining sections require `refusal_sound`.** Base uses `radar_deny.wav`.
- **Ships hold only ammunition their weapons reload**, and the base hull ids
  are new: `mining_tug`, `salvage_hauler`, `light_freighter`,
  `freighter_derelict`, `interceptor`, `raider`, `patrol_picket`,
  `training_picket`, `line_warship` and `debris_plate`.
