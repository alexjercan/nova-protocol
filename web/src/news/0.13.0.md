# v0.13.0 - The game on screen

v0.12.0 made the editor the star. It could hold a whole mission - ships, range
and script under one tree - and save it as a mod. What it had to place was
another matter. A hull cell was a cube. A point-defence mount was a cube with
a barrel. The one modelled fleet was three ships that had shipped with a
campaign. Every rock in every belt was the same rock. And when any of it fired,
took a hit, or came apart, it did so in silence, because the game had never
had an audio pass.

v0.13.0 is the release that puts the game itself on screen. Every section
wears an authored model, and three of them move: a point-defence mount sinks
into a housed pit when the guns go cold, a torpedo bay winds its muzzle iris
open before a launch, and a railgun - the release's new spinal weapon - walks
a charge bolt up its bore before the slug leaves. The base game's ships are now
block hulls the game can draw itself, with a grammar that says where a part
may stand and an editor button that generates a hull into the ship you are
inside. A scenario places a planet by type and seed, and an asteroid names the
material it is made of. And all of it makes a sound: a re-recorded set twice
the size of the old one, positional in the world and structure-borne through
your own hull.

Underneath, every number moved to meters. Content, code, the editor, the wiki
and the scopes all read in the unit a pilot would use, and the flight model
got a budget worth reading: RCS reaches 100 m/s at 5 G whatever the ship
weighs, and GOTO parks off a target's surface measured from your own hull.

## The railgun

Every weapon before this one traversed. A turret slewed onto a target and a
bay's torpedo steered itself; the pilot's job was to get within reach and let
the mount do the aiming. A railgun has no traverse. It fires down the axis of
the section it is built into, so the ship is the mount, and aiming it means
flying the hull onto the line.

### Commit, then release

Tapping the trigger commits. A charge bolt starts walking the bore from the
breech, the rails light behind it, and 1.5 seconds later the slug leaves -
whether or not the nose is still on the target. There is no cancelling a
commit. The tempo that gives a railgun is the whole weapon: you line up, you
commit, and for a second and a half you hold the line while the ship you are
flying keeps drifting off it.

The gun holds one shell. A spent lance gets it back on a twelve-second idle
reload, drawn on the same ammo gauge a turret or a bay carries, in the slug's
pierce blue, so a spent shell and its long reload read at a glance.

### What a slug takes

A railgun slug does not stop at the first section it meets. It rakes a corridor
three cells wide through everything in line, gutting hull sections and
crippling the big drives, until the power it carries is spent. Power, not a
layer count or the bore, bounds what one shot takes - the same rule that made
Pierce rounds a rake in v0.11.0, at a scale where a single shot can open a
capital hull from bow to stern.

Two lances ship. The standard one is priced to cross a warship: 300 damage per
section, 1,800 power, a 10 m rake. The siege lance is the same gun at capital
grade - 500 damage through 360,000 power and a 30 m rake - and the description
says what it is for: a scripted capital, not a balanced duel.

### The gun shoves the ship

Firing a railgun shoves the ship that fired it. The impulse lands at the
muzzle, not at the centre of mass, so a lance mounted off the ship's axis yaws
the hull as well as pushing it back. A generated ship seats its bow gun on the
keel for that reason, and a hand-built one that bolts a lance to a flank gets
what it asked for.

### The bore and the wake

A charging railgun lights its own bore, and a fired slug leaves an ionized
wake: cyan haze along its path, violet filaments through it, gone in half a
second. A blue light rides the slug and lights the hulls it passes. Low draws
neither. The wake was tuned on a bench of its own - three slug speeds under
three lifetime policies, every knob live and in slow motion - because a
half-second effect that reads at 1.5 km is not something you get right by
guessing.

### Cover breaks a lock

A lock is a radio link, and rock stops radio. A contact behind an asteroid or a
planetoid cannot be locked, and cover drifting across a held lock breaks it.
Hostiles lose you the same way - the AI's acquisition asks the same question
of the same world, so a hostile cannot pick a ship it has no line to either.

Combined with a weapon that reaches 18 km and needs a second and a half of
held line, cover becomes a thing to fly for. A belt is somewhere to fight from
now, not a backdrop to fight in front of.

## Every section is a machine

Until this release the thruster was the only section with an authored model.
The rest - the hull cells, the controller core, the point-defence mounts and
the torpedo bay - were placeholder cubes with the right sockets and a greeble
skin closed over them. The skin did a lot of work. It could not do the work of
a part that moves.

Every section now wears a model at the thruster's standard, and the cubes are
gone.

### The PDC stows

A point-defence mount out of combat sinks into a housed pit and two lids slide
shut over it. It deploys on weapons hot, a tracked target or a point-defence
assignment, and it cannot track or fire until it is fully up. A ship at rest
is a ship with its guns put away - and the beat between the threat and the
first round is now a beat you can see, and hear.

### The bay has doors

A torpedo leaves through the bay's muzzle iris. The petals wind open before
ejection, the round slides its whole length out of the tube, and held fire
keeps the doors gaping. The v0.12.0 cold launch - a torpedo dropped inert and
lit clear of the hull - now starts with a machine opening.

The bay is a 1x1x2 tube now, with an unlinkable muzzle face; an authored ship
seats one with a half-cell shift so it sits on the section grid. This is a
format break, and the compatibility notes say how to move a bay.

## The game builds its own ships

The base game shipped three modelled ships for eleven releases - the Racer,
CargoA and CargoB - and they were the reason every menu backdrop, every
scenario and every wiki figure needed a fleet that had come with a campaign
mod. That fleet has moved into The Ledger, which now carries its meshes
itself, and the base game ships BLOCK hulls only: ships built from the sections
above, that the game can draw for itself.

Eleven block ships join the catalog - a cutter, a hauler, a gunship, a raider,
an industrial carrier, a stolen warship and five cleanup craft - plus four
wreck fragments of the carrier. Every main-menu backdrop flies them now, and
no backdrop needs a modelled ship.

### A grammar says where a part stands

The hull generator collapses a hull from a grammar - a draw table of parts,
weights and aims. It could say which way a part pointed. It could not say where
on the hull it stood, so a generated warship could carry its drive at the bow
and its gun on the stern and be, by every rule the collapse knew, correct.

A grammar now pins a prototype to a `zone` - the bow, amidships, stern, dorsal,
ventral or flank third of a hull - for every cell of it. And it seats a spinal
gun with `bow_gun`: the lance stands on the bow of the keel and the mirror
makes a pair, so the gun the arena used to bolt on after the collapse is a
role the grammar asks for. The collapse also places multi-cell parts whole - a
torpedo bay or a 5x5x3 capital drive as one block of tiles, mated and cleared
together - instead of skipping anything wider than a cell.

### Generate a hull into the ship you are in

The editor generates a HULL into the ship you are inside. Name a seed, tick
which sections the collapse draws from, and it replaces what that ship holds.
The biggest drive you tick is seeded as the generated hull's main engine and
the biggest railgun as its bow gun, and the grid grows to hold both. A HULL
PLAN line names what the ticks chose. A ticked row carries a zone chip: press
it to cycle where the collapse may stand that section. The Generate block
picks a HULL LINE - a row per grammar in the merged content - and the draw
list re-ticks to the picked one.

A hull the game's lint refuses never lands. And a weapon takes its own key when
it is placed or generated - Space thrusts, LMB the PDCs, `F` the torpedoes, `R`
the railgun - so a generated ship set to Player flies without rebinding a
thing.

## The world has bodies

### A planet is a planet

A scenario places a `Planet`: a type and a seed draw a world with terrain,
biomes and a cap. Six types ship - barren rock, dust world, ice world,
volcanic, greenhouse and one temperate - as archetypes rather than places,
because the fiction is not the Solar System. A type says what a world is made
of and the scenario says what it is called.

A planetoid used to be a big `Asteroid`, and it was as destructible as a small
one, which meant a patient pilot could carve a crater into a world. A `Planet`
must author `invulnerable: true`. A destructible one is a lint error and
refuses to load: nothing carves a planet, so it would sit there taking hits and
never break.

### Rock is a material

An asteroid's `material` names its KIND: rock, metal, ice, carbon or plain.
Each gets its own palette, strata and specular over a triplanar surface, so a
belt reads as several materials instead of one repeated rock. `ScatterObjects`
takes `asteroid_kinds`, a weighted mix drawn from the scatter's own seed, so
one field is mostly rock with a little metal, and the weights are relative
counts - `[("rock", 24), ("carbon", 5), ("metal", 1)]` - not percentages that
have to add up.

The material is required and there is no default. A rock without one fails to
load, and an id nobody ships is a lint error.

## The game has a voice

This is the first dedicated audio pass. The sound set is re-recorded and twice
the size: guns, ordnance, impacts, destruction, drives, cockpit, menus and
editor, all synthesised from scratch. Only the ship computer's family is
untouched.

### Where a sound is

Sound is positional. A cue out in the world falls off with distance and pans
with bearing, so the menu backdrop's traffic is heard passing the camera
instead of sitting mute behind it. Your own ship is the exception, and it is
an exception by routing rather than by special case: its guns, its drives and
the damage landing on it are structure-borne through the hull you are sitting
in, so they never attenuate and never pan. Everything else is out there across
the gap.

### What a hit sounds like

A hit is named by the round as well as by what it struck. A slug, a penetrator
and a warhead each land differently, and each lands differently again on

## Flying it, in meters

### Everything is in meters

Content and code are authored in meters through one set of quantity types.
World units - one of which is 10 m, and one build-grid cell - appear only at a
Bevy, physics, rendering or build-grid boundary, which says so locally and
converts. Every player- or creator-facing figure prints in meters: the editor's
inspector and gallery read and write lengths and speeds in meters, a section's
own mesh rows read in build-grid cells, and every wiki figure, scope and
catalog reads the same way.

This is the release's quietest break and its widest. A number in a shipped
mod that used to mean world units now means meters, and The Ledger and
Gauntlet Run are republished to say so. A portal mod installed on 0.12.0
carries the old numbers, and this build reads them as meters - the
compatibility notes say what to update.

### One RCS budget

RCS reaches 100 m/s at a mass-independent 5 G: one speed budget and one
acceleration budget for every direction. The old RCS was a force, so a heavy
hull nudged and a light one leapt; the new one is a true acceleration, so
every ship in the game answers a fine adjust the same way, and the cap is a
sphere the result is clamped onto rather than a line per axis. At the cap RCS
may still TURN your velocity - it just cannot grow it. STOP below that speed
brakes on RCS without turning the ship.

### GOTO parks off the surface

GOTO used to park a fixed distance from a target's centre. A warship and a
shuttle sent to the same mark would stop at the same point in space, which put
the warship's bow somewhere inside the mark. GOTO now parks a margin off the
target's SURFACE, measured from the ship's own hull: a warship and a shuttle
sent to one mark stop the same distance clear of it.

To do that, GOTO sizes every target it can. A beacon's orb and another ship's
hull push the park point out the way an asteroid's geometry already did; a
bare position still has no size. A GOTO that ends at a planetoid arrives on a
ring ORBIT accepts, so the parking handoff no longer burns the ship back
outward to reach a legal orbit - and it parks itself in orbit only where the
ship's computer grants ORBIT, handing the ship back at the standoff where the
verb is withheld.

## NOVA OS gets a second shell

Press `:` anywhere - menu, editor, flight, pause screen - and the ship computer
opens on a second shell. One CRT, two languages: `commands` switches, and each
keeps its own transcript and history. The first shell is the NOVA OS you know,
the diegetic terminal. The second reads the run and writes your settings.

The command shell holds 27 commands in four classes. Utility commands control
the shell or load another scenario. Read-only commands observe and never
mutate. Setting commands change the same persisted settings the panel does.
Cheat commands change the live world, and are refused until you run `cheats
enable`, which marks the run one way and turns the header amber. A fresh
scenario is a fresh run and clears the mark.

`help` and Tab completion come from one registry, and Tab offers the ship and
section ids the world holds NOW - so a cheat that names a section names one
that exists.

## A scenario can direct

The modding vocabulary grew more in this cycle than in any before it, and
almost all of it is about one thing: a scenario being able to stage a scene.
Basic Training is written on it, and so is the story that replaces the
campaign.

### Cinematics

`Cinematic` plays a beat chain as a scene the player may leave, reporting
`OnCinematicFinished` on every path out and `OnCinematicSkipped` first on a
skip; `CancelCinematic` ends one early. A `Cinematic` filter matches an ending
by its scene key, so a scenario with two scenes answers the right one, and an
unplayed key is a lint error.

`SetCameraAnchor` rides the camera on an object at a local or world offset,
facing a point or another object, so a cinematic keeps the player's ship in
frame; `ReleaseCamera` hands the view back. `SetCamera` and `SetCameraAnchor`
take an optional `blend` - seconds and an easing - that turns a cut into a
move that keeps tracking its anchor. `CinematicTitle` posts a shot's title
card in an authored screen corner - location, stamp and one line - and expires
on the scenario clock, so no handler has to take it down.
`SuspendPlayerControl` and `ResumePlayerControl` give a scene explicit,
teardown-safe authority over the stick.

### Helm orders

Six actions take a ship's helm: `MoveShipTo`, `ForceAlign`, `StopShip`,
`PatrolShip`, `OrbitShip` and `ClearShipOrder`. One order at a time, under a
named key, on a `None`- or an AI-controlled ship. An order on an AI ship
outranks the bot: the AI stops flying and keeps shooting, and
`order_interruption` lets it break off for contact or damage, then resume the
same order where it left it. Five events - `OnShipOrderComplete`,
`OnShipOrderInterrupted`, `OnShipOrderResumed`, `OnShipOrderCanceled` and
`OnShipOrderFailed` - report an order by key, ship and kind through one
`ShipOrder` filter.

`SetAILeash`, `SetAIEngageRange` and `SetAIPointDefenseRange` retune an AI
ship's tether, detection reach and point-defence reach at runtime, and an armed
AI ship can stand down: `non_combatant: true` flies its routine and never
acquires or fires.

### The rest of the vocabulary

`NarrativeCue` replaces `StoryMessage`, and every line authors a `channel` -
`"comms"`, `"crew"` or `"guard"` - which is how a mod authors a band of its
own instead of borrowing the base three. `PlaySound` plays one authored cue in
the cockpit on the Interface or Hull route. `SetInfiniteAmmo` and `RefillAmmo`
reach a live ship's magazines, which is what replaces a player controller's
`infinite_ammo` field. `ForceTorpedoFire` names ONE bay and `ForceRailgunFire`
one lance. `OnGotoComplete`, `OnStopComplete` and `OnOrbitLap` let a scenario
react to the player's real autopilot. A `Railgun` is a section kind and a
`Grammar` is content. And a catalog entry can set `enabled_by_default`, so a
fresh install with no saved set switches that mod on.

`content lint` checks all of it - a helm order's ship and key, an empty
patrol route, an orbit's well, an AI constraint's ship, a forced shot's
section id and class - and reports each scenario's creative map, the
world-injecting actions it runs, as context on the report.

Underneath, one TABLE declares the action vocabulary. A row generates the enum
arm, the dispatch, the RON name, the menu label, the id stem and the injection
class, so a new action is five edits, not fifteen. That is the reason this
cycle could add thirty of them.

### The editor writes many mods

The editor writes as many mods as you name ranges, each an `editor_`-prefixed
bundle of its own, instead of the one `editor_save` slot every save replaced.
File > Save As names the file - type a name, and the window says which slot it
derives and whether a range you already saved is there. File > Open lists what
there is, and a row opens it. A document remembers its file: the first Save
asks for a name, every later one writes back to it, and New Scenario forgets it
so a fresh range cannot land on the last one.

File > New Scenario also asks which world to start from: the free-flight
range, a duelling arena of rock rings around an empty middle, or nothing at
all. And the editor places a rock by KIND: the inspector's Material row is a
pick list of the shipped kinds, and the body on the stage is rebuilt in
whichever one you pick.

## An agent flies it

The process channel has driven the game's own tests for several releases. This
cycle it got a pilot. `bench play` seats an agent at the channel - a scripted
baseline, `pi` with any model, or any process on a unix socket - and scores
the run from the game's own state.

What the agent reads is a pilot's view, not a census. The bench condenses each
snapshot into meters - bearings from the nose, contacts, beacons, mounts - and
near and in-the-way bodies carry in full while the rest is one line per band
and quarter; `observe {"expand"}` reopens a group. The manual is five game
pages - targeting, weapons, travel, orbit, fighting - read on demand through a
`page` tool instead of riding in every prompt. Every line both ways lands in
an `audit.jsonl` beside the `score.json`.

The score is honest about what it grades. A run that armed NOVA OS's cheats is
scored as cheated. Unparsable wire lines are counted rather than treated as
refusals. An objective completed between two samples is credited from the
flight log. `bench replay` feeds an audit's wire lines to a fresh game and
grades the end `match`, `close` or `mismatch`.

## Performance

Four measured wins, and all four are ceilings rather than trims. A ceiling
bounds work instead of shaving it, which is the kind of win that survives the
next capital hull.

A collapsing hull measures its pre-cut centre of mass once per frame, not once
per destroyed section. A siege railgun through a capital hull spent 55 ms of
one impact on that walk; now under 1 ms. A frame throws at most 128 carve
chips however many craters opened in it: one crater is untouched, and a
capital hull raked open threw about 10,000 chips and now throws about 2,000.
Wreck pieces go physical two dozen a frame instead of all at once, so a hull
shedding 700 sections in one instant no longer grows 700 colliders and their
contacts on a single frame. And the editor stops re-walking the document each
frame: the duplicate-id scan runs only when an id or a parent moves.

## More in v0.13.0

The feature story above is the release, but a great deal else shipped:

- **The sandbox's farthest picket mounts a railgun.** It wakes like the others,
  but a shot that lines up on you crosses your whole ship.
- **Nav beacons are radar-acquirable within 12 km**, up from 6 km.
- **A scenario's spawn queue may take a fifth of each frame**, so a machine
  drawing slow frames lands its scatter in a few frames instead of one object a
  frame.
- **Only the pane under the pointer scrolls.** Every scrolling pane on screen
  was moving on one wheel notch, because no viewport reported hover.
- **A trigger volume reports a ship LEAVING again.** A damaged ship despawns
  section colliders inside the volume, and the occupancy count that never saw
  those colliders end held it inside forever.
- **A flight computer severed onto a wreck aims at the wreck**, instead of the
  hull it left.
- **A COMPLETED helm order gives back the `arrival_standoff` it staged**, as a
  canceled or interrupted one already did, so tight staging cannot retune every
  later GOTO the hull flies.
- **The Gauntlet's course ship is the base cutter**, so the time trial no
  longer depends on a fleet that shipped with a campaign mod.
- **A `--features debug` build shows the commit it was built from** on the
  status bar, and restamps it on the next commit.
- **Every example flies on your saved settings** - mouse sensitivity, keybinds,
  volumes, quality, skin - and only the settings panel can write them back.
- **The process channel gains a `command` lane** carrying the exact text a
  player would type, answered with the same result the screen prints; an input
  ack is now an ECHO of what a frame consumed, and the snapshot carries
  `beacons`, `bodies`, a `mission` block and the ship's autopilot, well and
  radar dwell.
- **The wiki reads as a book** - Start here, World, Ship sections, Interface,
  Reference - with a Railgun page carrying a corridor scope, a combat range
  ladder ranking PDC, railgun and torpedoes, a Commands page for the `:`
  shell, and every figure in meters. Every living still and loop is re-shot on
  the block fleet from a clean install.
- **The site gains a lore encyclopedia and a story archive.** They are not the
  subject of this post; the story is being rewritten, and a later post will be
  theirs.

### Modding and compatibility

v0.13.0 breaks more formats than any release before it. Every break has a
migration, and `content lint` names each one:

- **Meters.** Every length and speed in content is meters now, where it was
  world units. One world unit is 10 m. Multiply an old figure by ten. The
  Ledger (1.28.0) and Gauntlet Run (1.12.0) are republished in meters; update
  both, because a portal mod installed on 0.12.0 carries the old numbers and
  this build reads them as meters.
- **`Planet`.** A planetoid authored as a big `Asteroid` is now a rock.
  Re-author it as a `Planet` with a type and a seed at its true radius, and
  author `invulnerable: true` - a destructible planet refuses to load.
- **Asteroid `material`** names the KIND and is required: `rock`, `metal`,
  `ice`, `carbon` or `plain`. A rock without one fails to load, and an id nobody
  ships is a lint error. `ScatterObjects` takes `asteroid_kinds`; an asteroid
  template must author one.
- **The torpedo bay** is a 1x1x2 tube with an unlinkable muzzle face. An
  authored ship seats a bay with a half-cell shift to sit on the section grid.
- **The base game ships BLOCK hulls only.** The modelled Racer, CargoA and
  CargoB fleet and its part prototypes moved into The Ledger, which carries
  their meshes itself. A mod that borrowed one through `dep://base/` now
  depends on The Ledger.
- **The game ships no campaign.** The `nova_protocol` campaign and its
  `shakedown_run`, `broadside`, `broadside_gunship`, `lifeline` and
  `final_tally` chapters are gone. Retarget a mod that named one.
- **`StoryMessage` is `NarrativeCue`**, and every line authors a `channel` id:
  `"comms"`, `"crew"` or `"guard"`. Rename and add the field.
- **`ForceTorpedoLaunch` is `ForceTorpedoFire`** and names ONE bay by section
  id. Rename it, rename `id:` to `ship:`, and add `section:`.
- **A player controller's `infinite_ammo` is gone.** Delete the field: a gun
  that should never run dry omits its `ammo_capacity`, and content grants the
  rest with `SetInfiniteAmmo`.
- **An input ack is an ECHO**, not a verdict - `line`, `input`, `phase`,
  `tick`. Read the effect off the world; `schema` is `2`, which tells the two
  shapes apart. `action` is refused for good and points at the `command` lane.
- **A `Grammar`** the collapse cannot run is refused by id at lint and at load.

Run `cargo run content lint` against each bundle. The current `/create/`
reference owns the authored vocabulary, and the bundled content shows the
migrated forms.
